# Gold and Real Yields

Gold and real yields describes the relationship between gold-price performance and the real-interest-rate backdrop as presented in the In Gold We Trust reports. The reports distinguish this relationship from a simple inflation narrative: falling real rates can support gold even when gold has “emancipate[d] itself from the inflationary trend,” while positive real yields can weigh on sentiment. Its significance lies in real yields serving both as an explanatory factor for gold-price movements and as a potential psychological influence on the market.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** The report’s discussion of inflation dynamics places real interest rates at the center of its explanation for gold’s performance. It observes that “The gold price was able to emancipate itself from the inflationary trend,” thereby separating gold’s behavior from an interpretation based on inflation alone. The report attributes this development primarily to a change in real rates: “which is primarily due to falling real interest rates.” In this framing, the relevant question is not simply whether inflation is present, but how inflation and interest rates combine in real terms.

The 2020 treatment reinforces that emphasis through the relationship between gold and inflation-linked bond markets. “Yields on inflation-linked bonds show an extremely high correlation with the gold price.” This wording presents inflation-linked yields as a particularly close market reference point for gold, and it gives the real-yield argument a measurable counterpart rather than leaving it solely as a broad macroeconomic explanation. Gold’s separation from the inflationary trend is therefore not portrayed as a rejection of inflation’s relevance, but as an indication that falling real rates were the more immediate driver in the report’s account.

**2026** The later technical-analysis discussion applies the same real-yield lens under less favorable conditions for gold. “The US real yield is slightly positive and is not really supporting gold at the moment.” Where the 2020 chapter emphasized falling real rates as a primary explanation for gold’s strength, the 2026 observation identifies slightly positive real yields as an absence of support.

The report illustrates this setting with “3.61% T-bill yield minus 2.3% inflation.” Its conclusion is framed cautiously rather than as a deterministic price forecast: “this may at least create some psychological headwind for the gold market.” The evolution across the two discussions is thus one of contrast within a consistent framework. Falling real interest rates were highlighted as supportive and closely associated with gold-price performance in 2020, whereas slightly positive US real yields in 2026 were characterized as a market headwind, particularly through their psychological effect.
