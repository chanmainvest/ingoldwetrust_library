# Central Bank Gold Demand

Central bank gold demand refers to the sustained purchasing of gold by monetary authorities, presented in the reports as a structural force in the gold market. Its significance lies in the argument that this demand is not merely episodic: it has been persistent since 2009, accelerated sharply in 2022, and is expected to remain an important driver of the gold bull market amid geopolitical and geo-economic confrontation. The reports therefore frame official-sector buying as both a measure of changing global conditions and a source of support for gold.

## How the argument evolved

**2023** The introduction established the historical and quantitative basis for the theme. It noted that “Central banks have been net buyers of gold since 2009,” positioning official-sector demand as an established trend rather than a new development. The report emphasized a major acceleration in purchases, stating that “central banks increased their purchases by 152%, to over 1,136 tons.” This increase was also geographically concentrated, as “Asian central banks again made the bulk of gold purchases.” On this basis, the report expected central-bank demand to help drive the gold bull market.

**2023** The technical-analysis chapter broadened the argument from purchasing data to the global setting behind demand. It argued that “The geopolitical showdown around the reshuffling of the world order is already in full swing,” linking this reshuffling to a more durable change in central-bank behavior. Rather than treating gold buying as a short-lived response to market conditions, the chapter described “The structurally higher demand for gold from central banks” as a key driver of the gold bull market. Central-bank demand thus became part of a larger interpretation: geopolitical realignment was presented as reinforcing demand for gold and strengthening the market’s longer-term foundations.

**2024** The subsequent technical analysis retained this framework while extending it. “The geopolitical showdown continues,” the report stated, but it also stressed that “the showdown is also increasingly taking place on a geo-economic level.” Within this continuing confrontation, the relevant demand condition was no longer simply an acceleration in purchases or an emerging structural shift, but “persistently high central bank demand for gold.” The treatment of the concept consequently evolved into a continuing thesis: official gold demand remained elevated as geopolitical tensions endured and took on an increasingly geo-economic character, preserving its role in the report’s gold-bull-market argument.
