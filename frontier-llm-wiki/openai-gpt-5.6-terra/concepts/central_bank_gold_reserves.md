# Central Bank Gold Reserves

Central bank gold reserves are official holdings of gold maintained and expanded as long-term reserve assets. In the reports, their significance lies in gold’s historical role as a store of value, its performance in crises, and its capacity to diversify geopolitical risk. The evolving treatment of these reserves also places them within a broader movement away from dollar-centric reserve management and toward gold as a neutral asset.

## How the argument evolved

**2021** The report presented central-bank demand as a durable institutional foundation for gold rather than a short-lived market impulse. It noted that “Central banks remained net buyers of gold in 2020,” connecting this behavior to “the history of gold as a long-term store of value.” Gold’s appeal was framed not only in terms of preserving value over time, but also in its role during periods of stress: central banks hold and purchase it for its crisis performance and as a means of navigating an uncertain geopolitical environment.

This early treatment gave particular weight to diversification. Gold was valuable because “it diversifies the geopolitical risk,” distinguishing it from reserve assets more directly tied to individual governments, currencies, or political blocs. Consequently, reserve accumulation was associated with de-dollarization: gold could help central banks reduce reliance on the dollar while retaining an asset with a long-established monetary role. The 2021 argument thus portrayed official gold holdings as a strategic response to both financial uncertainty and the geopolitical concentration embedded in dollar-based reserves.

**2024** The report developed this perspective into a more forceful account of changing reserve preferences. It argued that “foreign central banks are on a rapid goldbuying spree” as Treasuries and the dollar may be losing reserve status. Rather than treating gold purchases principally as a defensive diversification measure, the chapter described a wider rotation in central-bank reserve management: “gold has rotated back into becoming a favored asset among central bankers.”

The emphasis therefore shifted from gold’s usefulness alongside the existing reserve order to its potential place in a changing one. Gold’s lack of attachment to a particular national issuer became central to the argument, with the report stating that “gold is returning to its rightful spot as the global neutral reserve asset.” Across the two chapters, central bank gold reserves move from being evidence of continuing institutional demand for long-term value and geopolitical-risk diversification to a prominent expression of de-dollarization and a possible reordering of global reserve assets.
