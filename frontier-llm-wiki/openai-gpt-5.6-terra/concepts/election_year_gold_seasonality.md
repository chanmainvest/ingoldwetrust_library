# Election-Year Gold Seasonality

Election-Year Gold Seasonality describes the report’s observation that gold’s historical seasonal behavior can differ around the US election cycle and within the calendar year. Its significance lies in treating timing patterns as context for technical analysis: election years were associated with relative weakness in the 2015 discussion, while the 2020 discussion identified recurring seasonal windows of support and weakness. Together, these treatments frame seasonality as a pattern to be monitored rather than a single, uniform signal.

## How the argument evolved

**2015** The report introduced the election-cycle dimension in a notably cautious but directional way. It stated that “gold often tends to be weak in election years,” contrasting this with the observation that “the years preceding election years exhibit a positive trend.” This formulation distinguished between election years and pre-election years rather than presenting the political calendar as a simple bullish or bearish driver. The emphasis was on a recurring historical tendency: weakness could characterize the election year itself, whereas the preceding year had displayed a more positive pattern. In the context of technical analysis and the question of whether gold was approaching the final stages of a bottoming phase, the election-year observation supplied an additional seasonal lens through which market behavior could be assessed.

**2020** The treatment shifted from the broader election-cycle comparison toward a more granular calendar-year map of gold’s seasonal phases. Rather than restating the earlier election-year weakness pattern, the chapter described specific periods in which seasonal conditions changed: “seasonal tailwinds set in from mid-May,” the advance “reversed at the beginning of July,” and “another seasonally strong phase begins in mid-September.” This later account made seasonality more immediate for technical interpretation, identifying not only supportive periods but also an intervening reversal.

Read together, the two discussions show an evolution from a high-level distinction between election and pre-election years to a closer focus on the sequence of seasonal phases within a given year. The 2015 treatment highlighted the possible relevance of the US electoral calendar, while the 2020 treatment emphasized that gold’s seasonality could also involve alternating periods of strength and weakness across the months. The common thread is that seasonal patterns were presented as useful analytical context for gold, with the later chapter supplying a more detailed timetable for that context.
