# Inflation and Price Inflation

Inflation and price inflation are treated as related but distinct phenomena: inflation refers to an increase in the money supply, whereas price inflation denotes the subsequent rise in consumer prices. This distinction is significant because it separates the monetary cause from its visible effect in prices, clarifying how monetary expansion can shape purchasing conditions without reducing the concepts to a single term.

## How the argument evolved

**2014** The report explicitly urged readers to “differentiate between inflation and increasing prices (or price inflation).” Its framing linked a “rising money supply (inflation)” with “higher prices (price inflation),” presenting price increases as connected to monetary inflation while preserving the analytical distinction between the two. Rather than using inflation merely as shorthand for more expensive goods and services, the chapter identified money-supply growth as the underlying monetary development and higher prices as its price-level manifestation.

**2015** The following year retained and sharpened the same conceptual separation. The report again emphasized the need to “differentiate between the terms inflation and rising prices (or ‘price inflation’),” defining the relationship as an “increase in the money supply (inflation)” and “rising prices (price inflation).” This continuity shows that the distinction was not incidental: the report consistently treated inflation as a monetary process and price inflation as the rise in prices associated with it. Across both chapters, the terminology therefore functions as a discipline for discussing inflation—one that keeps attention on the difference between expansion of the money supply and the observable increase in consumer prices.
