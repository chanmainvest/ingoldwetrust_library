# Alternative Payment Systems

Alternative payment systems are payment channels and settlement arrangements designed to operate outside the US dollar and the SWIFT network. In the reports’ de-dollarization framework, they matter because they offer countries practical mechanisms for reducing reliance on dollar-based financial infrastructure. The treatment develops from a European-led institutional response into a broader set of alternatives that includes bilateral agreements, central bank digital currencies, and China’s CIPS.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** The report framed alternative payment systems primarily as a response by Europe and other countries seeking channels that could function independently of the United States’ monetary and payments infrastructure. Its emphasis was on “the establishment of independent payment systems bypassing the US dollar,” linking the desire for autonomy directly to the creation of new payment and settlement arrangements. In the European context, this involved the prospect of “creating a European Monetary Fund and building up an independent SWIFT system.” The argument therefore focused not merely on changing the currency used in transactions, but on developing the institutional capacity to settle them outside the established dollar- and SWIFT-centered framework. The report also described the objective in broad terms: to “create settlement systems independent of the dollar.”

**2023** By 2023, the discussion had broadened from the construction of independent European-style infrastructure to a wider menu of mechanisms for reducing dollar use. The report explicitly grouped the “Establishment of alternative payment systems and bilateral currency agreements” among the avenues that “enable a departure from the US dollar.” This framing placed payment systems alongside direct arrangements between countries, suggesting that de-dollarization could proceed through both new financial networks and bilateral settlement practices. The chapter also incorporated CBDCs into this set of mechanisms, extending the concept beyond conventional cross-border payments infrastructure.

The later treatment additionally identified a concrete non-Western alternative in “the Chinese SWIFT alternative CIPS.” Whereas the 2019 account stressed the aspiration to build independent systems, the 2023 account situated that aspiration within a more diverse and increasingly identifiable landscape of alternatives. Across both chapters, alternative payment systems remain significant because they translate the goal of reducing dollar dependence into operational payment and settlement capabilities.
