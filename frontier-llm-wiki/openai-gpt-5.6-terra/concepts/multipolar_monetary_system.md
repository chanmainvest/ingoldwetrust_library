# Multipolar Monetary System

A multipolar monetary system is a global order in which monetary influence is distributed among several major currencies and geopolitical centers rather than concentrated in the US dollar alone. Across the *In Gold We Trust* reports, de-dollarization is presented as the pathway toward this arrangement: first as an emerging multilateral framework, then as a gradual rebalancing of currency roles, and finally as an already-arrived shift beyond the “unipolar moment.” Its significance lies in the changing relationship between economic power, reserve currencies, and the organization of the international monetary system.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** The report introduced de-dollarization as a movement “on the way to a multilateral monetary world.” Rather than depicting a simple replacement of the dollar by a single rival, it framed the prospective order as a “multipolar system” based on three to five currencies. The dollar, euro, and renminbi were identified as central components of this framework, suggesting that the emerging monetary architecture would distribute influence among several large currency blocs. Europe’s participation in de-dollarization was thus treated as part of a broader transition away from a dollar-centered system toward a more plural monetary structure.

**2020** The argument became more explicit about the mismatch between existing dollar dominance and the underlying shape of the world economy. The report described the task as one of seeking to “build a system worthy of the diverse, multipolar global economy,” while emphasizing that the transition would follow “a slow path to a multipolar world.” This formulation retained the dollar’s importance rather than forecasting its immediate displacement: the US dollar, euro, and renminbi were described as currencies that “play roles as oil and reserve currencies.” Multipolarity therefore appeared not as an abrupt monetary rupture, but as a gradual redistribution of the functions historically concentrated in the dollar.

**2023** The treatment shifted from expectation to declaration. The report described a world moving from US-dollar-centered unipolarity toward multiple geopolitical and monetary centers, with actors “openly talking about a new world order that has more than one center.” Its assessment was more definitive: “the time of multipolarity has already arrived” and “The unipolar moment has passed.” In this later framing, the question was no longer merely whether a multilateral monetary world might emerge, but how the consequences of an already multipolar geopolitical environment would shape the monetary order and the dollar’s position within it.
