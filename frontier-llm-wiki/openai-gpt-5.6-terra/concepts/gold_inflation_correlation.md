# Gold Inflation Correlation

Gold Inflation Correlation describes the report’s view that gold’s relationship with inflation becomes most pronounced not under ordinary conditions, but when inflation reaches unusually high or “extreme” levels. Its significance lies in framing gold as an asset whose inflation sensitivity is conditional: the reported correlation strengthens during periods of severe inflation, making historical high-inflation episodes central to the argument.

## How the argument evolved

**2009** The report introduced the relationship in direct terms: “The development of the gold price is strongly correlated with inflation” “as soon as inflation hits extreme levels.” Rather than presenting gold and inflation as uniformly linked across all environments, this formulation placed the emphasis on a threshold condition—extreme inflation. The chapter supported that emphasis with a historical reference to “the period of high inflation from 1978 to 1982,” during which the reported correlation was 0.76. This evidence made the argument concrete: gold’s connection with inflation was presented as especially strong in an identifiable period of elevated inflation rather than as an abstract or constant market rule.

**2010** The subsequent treatment preserved the central conditional argument while expressing it with even stronger language. The report stated that “The gold price correlates very strongly with inflation” “as soon as the latter hits extreme values.” This continued the earlier focus on inflation’s extremes as the setting in which the relationship matters most, while shifting the wording from the development of the gold price to the gold price itself. The chapter also introduced a monthly measure, stating that “the monthly correlation coefficient of gold and the inflation rate was 0.48.” Together, the two annual treatments established a consistent narrative: the gold–inflation relationship should be understood through the behavior of inflation at extreme values and through historical measures of correlation, including the high-inflation experience of 1978–1982.
