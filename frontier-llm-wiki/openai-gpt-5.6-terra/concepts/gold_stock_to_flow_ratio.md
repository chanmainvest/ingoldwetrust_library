# Gold Stock-to-Flow Ratio

Gold’s stock-to-flow ratio compares the existing stock of gold with the annual flow of newly produced metal. Its significance rests on gold’s unusually large above-ground stock and limited annual mine supply: the ratio is “measured in decades,” helping explain why gold is associated with scarcity and comparatively low inflationary pressure from new production.

## How the argument evolved

**2022** The report grounded gold’s stock-to-flow characteristics in the physical structure of the gold market. Unlike commodities whose available supply is rapidly consumed or replenished, “virtually all of that gold is still held in human hands.” As a result, total accumulated above-ground gold is large relative to the annual addition from mining, because “total annual mining is a small percentage of total existing gold.” The report therefore characterized gold’s stock-to-flow ratio as one that “for gold is measured in decades.” This framing emphasized that the relevant supply base is not merely current mine production, but the extensive stock already held above ground.

**2025** The treatment became more explicit and conceptual, defining the metric itself: “The stock-to-flow ratio compares the current stock of a commodity” “against the flow of new production.” Building on the earlier observation that gold’s stock vastly exceeds annual mining output, the report linked a high ratio to scarcity, stating that “A high ratio, typical for gold or Bitcoin, indicates scarcity”. It further associated this scarcity with lower inflationary pressure, since new production represents a relatively limited increment to the existing stock. In this development, stock-to-flow moved from an explanation of gold’s supply-and-demand fundamentals to a broader framework for understanding why gold’s existing supply profile distinguishes it from commodities more dependent on ongoing production.
