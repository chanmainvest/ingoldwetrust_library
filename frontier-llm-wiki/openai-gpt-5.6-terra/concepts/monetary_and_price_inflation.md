# Monetary and Price Inflation

Monetary and price inflation are treated as distinct but connected concepts: monetary inflation concerns an expansion of the money supply, while price inflation concerns the resulting rise in prices. This distinction is significant because it identifies a causal sequence rather than using “inflation” as a single, undifferentiated label. Across the reports, the argument stresses that rising prices are a consequence of monetary inflation, not its definition.

## How the argument evolved

**2016** The report establishes the conceptual foundation by emphasizing the “difference between monetary inflation and price inflation.” Its formulation is direct: “Monetary inflation is the cause, price inflation is its consequence.” The treatment therefore separates the monetary process from the price-level outcome. Inflation, in this framing, cannot be reduced simply to observed price increases, because those increases belong to the consequence side of the relationship. The distinction makes the report’s terminology explicitly causal: monetary inflation comes first, and price inflation follows from it.

**2018** The later discussion restates and further specifies this distinction. It notes that inflation “originally means ‘monetary inflation’” and identifies that monetary inflation with “the expansion of the money supply.” Price inflation is then defined more precisely as “price inflation (i.e., continuously rising prices),” while retaining the earlier causal ordering: it “is its consequence.” The development from 2016 is therefore one of clarification rather than reversal. The earlier cause-and-consequence distinction is preserved, while the meaning of monetary inflation is made explicit and the nature of price inflation is described as continuously rising prices.

Taken together, the two chapters present a consistent vocabulary for discussing inflation. Monetary inflation refers to money-supply expansion; price inflation refers to continuously rising prices. The reports’ central point is not merely that the two can be distinguished, but that their distinction explains their relationship: the monetary expansion is the cause, and the rise in prices is the consequence. This framing places attention on the monetary source of inflation rather than treating the resulting movement in prices as the whole phenomenon.
