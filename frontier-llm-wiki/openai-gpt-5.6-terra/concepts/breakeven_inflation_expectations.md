# Breakeven Inflation Expectations

Breakeven inflation expectations are the inflation outlook implied by investors, measured through the difference between nominal government-bond yields and those of inflation-linked bonds. In the reports, they serve as a market-based signal of changing inflation concerns and of whether investors anticipate deflation. The 2020 and 2022 discussions trace a movement from modestly renewed inflation concern to markedly higher expected inflation.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** The report presented break-even rates as a window into investor-implied inflation expectations at a time when inflation concerns were beginning to return, albeit cautiously. It observed that “inflation concerns have risen slightly again, according to the break-even rates,” while also giving the market’s five-year expectation a precise, subdued form: “investors expect the inflation rate to average 0.76% over the next five years.” This was not an argument for an imminent inflation surge. Rather, the break-even reading was used to establish that the market did not foresee a repeat of the severe deflationary episode associated with the global financial crisis: “A deflationary development as in 2008/09 is not expected so far.” In this framing, breakevens helped distinguish a low-inflation environment from an expectation of outright deflation.

**2022** By 2022, the report stated the construction of the measure explicitly, calling it “the break-even rate, i.e. the yield differential between nominal government bonds and inflation-linked bonds.” The focus had shifted from the limited re-emergence of inflation concern in 2020 to the magnitude and historical prominence of the increase. The break-even rate “has rallied to almost 3%,” reaching “its highest level in two decades.” Thus, the later discussion gave the same market indicator a more forceful role: rather than merely signaling that 2008/09-style deflation was not expected, it documented a sharp rise in the inflation premium embedded in government-bond markets. Taken together, the two chapters show breakevens as a contemporaneous measure through which the reports tracked a changing inflation narrative—from modest expectations and the absence of anticipated deflation to a substantially elevated market-implied inflation outlook.
