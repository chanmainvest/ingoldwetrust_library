# Persistent Core Inflation

Persistent core inflation describes inflationary pressure that remains embedded even as headline measures may ease, particularly when underlying prices and services continue to rise. In the *In Gold We Trust* reports, this persistence matters because it challenges expectations of a rapid return to low inflation and points to inflationary dynamics that are more durable than temporary headline shocks.

## How the argument evolved

**2023** The report framed the relationship between core and headline inflation as an important diagnostic of persistence. It observed that “core inflation, is now higher than the headline inflation rate,” treating this reversal as “another sign that inflation is more persistent than expected.” Rather than viewing a lower headline rate alone as evidence that the inflation problem had been resolved, the chapter emphasized that the underlying measure had become comparatively stronger. The implication was that inflation’s apparent moderation could obscure continued pressure in the components captured by core inflation.

**2024** The discussion became more specific about where this underlying persistence was occurring. While the report noted that “the situation is different for core inflation,” it stressed that inflation “remains above the 3% mark in the US.” The chapter identified the services sector as a key reason that core inflation had not subsided more decisively: “Stubbornly high inflation in the services sector is a major contributor.” This developed the earlier argument from a broad warning about core inflation exceeding headline inflation into a more focused account of the mechanisms sustaining it. Core inflation was therefore presented not simply as a lagging indicator, but as evidence that inflationary pressure could remain entrenched even when other measures suggested improvement.

Across the two years, the report’s treatment moved from recognizing the divergence between core and headline inflation to locating the persistence in services. The common thread is caution against interpreting softer headline readings as a complete normalization of inflation: elevated core inflation signals that the underlying trend may be more resilient than expected.
