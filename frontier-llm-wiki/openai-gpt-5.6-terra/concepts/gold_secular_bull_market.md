# Gold Secular Bull Market

A gold secular bull market is presented as a long-duration upward trend that can encompass sharp corrections, shifting market structures, and changing sources of demand without losing its broader direction. Across the reports, the concept matters because it frames gold not as a trade defined by short-term volatility, but as an asset whose advance is supported by persistent monetary, technical, and geopolitical developments.

## How the argument evolved

**2008** The report’s initial treatment emphasizes the distinction between a temporary decline and a reversal of the larger trend. Gold’s correction is described as “an overdue and technically necessary correction,” but this adjustment is explicitly situated “within the setting of a secular bull market.” The implication is that weakness should be interpreted in relation to the longer cycle rather than in isolation: the report continued to “expect the gold price to rise further.” In this formulation, the secular bull market provides the context that makes a correction compatible with a constructive outlook.

**2016** The argument is reaffirmed with greater continuity and a specific forward expectation. The report states that “gold was still in a secular bull market,” maintaining the view despite the intervening market developments. This conviction was connected to an “ambitious price target of USD 2,300 by June of 2018,” with the authors stating that “we are maintaining our price target.” The secular-bull-market thesis therefore became not only an explanation for prior volatility, but also the basis for retaining a long-term valuation outlook.

**2026** The technical analysis recasts the bull-market case as a new phase rather than merely a continuation of an existing price trend. It identifies “a paradigm shift that heralded a new phase of the bull market,” grounded in changes to the forces shaping gold’s price formation. These include “central bank purchases, Asian price formation, geopolitical remonetization,” while the market’s positioning remains supportive because “Speculation in the futures markets is not excessive.” The report also points to neutral sentiment, sidelined buyers, and mining-stock strength as features consistent with further upside. By this stage, the secular-bull-market argument has broadened from resilience through correction and confidence in a price target to a structural account of evolving demand, market leadership, and technical conditions.
