# Gold in De-Dollarization

Gold in de-dollarization refers to gold’s role in the gradual effort to reduce reliance on the US dollar in trade, reserves, and the international monetary system. Across the reports, gold is presented not as a peripheral asset but as a central instrument in the search for alternatives to dollar dependence. Its significance lies in the connection between geopolitical divergence, changing reserve preferences, and the desire for monetary autonomy.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** The report framed gold as an important component of a slow-moving shift away from the dollar-centered order. It described a process in which “gold plays a major role in this slow departure from the US dollar,” linking de-dollarization to the renewed relevance of gold in official monetary strategy. Global central-bank activity supplied a concrete expression of this theme: “Global central banks have recently bought more gold than they have in decades.” In this treatment, gold’s role was closely associated with reserve diversification and with the reassessment of the dollar’s monetary position.

The historical backdrop also mattered. The report characterized “the US turning away from gold in 1971 as a historical mistake,” placing contemporary interest in gold within a longer critique of the post-gold-dollar monetary arrangement. Gold therefore appeared both as a reserve asset being accumulated in the present and as a reference point for evaluating the monetary changes that had reinforced the dollar’s international role. De-dollarization was not depicted as a sudden replacement of the dollar, but as a gradual departure in which gold could serve an important function.

**2021** The argument broadened from reserve behavior and the legacy of 1971 toward a more explicitly geopolitical account. The report linked geopolitical divergence with expanding demand for alternatives, observing that “The quest for new trading and reserve currencies continues to grow.” De-dollarization was thus treated as an advancing process rather than merely an isolated discussion of central-bank gold accumulation: “de-dollarization is advancing.”

Within that evolving setting, the report made gold’s importance especially direct. “Gold plays a central role in this emancipation from the US dollar.” The wording sharpened the earlier portrayal of gold as a major participant in a slow departure. By 2021, gold was positioned at the intersection of alternative trading arrangements, reserve-currency ambitions, and the broader pursuit of reduced dependence on the United States dollar. Taken together, the chapters present gold as a monetary asset whose relevance rises alongside the search for greater independence in an increasingly geopolitically divergent world.
