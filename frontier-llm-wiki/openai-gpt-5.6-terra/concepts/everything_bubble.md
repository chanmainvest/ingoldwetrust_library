# Everything Bubble

The Everything Bubble describes a broad, economy-wide inflation of asset values rather than excess confined to a single market. In the In Gold We Trust reports, it is significant because it frames buoyant conditions in financial and real assets as the product of monetary expansion and an unusually prolonged economic cycle. The concept therefore shifts attention from isolated market booms to the systemic breadth of the apparent prosperity.

## How the argument evolved

**2017** The report introduced the idea as an “illusionary bubble economy created by money supply inflation.” Its central contention was that the resulting excess could not be understood as a sector-specific phenomenon: “the excess is not limited to certain sectors.” Instead, the bubble encompassed equities, bonds, and property simultaneously, warranting the label an “Everything Bubble.” This framing emphasized the common monetary backdrop behind multiple asset classes and presented the breadth of the boom itself as the defining feature.

**2019** The report retained “The Everything Bubble” as the name for the phenomenon while describing it through a wider set of visible manifestations. Tax relief and an extended expansion supported the view that “the economic cycle could actually be extended once again.” In that setting, the boom was not limited to stock markets: “stock markets but also corporate bonds, luxury real estate, and works of art boomed.” The concept thus developed from a diagnosis of monetary-inflation-driven excess across core asset markets into a portrayal of a broad boom reaching financial assets, high-end property, and collectible real assets alike.

Across the two treatments, the Everything Bubble remains a label for synchronized appreciation across otherwise distinct markets. The 2017 account foregrounds money-supply inflation and the economy-wide character of the excess, while the 2019 account highlights the persistence of the cycle and the expanded range of assets participating in the boom. Together, they depict a condition in which market strength is notable not merely for its scale in one area, but for its reach across the investment landscape.
