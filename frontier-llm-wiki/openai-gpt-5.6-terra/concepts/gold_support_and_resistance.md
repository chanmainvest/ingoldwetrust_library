# Gold Support and Resistance

Gold support and resistance describes the price areas that the reports treated as crucial tests of the market’s bottoming process and potential recovery. Repeatedly successful tests of support were presented as evidence of a durable low, while USD 1,530 remained the key upside threshold whose clearance would confront “massive resistance.” Across 2014 and 2015, the analysis refined the location of near-term support but retained the same central resistance benchmark.

## How the argument evolved

**2014** The report characterized gold’s technical position through a support zone between USD 1,250 and USD 1,270. This area had been subjected to repeated market tests: “The support zone between USD 1,250 and USD 1,270 has by now been successfully tested several times.” Rather than treating these tests as a sign of continuing weakness, the analysis linked them to an expectation that the bottoming phase was nearing completion and that “we expect higher prices in coming months.” The anticipated advance, however, was not portrayed as unconstrained. USD 1,530 was identified as the decisive overhead obstacle: “The USD 1,530 level should represent a massive resistance level.” Support therefore served as the foundation for a constructive outlook, while resistance marked the level at which that outlook would face its most substantial technical challenge.

**2015** The subsequent analysis preserved this framework while relocating the support area lower, to between 1,140 and 1,170. Again, the emphasis was on repeated validation rather than a single price event: “The support area between 1140 and 1170 has been tested successfully several times by now.” The report added a more specific chart-pattern interpretation, arguing that “the large double-bottom formation of November 2014 and March 2015 represents a solid low.” This made support not merely a range that had held, but part of a broader formation understood to indicate a reliable bottom.

The resistance assessment remained notably consistent between the two years. Despite the lower support range and the identification of the double bottom, the report still held that “the level of 1,530 would likely represent massive resistance.” The evolving argument thus distinguished between evidence that gold had established a base and the separate task of overcoming the long-standing resistance level. Repeated support tests and the double-bottom formation supported the case for stabilization, but the 1,530 threshold continued to define the major technical hurdle for a more decisive upward move.
