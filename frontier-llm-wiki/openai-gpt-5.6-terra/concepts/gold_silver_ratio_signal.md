# Gold-Silver Ratio Signal

The gold-silver ratio is used in the reports as a market-based signal linking the relative performance of gold and silver to inflation dynamics, monetary conditions, and the probability of a gold bull market. Its direction matters more than any single level: a rising ratio is generally read as a deflationary warning, while a falling ratio suggests stronger inflation momentum and a more constructive setting for gold and especially silver. The signal is therefore both a macroeconomic indicator and a tactical guide for precious-metals investors.

## How the argument evolved

**2015** The report introduced the ratio as an “excellent indicator of the interaction between inflation and deflation.” In this framing, gold’s relative strength against silver—expressed in a rising gold-silver ratio—pointed toward disinflationary or deflationary pressure and constituted “a warning signal for gold investors.” Yet the interpretation was not simply a short-term trading rule: the report also stressed that “a reversion to the mean has to take place over the long term,” placing extreme relative valuations within a longer cyclical process.

**2016** The treatment became more explicitly tied to identifying a durable gold advance. The report argued that “a sustainable rally in the gold price is improbable if the gold/silver ratio rises concurrently,” whereas “a declining g/s ratio significantly increases the probability that gold has entered a bull market.” A “renewed downtrend in the ratio” was consequently presented as a positive signal for gold, connecting silver’s relative strength with accelerating price-inflation momentum.

**2018** The technical-analysis discussion reinforced this relationship. A falling ratio again “significantly increases the probability of a bull market in gold,” but the emphasis also clarified the expected relative-performance implication: “silver would outperform gold.” The ratio thus served as confirmation not only of a more favorable gold-market regime but also of “rising inflation momentum.”

**2020** In the inflation-dynamics chapter, the report maintained the same core framework: “A falling G/S ratio significantly increases the probability of a bull market in gold.” A “new downward trend in the ratio” would simultaneously point toward a more positive gold outlook and “a rising inflation dynamic on the other.” Later that year, technical analysis applied the ratio more directly as a silver timing signal. After reaching “a new all-time high in mid-March at 1:125,” the GSR was said to have provided “a clear buy signal for silver” since April 20th; that bullish signal would be reversed only by a further strong rise to 124.

**2022** The report distilled the indicator into a monetary interpretation. “A rising ratio signals deflationary monetary circumstances,” while “a falling ratio, i.e. silver gaining in relative value,” signals “an expansion in monetary supply.” This formulation unified the earlier observations: relative silver strength can indicate expanding monetary conditions, rising inflation momentum, and an environment more supportive of a gold bull market.
