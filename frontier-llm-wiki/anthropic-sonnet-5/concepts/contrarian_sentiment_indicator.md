# Contrarian Sentiment Indicator

The Contrarian Sentiment Indicator is a technical-analysis tool used throughout the In Gold We Trust reports to gauge the psychological extremes of gold market participants — investors, newsletter writers, and fund managers — on the premise that crowd sentiment is most reliable at its extremes. When sentiment readings are extremely bullish or "euphoric," the reports treat this as a warning sign of an impending top; when sentiment is extremely negative or depressed, this is read as a bullish signal that a bottom is near or has already formed. Because gold sentiment tends to swing quickly and sharply, especially during corrections, this indicator became a recurring pillar of the reports' technical case for gold's medium- and long-term prospects.

## How the argument evolved

In **2011**, the indicator was introduced in the context of a still-rising gold price. The authors observed that sentiment "turns bearish very quickly during corrections," yet even amid strong gains, "the gold price is still nowhere near any excessive euphoria." They benchmarked current readings against the prior all-time high, noting the sentiment index was then "54 percentage points higher," implying substantial room for further appreciation before euphoric conditions — and thus a market top — could be expected. At this stage the tool was used primarily to reassure readers that the bull market was not yet overextended.

By **2013**, the market backdrop had shifted dramatically, and so had the indicator's application. With gold under heavy pressure, sentiment measures — including newsletter writer allocations — collapsed to historic lows, with recommended allocations hitting "minus 44%, an all-time low." Rather than reading this as confirmation of a bearish trend, the report inverted the signal: sentiment being "at the most negative level since the beginning of the bull market" was framed as cause "to be clearly positive about the long term." This marked the indicator's transformation into an explicit contrarian bottoming tool, used to argue that capitulation-level pessimism among market participants historically precedes major lows.

The **2014** chapter reinforced and extended this contrarian bottoming thesis. Fund manager surveys showed that "only 4% of all fund managers currently regard gold as overvalued," while broader sentiment metrics confirmed "there is currently anything but excessive euphoria in the gold market." Echoing 2013's language almost verbatim, the report again characterized sentiment "at the most negative level since the beginning of the bull market" as "clearly positive," reinforcing the view that the bottoming phase was nearly complete. Across these three years, the indicator's narrative arc moved from monitoring the absence of euphoria at high prices (2011), to identifying capitulation-level pessimism as a buying signal during the downturn (2013), to reaffirming that same extreme-negative-sentiment thesis as corroborating evidence that a durable low was forming (2014).
