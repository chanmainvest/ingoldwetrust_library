# Fed Average Inflation Targeting

Fed Average Inflation Targeting refers to the Federal Reserve's shift away from a strict, point-in-time 2% inflation ceiling toward a framework that tolerates overshoots so that inflation averages 2% over time. In the In Gold We Trust reports, this policy shift is treated as a pivotal and consequential change in monetary policy doctrine — one that, by design or by consequence, loosens the constraint on inflation and has lasting implications for the gold thesis, real interest rates, and the credibility of the Fed's price-stability mandate.

## How the argument evolved

The concept first appears in **2020**, still in a speculative, forward-looking register. At that point the Federal Reserve was reported to be *"considering introducing a rule that would allow the inflation rate to overshoot its 2 percent target"* rather than treating 2% as a hard ceiling. The report frames this as a deliberate response to entrenched low inflation: the idea being *"that it is acceptable that for an average of 2 percent, one cannot have observations that are below 2 percent"* — meaning periods of below-target inflation should be offset by periods above target. This nascent policy proposal is labeled with the term that would come to define it, *"makeup inflation,"* signaling that the Fed was actively weighing a structural change to how it interprets its own mandate, even before the policy was formally adopted.

By **2025**, the report looks back on this shift as an accomplished and consequential fact rather than a possibility. It notes plainly that *"the Federal Reserve has been aiming to achieve its inflation target on average since August 2020,"* confirming that the makeup-inflation framework floated in 2020 was indeed implemented. Crucially, the 2025 treatment sharpens the critique: average inflation targeting is reframed not as a technical refinement but as *"nothing more than a covert increase in the US inflation target."* The report unpacks the mathematical logic of this critique, observing that to genuinely average 2% after a period of overshoot, *"US monetary policy would have to aim for an inflation rate of significantly below 2% for several years to achieve the inflation target of 2% on average"* — a discipline the report implies the Fed has shown little appetite for enforcing. This retrospective framing transforms the concept from a well-intentioned policy tweak into evidence of a structural, and arguably permanent, loosening of the Fed's inflation ceiling, one with direct bearing on the erosion of purchasing power and the case for gold as a beneficiary of sustained, tolerated inflation.

Together, the two chapters trace an arc: from a tentative 2020 proposal designed to prevent inflation from staying too low, to a 2025 verdict that the policy has effectively functioned as a quiet upward revision of the inflation target — a shift the reports treat as structurally favorable to the inflation and monetary-debasement narrative underpinning the broader gold thesis.
