# Seasonal Price Patterns

Seasonal price patterns refer to recurring, calendar-based tendencies in the price behavior of gold and silver that repeat across multiple years, often tied to the phase of the U.S. presidential election cycle. In the In Gold We Trust report's technical analysis chapters, this concept underpins the annually recurring observation that both metals face predictable headwinds in late spring before reversing into a stronger summer uptrend. Its significance lies in offering technically minded investors a recurring, cycle-aware framework for timing entries and interpreting short-term weakness as consistent with historical patterns rather than a break in the longer-term bullish thesis.

## How the argument evolved

The seasonal-pattern argument first appears clearly in **2021**, framed within the context of a post-election year. The report observes that "seasonal headwinds set in from the end of May but reverse again at the beginning of July," and specifically flags June as historically troublesome, noting it is "after January – the second weakest month in post-election years." Silver is highlighted as the metal with the more pronounced seasonal turn, showing "a strong seasonal uptrend... from July." This year establishes the core template: a spring slump followed by a summer recovery, calibrated to the particular point in the four-year electoral cycle.

In **2022**, the analysis is recalibrated for a midterm election year rather than a post-election one, but the underlying pattern proves remarkably stable. The report repeats almost verbatim that "seasonal headwinds set in from the end of May, but reverse again at the beginning of July," reinforcing that the May-to-July window is a structural feature of gold and silver seasonality rather than an artifact of any single electoral phase. The characterization of June as the low point is sharpened further, with the report stating that "a strong seasonal uptrend can be seen from July. June marks the weakest month" — collapsing the prior year's more nuanced ranking (second-weakest after January) into a blunter, more emphatic claim that June is simply the trough of the year.

By **2023**, the framing shifts again to a pre-election year, and the report continues to find the same rhythm holding: "seasonal headwinds set in from the end of May but reverse again at the beginning of July." What is new this year is a more explicit decoupling of silver's behavior from gold's, with the report noting that "Silver also shows a seasonal downtrend until July," implying that silver's weakness may extend slightly longer or run somewhat independently of gold's dip before both metals recover in tandem. Across all three years, the persistence of the identical late-May-to-July language signals that the analysts treat this as a durable, near-mechanical seasonal regularity — one robust enough to be restated each year regardless of where the current year sits in the presidential cycle, even as the accompanying electoral-cycle commentary (post-election, midterm, pre-election) is updated to match.
