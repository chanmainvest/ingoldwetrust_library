# Gold/Silver Ratio Dynamics

The gold/silver ratio — the number of ounces of silver required to buy one ounce of gold — is a technical barometer of relative strength between the two metals that the In Gold We Trust reports use to gauge the health and stage of gold bull markets. Its extremes and trend reversals are treated as signals of shifting inflation expectations and monetary regime change, making it a recurring tool for timing calls on both gold and silver performance.

## How the argument evolved

**2018** established the foundational framework: the ratio's rise and fall was explicitly tied to gold bull markets and inflation expectations, with the report asserting that "a sustainable increase in the gold price is unlikely to happen in tandem with an increase in the gold/silver ratio." This framed a falling ratio as bullish confirmation, since "a falling gold/silver ratio significantly increases the probability of a bull market in gold." To ground this claim historically, the chapter pointed to the dramatic swings of the early 1980s and beyond, noting the ratio "hitting a low of 16x around 1980 and then reaching 100x in 1991" — evidence that the ratio can move across extraordinarily wide extremes depending on the macro-monetary backdrop.

**2026** returned to the ratio with a much more granular, chart-based technical lens, situating its behavior within a multi-year trend channel dating back to the pandemic era. The analysis identified a precise turning point, observing that "the gold/silver ratio turned precisely at 43.57 on the lower boundary of the major downtrend channel originating from the March 2020 COVID crash." This followed what the report described as a long structural decline — "a nine-month decline from 107 to 43.57" — before the ratio staged a counter-move. Unlike the 2018 framing, which treated a falling ratio as unambiguously bullish for gold, the 2026 discussion drew a more nuanced, silver-focused conclusion: the rebound off the channel's lower boundary "likely implies not only a relatively weak silver performance" going forward, and the report entertained the possibility that "a prolonged consolidation or countertrend move could theoretically push the ratio back toward 82." This represents an evolution from a broad, cycle-level heuristic linking the ratio to gold bull markets toward a precise, channel-based technical model forecasting near-term relative underperformance in silver specifically.

Taken together, the two chapters bookend a shift in how the reports have used the ratio: 2018 treated it as a macro confirmation tool for gold's bull-market thesis, rooted in historical extremes from the 1980s and 1990s, while 2026 applied a disciplined technical-channel methodology to extract a tactical, silver-specific implication from the ratio's current position. The throughline is that the gold/silver ratio remains a durable diagnostic across nearly a decade of reports, even as the analytical apparatus applied to it has grown more quantitatively precise.
