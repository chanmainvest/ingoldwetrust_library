# Analyst Price Forecast Consensus

Analyst Price Forecast Consensus refers to the aggregated Bloomberg survey of professional analysts' gold and silver price targets, tracked year-over-year in the In Gold We Trust report's technical analysis chapters. The report treats this consensus not as a reliable predictive tool but as a sentiment gauge — its tendency to extrapolate recent price action into flat or declining forward targets is presented as evidence of structurally procyclical, backward-looking analyst behavior, often standing in tension with the report's own bullish technical read of the gold and silver charts.

## How the argument evolved

**2021** introduced this theme by juxtaposing a bullish technical setup for gold and silver with a Bloomberg consensus that told a much more subdued story. Analysts expected "a median price of USD 1,777" for gold, but the multi-year trajectory was one of decline: "USD 1,700 (2022), USD 1,650 (2023), USD 1,610 (2024) and USD 1,624 (2025)." Silver followed the same pattern, with "a median price of USD 25.50 is expected, followed by a fall to USD 23.70 in 2022." The report framed this downward drift through 2025 as symptomatic of procyclical forecasting — analysts projecting recent price weakness forward rather than anticipating a turn, even as chart-based signals argued for upside.

**2022** carried the same analytical framework forward, updating the numbers while reaffirming the underlying critique. The gold consensus had shifted to "a median price of USD 1,900," but the multi-year shape remained essentially flat-to-declining, prompting the report to conclude bluntly that "the analysts therefore expect a sideways movement" through 2026. Silver's consensus again showed the same near-term erosion pattern, with "a median price of USD 24 is expected, followed by a drop to USD 22.94 in 2023." By repeating this comparison a second year running, the report reinforced its thesis that the analyst consensus mechanism itself is structurally procyclical — anchored to trailing price levels rather than forward-looking macro or technical catalysts — and therefore should be read by investors as a contrarian or sentiment signal rather than a genuine forecast.

Across both years, the consensus data serves the same rhetorical purpose: it is presented alongside more optimistic technical chart analysis to highlight a gap between what analysts extrapolate and what the report's own methodology suggests is possible. The consistency of the pattern — declining or sideways targets in both 2021 and 2022, despite different starting price levels — is used to argue that the professional forecasting community persistently underestimates gold and silver's upside potential during periods when the technical picture is constructive.
