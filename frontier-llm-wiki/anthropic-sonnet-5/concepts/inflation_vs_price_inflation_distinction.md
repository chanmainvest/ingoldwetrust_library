# Inflation vs Price Inflation Distinction

The 'In Gold We Trust' reports insist on a conceptual separation between **inflation**, understood as an expansion of the money supply, and **price inflation**, the resulting rise in consumer prices. This distinction matters because conflating the two obscures the monetary root cause of rising prices and undermines clear analysis of gold's role as a monetary asset. By keeping the terms apart, the reports build a foundation for arguing that gold should be understood primarily in relation to monetary debasement rather than headline CPI figures alone.

## How the argument evolved

**2014** established the terminological groundwork for this distinction. The report argued that "it makes sense to differentiate between **inflation and increasing prices (or price inflation)**," framing inflation strictly as a monetary phenomenon and price inflation as its visible market consequence. It further noted that "the causal connection between a rising money supply (inflation) and higher prices (price inflation) is barely disputed by economists," signaling that while the mechanism is widely accepted in principle, public and media discourse routinely blurs the two concepts together.

**2015** reaffirmed and reinforced this same conceptual framework almost verbatim, underscoring its continued centrality to the report's analytical approach. The chapter stated that "it is crucial to differentiate between the terms **inflation and rising prices (or 'price inflation')**," elevating the distinction from something that merely "makes sense" to something the authors deemed essential for sound reasoning. It repeated the point that "most economists don't dispute the causal connection between an increase in the money supply (inflation) and rising prices (price inflation)," preserving the earlier year's acknowledgment of broad economic consensus on causality even as popular usage continues to muddy the terms.

Across both editions, the argument does not so much evolve in substance as it solidifies into a recurring definitional anchor: the reports use this year-over-year restatement to ensure readers approach subsequent discussions of gold, monetary policy, and consumer prices with the correct conceptual lens. The consistency of the wording — from "makes sense to differentiate" in 2014 to "crucial to differentiate" in 2015 — suggests a deliberate sharpening of emphasis, reflecting the authors' view that this distinction is not a minor semantic nuance but a necessary prerequisite for understanding gold's relationship to monetary conditions rather than to short-term price movements alone.
