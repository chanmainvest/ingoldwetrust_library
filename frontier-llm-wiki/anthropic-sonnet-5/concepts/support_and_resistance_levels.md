# Support and Resistance Levels

Support and resistance levels are price zones on the gold chart that have historically halted or reversed price movements, serving as technical markers for where buying or selling pressure is expected to emerge. In the In Gold We Trust reports, these levels form the backbone of technical analysis used to argue that gold's bear market is approaching, or has reached, a bottoming phase — a thesis with significant implications for timing entry points and gauging the health of the broader gold trend.

## How the argument evolved

**2014** established the core technical framework for reading gold's price action through support and resistance zones. The report pointed to the support zone between USD 1,250 and USD 1,270 as a critical floor, noting that it "has by now been successfully tested several times" — repeated tests being read as evidence of a durable base rather than a level on the verge of breaking down. Looking further out, the analysis flagged USD 1,530 as a formidable ceiling, arguing "the USD 1,530 level should represent a massive resistance level on the upside." Underlying both observations was the classic technical principle invoked explicitly in this chapter: "support becomes resistance, resistance becomes support," a rule that would frame how prior lows and highs were interpreted as the market evolved.

**2015** carried this framework forward but shifted the key support zone lower, to the 1140–1170 range, reflecting the market's continued decline since the prior year. As before, this new support was validated by repetition, "tested successfully several times by now," reinforcing the report's confidence in identifying a genuine floor rather than a temporary pause. The 2015 analysis also introduced a specific chart pattern to bolster the case — the "large double-bottom formation of November 2014 and March 2015," which was described as representing "a solid low." This pattern gave structural, visual confirmation to the support level, strengthening the argument that the bottoming process was advancing. On the resistance side, the 1,530 level carried over unchanged from 2014, with the report reaffirming that it "would likely represent massive resistance" — a continuity that shows the same support-becomes-resistance logic still anchoring the long-term technical outlook a year later.

Across the two years, the narrative shows a consistent methodology applied to a moving target: as gold's price found new lows, the identified support zone was revised downward (from 1,250–1,270 to 1,140–1,170), while the distant resistance ceiling at 1,530 remained a fixed technical marker, repeatedly cited as the level that would eventually need to be overcome for the bull market to resume. The persistence of the double-bottom pattern language in 2015 signals a maturing of the bottoming-phase thesis, moving from a single tested support zone toward a fuller chart pattern used to argue that the final stages of the bottoming process were near.
