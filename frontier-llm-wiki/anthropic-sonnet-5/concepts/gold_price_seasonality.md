# Gold Price Seasonality

Gold Price Seasonality refers to the recurring, calendar-based patterns in gold's price performance that the In Gold We Trust reports have tracked across editions — most notably a tendency toward weakness in the middle of the year and strength in the fourth and first quarters. This pattern is significant because it has repeatedly been cited as a technical explanation for otherwise puzzling sideways or corrective phases in the gold price, and it offers traders and long-term investors a framework for timing entries independent of macro fundamentals.

## How the argument evolved

The seasonality thesis was already fully formed in **2011**, when the report identified "an essential reason for the recent sideways phase of the gold price" as being rooted in "the profound seasonality" of gold demand. The explanation offered was structural: Indian wedding season and Diwali festival demand drive buying at specific times of year, producing a pattern in which "the fourth and the first quarter of the year show the best performance, whereas the gold price corrects significantly in the second quarter." Crucially, the report was careful to frame this as a probabilistic rather than deterministic pattern, noting that "this seasonality has been observed in 75-80% of the cases."

**2012** reiterated this framework almost verbatim, reinforcing it as an established analytical tool rather than a one-off observation. The report again attributed "the profound level of seasonality" to the same demand-side drivers — Indian wedding season and Diwali — and repeated that "gold tends to perform best in the fourth and the first quarter," with the same 75-80% reliability figure. The consistency across these two years shows the seasonality concept becoming a fixture of the report's technical toolkit, used to contextualize the sideways price action gold was experiencing at the time.

By **2019**, the framing had shifted slightly in emphasis, introducing a specific-cycle nuance: seasonal patterns in pre-election years. Rather than centering on India-driven demand, this chapter emphasized the annual rhythm of weak summers followed by stronger second halves, stating that "the seasonal tailwind is particularly positive in the second half of the year, but beforehand a low price is reached in summer." This represents a refinement of the earlier Q2/Q3-weakness, Q4/Q1-strength model into a more granular within-year narrative pinpointing summer as the trough.

**2024** sharpened this seasonal narrative further by isolating June specifically as the weakest point in the calendar, describing it starkly as "traditionally an extremely weak month for the gold price," with "a dark red seasonal component" awaiting bulls. The report went on to describe the expected mechanics of this weak phase in more technical detail than earlier editions, suggesting "the gold price could then typically enter a sell-off, which should bottom out from mid-July." This marks the most granular articulation of the seasonality argument across the corpus — moving from broad quarterly patterns in 2011-2012, through a summer-low/second-half-strength framing in 2019, to a month-specific bottoming pattern by 2024, while preserving the same underlying thesis that gold's price path follows a recurring, largely demand-driven annual rhythm.
