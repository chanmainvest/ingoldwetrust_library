# Incrementum Inflation Signal

The Incrementum Inflation Signal is a proprietary, market-data-based composite indicator developed by the *In Gold We Trust* authors to gauge whether inflationary or deflationary forces are dominant in the economy at any given time. Built from a combination of market-based factors — most notably the gold-silver ratio and other inflation-sensitive assets such as gold, silver, commodities, and gold miners — it reacts faster than official inflation statistics and serves as a direct guide for fund asset allocation. Over more than a decade of reports, it has evolved from a simple "inflation seismograph" into a central, actively refined tool for anticipating regime shifts between inflation, reflation, disinflation, and deflation.

## How the argument evolved

The signal was introduced in **2014** as an "inflation seismograph," described as a combination of market-based indicators — including the gold-silver ratio — that "result in a proprietary signal," explicitly used so that "our Incrementum Inflation Signal gauges the inflation trend and we position the fund accordingly." That same year, the tool proved its early-warning function by triggering "a 'rising inflation signal' in mid June," marking a shift after "periods of between 6 and 24 months during which disinflationary forces were dominant."

By **2015**, the indicator was formally named the "Incrementum Inflation Signal," defined as depicting "the prevailing trend of price inflation" and already showing "a slightly rising inflationary trend since May." In **2016**, the authors emphasized its market-derived, faster-reacting nature relative to conventional inflation statistics, and reported a milestone: "For the first time in 24 months the Incrementum Inflation Signal indicates that a full-fledged inflation trend is underway."

The **2018** chapter reframed the tool within a broader "inflation vs. deflation" framework, introducing graduated signal strength — "a rising inflation tendency at a signal strength of 50%" — and dating the return of global inflationary tendency to January 2016. In **2020**, amid the Covid-19 shock, the signal's asset basis (gold, silver, commodities, gold miners) was made explicit, and it registered a dramatic weakening: "the inflation signal has weakened dramatically."

The recovery narrative continued in **2022**, when the signal indicated inflation had been rising "since the Covid-19 crisis," while flagging a possible "brief cooling-off phase." By **2023**, the signal reached "its maximum level again since the beginning of April 2023," with the authors crediting it for having "first indicated rising inflation momentum in June 2020" — underscoring its early-warning credentials.

**2024** brought a moment of self-critique: despite being "a central tool in our inflation protection strategies," the signal "failed to correctly capture the continuing disinflationary phase throughout 2023," prompting a revision after which it settled into "a neutral inflation tendency." **2025** noted the model's "significantly increased frequency of signal changes," reflecting choppier conditions, though it "predominantly indicated a positive inflationary environment," with hopes it could again "anticipate an upcoming wave of inflation" as it had in 2020. Most recently, **2026** reported that the signal had "jumped from neutral to offensive," its first offensive reading "in around a year," attributing renewed momentum to broad commodity strength beyond precious metals following the Iran war.
