# Commitment of Traders Positioning

The Commitment of Traders (CoT) report — published by the CFTC and tracking the futures positioning of commercial hedgers ("smart money") versus large and small speculators — is one of the *In Gold We Trust* report's recurring contrarian sentiment tools. Across more than a decade of technical analysis chapters, the authors have used shifts in net long and net short positioning between these two groups to gauge whether gold sentiment is overheated or washed out, treating extremes (and their unwinding) as signals of coming turning points in the gold price.

## How the argument evolved

The framework begins in **2011**, when, despite gold trading near all-time highs, CoT data showed that "neither commercials nor large speculators have taken extreme positions." The absence of euphoric positioning was read as reassuring rather than alarming, and the report concluded this was "a reliable signal for further significant upside potential" — establishing the core contrarian logic: positioning extremes, not price levels alone, are what matter.

By **2012**, the lens sharpened into an explicitly contrarian bullish setup. Commercials were increasing their interest while Large Specs "reduced their net long positions by 60%," a divergence the report called "strongly positive... from a contrarian's point of view" and "an attractive time to invest."

**2013** pushed this dynamic to an extreme associated with market bottoms: speculators had slashed net longs by "more than 90%" while commercial hedgers cut net shorts by 84%, leaving commercials with "the smallest net short position since February, 2005" — a historical marker used to signal a bottoming process.

**2014** reframed the same mechanics around commercial behavior specifically, noting that commercials had "sharply reduced their gross short positions," a contrarian setup described as "a recipe for a pronounced rally." **2015** extended this cross-metal comparison, finding gold's CoT picture "largely positive" as commercials again reduced gross shorts — "an attractive counter-cyclical entry point" — even as silver and platinum/palladium showed contrasting positioning extremes, showing the tool's use for relative-value as well as directional signals.

**2017** offered a more measured, mixed reading: commercial hedgers "still hold an historically quite large net short position," yet "overall, pessimism predominates slightly in the gold market" — CoT data now folded into a broader technical model rather than standing as a standalone bullish trigger.

By **2026**, the analysis had matured into a fuller dashboard combining speculative positioning, open interest, and smart-money behavior. Speculative net longs stood at "around 160,000 contracts" and open interest at 369,530 contracts — both described as "well below" levels typical of euphoric secular highs — while commercials, again cast as "smart money," were shown buying the dip, having "reduced its short positions by 3,835 contracts during the recent correction phase." The throughline across fifteen years is consistent: whenever speculators retreat and commercials accumulate (or reduce shorts), the report reads this divergence as a counter-cyclical, bullish contrarian signal for gold.
