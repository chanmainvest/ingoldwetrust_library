# Gold and Commodities as Inflation Hedge

Gold and commodities are positioned in the In Gold We Trust reports as the asset classes best suited to protect and grow purchasing power when price inflation accelerates. Unlike traditional financial assets such as equities and bonds, which tend to struggle in inflationary environments, gold, energy, and broader commodities are described as "highly inflation-sensitive," making them essential building blocks for prudent portfolio construction. This concept underpins much of the reports' broader case for gold ownership: it is not merely a crisis hedge but a structurally superior asset during periods of rising prices.

## How the argument evolved

**2014** laid the qualitative groundwork for this thesis. The report identified gold, energy, and commodity stocks as "highly inflation-sensitive" assets, arguing that their sensitivity to price inflation gives them "attractive characteristics in terms of prudent portfolio diversification." At this stage, the argument was framed primarily around diversification benefits — the idea that including these assets in a portfolio helps offset the erosion of value that inflation inflicts on conventional holdings, rather than presenting hard performance data.

**2016** built directly on this foundation by introducing empirical support. Drawing on a PPC Metrics study, the report moved from a general diversification argument to a specific, data-driven claim: "when inflation rates accelerate, it is primarily gold and commodities which exhibit a positive performance." This was substantiated with performance figures showing commodities returning 17.30% and gold 11.88% in the relevant comparison, figures set against volatility and other statistical measures (3.92%/7.13%, 8.94%/8.00%, over a 40-period sample). This marked a significant evolution in the argument's sophistication — from an assertion about sensitivity and diversification value to a quantified demonstration that gold and commodities are the primary asset classes capable of delivering positive returns precisely when inflation accelerates, while other asset classes falter.

Taken together, the two chapters trace a natural progression: 2014 establishes the conceptual rationale for why gold and commodities should behave as inflation hedges, while 2016 supplies the empirical validation, reinforcing the thesis with concrete performance data and cementing gold and commodities' role as the standout performers in inflationary regimes.
