# De-Dollarization Trend

The De-Dollarization Trend refers to the gradual, multi-decade shift by nations — most prominently China, Russia, and increasingly Europe — away from reliance on the US dollar for trade settlement, reserve holdings, and commodity pricing. In the *In Gold We Trust* reports, this theme functions as a structural tailwind for gold, since a fraying dollar-centric monetary order implies growing demand for neutral reserve assets. Its significance lies in framing gold not merely as an inflation hedge but as a beneficiary of a slow-motion realignment of the entire global monetary architecture.

## How the argument evolved

**2017** introduced the theme by observing that "the world is looking for alternatives to the dollar – and finds them more and more often," pointing specifically to China and Russia. The report noted a concrete mechanism: "the big oil producers and the largest exporters have stopped accumulating US debt securities," and that as China and Russia develop alternatives for bilateral trade, "they need fewer dollars."

**2018** deepened the analysis while pausing to interrogate the label itself, asking "Is de-dollarization a correct term for what is happening?" and conceding "de-dollarization is of course a loaded word." Even so, it documented the substance of the trend: "China and other countries moving away from the dollar in regard to commodities," widening the lens from trade financing to reserve diversification via the yuan and euro.

**2020** escalated the framing into an "endgame," casting years of gold and oil market developments as one continuous story of "the gradual 'de-dollarization' of the world." Russia, China, and Europe were now described as actively working to "undermine the dominance of the US dollar," with "many countries in the world" turning away from the dollar as a reserve currency altogether — a marked hardening of tone from earlier, more tentative years.

**2021** refined the endpoint of this process, describing it not as dollar collapse but as "the slow but steady move from a system with a single world reserve currency to a multipolar world." Europe's gold buying and China's digital-currency initiatives were treated as "another significant step toward de-dollarization," reinforcing the expectation of a "multiple reserve currency" framework over time.

**2022**, via an interview with Luke Gromen, reframed de-dollarization as a matter of national survival rather than mere preference: moving away from dollar-priced energy was called "a matter of acute and urgent national security for China and for Russia and for others." The chapter also signaled that de-dollarization had become recognized as Gromen's core specialty within the gold-analysis community, with a broader warning that "the whole world is realizing that they are going to have their turn."

**2023** brought the argument to what it called "the final showdown," emphasizing concrete bilateral bypass agreements: "much of the effort to de-dollarize consists of agreements between states to bypass the US dollar in trade for energy and goods." It noted nuance, however — China's "countless" bilateral deals had not yet produced yuan-based oil trade with Saudi Arabia — while explicitly endorsing the trend as "a good thing for the world."

**2024** confirmed the theme's maturation from speculative narrative to observable policy, stating that de-dollarization "is no longer just a theoretical consideration but is increasingly manifesting itself in economic policy decisions, international agreements, and everyday life" — closing the arc from early speculation to acknowledged reality.
