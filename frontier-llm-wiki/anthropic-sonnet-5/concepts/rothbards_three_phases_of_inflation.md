# Rothbard's Three Phases of Inflation

Murray Rothbard's three-phase model provides the *In Gold We Trust* report with a recurring analytical lens for understanding how monetary expansion translates into visible price inflation and, in extremis, currency collapse. The framework traces an "ideal type" progression: from a first phase in which the money supply balloons while prices remain deceptively calm, through a second phase in which the public begins to anticipate and react to rising prices, to a terminal phase — the hyperinflationary "crack-up boom" — in which the currency itself is abandoned. Its significance for the report lies in offering a structural explanation for why gold investors should not be lulled by periods of aggressive money creation that are not yet accompanied by visible CPI increases.

## How the argument evolved

**2015** marks the first appearance of the framework in the report, introduced as a way to explain how hyperinflation develops "through three phases from stable prices to a currency-abandoning crack-up boom." The report describes the sequence in Rothbard's own logic: "In the first phase, the money supply is increased, but prices barely rise," lulling the public into complacency, before "the public slowly but surely begins to anticipate rising prices" in the second phase, setting the stage for accelerating monetary demand and, eventually, disorder.

**2018** revisits the model in the context of the broader "Inflation vs. Deflation" debate, reaffirming it almost verbatim while sharpening its terminology. The report again notes that "Rothbard identified three 'ideal type' phases of the inflation process," with the first phase characterized by a money supply that "is stepped up drastically, but prices hardly rise." This iteration explicitly names the terminal stage — "usually referred to as the 'crack-up boom'" — giving the framework a clearer vocabulary for describing the point at which the public loses all confidence in the currency and rushes to convert cash into real goods.

**2020**, amid the unprecedented monetary expansion following the pandemic shock, the report leans on the same Rothbardian structure to frame its "Status Quo of Inflation Dynamics" discussion. The phrasing is nearly identical to prior years — "In the first phase the money supply is increased, but prices hardly rise at all" — but the emphasis shifts toward locating where the current cycle stood within the sequence. The report again labels the endpoint "Phase 3... commonly referred to as the 'crack-up boom' or 'hyperinflation,'" using the model less as a novel introduction and more as an established diagnostic tool for interpreting the scale of central bank balance sheet growth against the still-muted price data of the time.

Across the three chapters, the treatment of the concept remains remarkably consistent in substance — the same three phases, the same defining quotes on the money supply and the crack-up boom — but its narrative role evolves from an explanatory introduction (2015), to a conceptual anchor within a formal inflation-versus-deflation debate (2018), to a diagnostic checkpoint for assessing real-time monetary conditions (2020).
