# De-Dollarization Movement

The De-Dollarization Movement refers to the coordinated and growing efforts of countries — chiefly China, Russia, the rest of the BRICS, Saudi Arabia, and African nations — to reduce their dependence on the US dollar in trade, reserves, and cross-border settlement. In the In Gold We Trust reports, this movement is framed as a structural shift in the global geopolitical and monetary order, one with direct implications for gold as an alternative reserve asset and beneficiary of waning confidence in dollar hegemony.

## How the argument evolved

**2023** marked the moment de-dollarization moved from theory to headline narrative in the reports. The chapter explicitly named the phenomenon — "we call it de-dollarization – the resistance of countries like China, Russia, the rest of the BRICS" — framing it as organized pushback against dollar dominance rather than incidental drift. Crucially, the report emphasized a change in pace and posture: "In 2023, we have moved from rather passive preparations to active reorientation of the East," signaling that what had been quiet, defensive positioning by Eastern powers had turned into deliberate, proactive strategy. The report also noted that "the ongoing changes in the global geopolitical fabric truly struck the mainstream," indicating that de-dollarization had graduated from a fringe or niche observation into a topic commanding broad attention — a "final showdown" framing that cast the dollar's global role as under genuine contest.

**2024** built directly on this foundation by cataloguing concrete, country-by-country evidence that the active reorientation described a year earlier was materializing into tangible policy. China's strategy was tied specifically to its Belt and Road Initiative, described as an effort "to internationalize the renminbi and reduce dependence on the dollar" — moving de-dollarization from rhetoric to infrastructure and currency policy. Russia's progress was portrayed as the furthest advanced, having "almost eliminated dollar use in bilateral trade with China," a striking demonstration that de-dollarization is not merely aspirational but operationally achievable between major economies. The report widened the geographic scope further by pointing to Africa, where "a Pan-African Payment System being built in Kenya" showed that de-dollarization efforts were no longer confined to the original BRICS core but spreading to a broader coalition of nations seeking alternatives to dollar-based settlement. Together, the 2023 and 2024 chapters trace an arc from identifying and naming a resistance movement to documenting its institutional and infrastructural embodiment across multiple continents — reinforcing the reports' broader thesis of an "Eastern push for gold" as both cause and consequence of this realignment.
