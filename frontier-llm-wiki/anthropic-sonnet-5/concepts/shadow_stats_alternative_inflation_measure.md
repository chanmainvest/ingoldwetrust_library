# Shadow Stats Alternative Inflation Measure

The Shadow Stats Alternative Inflation Measure refers to an alternative CPI calculation methodology, based on the government's pre-1980 approach, that critics argue captures the "true" erosion of purchasing power far more accurately than the official Consumer Price Index. Within the In Gold We Trust reports, this measure is used as a key analytical tool to challenge the conventional inflation-adjusted gold price targets, arguing that official statistics understate inflation and therefore understate what gold's real, inflation-adjusted high should be. Its significance lies in providing a much higher benchmark for gold's fair value, reinforcing the broader thesis that gold remains deeply undervalued relative to its historical purchasing-power peak.

## How the argument evolved

**2010** marks the introduction of the Shadow Stats measure into the report's analytical framework, first as a critique of the conceptual definition of inflation itself. The report notes that official CPI figures fail to capture the real decline in currency value since 1980, and cites Shadow Stats' finding that "gold would have to rise to USD 7,494 in order to reflect the real value of 1980." This figure is presented as a corrective to the mainstream inflation-adjusted gold price target, suggesting that conventional CPI-based calculations dramatically understate what gold's true real high should be.

This theme is developed further within the same **2010** report in the discussion of why an inflation-adjusted high of USD 2,300 is used as the target price. Here the authors acknowledge that if one instead applies the older, pre-1980 Shadow Stats methodology, the implied target rises sharply to nearly USD 7,500 — restating that "gold would have to rise to USD 7,494 in order to exceed the 1980 high in real terms." The report pushes the argument even further by invoking an alternative measure, MZM money supply growth since 1980, which implies an even more extreme target, noting that "gold would have to soar to almost USD 10,000." Taken together, these two chapters use the Shadow Stats framework not to replace the report's official USD 2,300 target, but to illustrate the wide range of plausible inflation-adjusted valuations depending on methodology — with Shadow Stats and MZM serving as the upper bookends of that range, underscoring the conservatism of the report's own baseline target while keeping the door open to a much larger revaluation of gold under stricter, alternative inflation accounting.
