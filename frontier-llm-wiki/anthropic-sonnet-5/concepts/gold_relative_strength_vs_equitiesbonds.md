# Gold Relative Strength vs Equities/Bonds

Gold Relative Strength vs Equities/Bonds refers to the tracking of gold's price performance relative to major traditional asset classes — stocks and bonds (particularly Treasuries) — using ratio charts as a technical and macro indicator. A sustained downward trend in the equities/gold or bonds/gold ratio (or, equivalently, an uptrend in gold's relative strength) signals that gold is outperforming these mainstream assets, reinforcing the case for gold as a strategic holding within a broader portfolio. This concept matters because it frames gold not merely as a standalone commodity but as an asset whose attractiveness is best judged in comparison to competing stores of value.

## How the argument evolved

**2011** introduced this relative-strength framework in the context of technical analysis of the gold price. The report identified a long-term structural shift: gold's relative strength versus equities and bonds had been "bottoming out between 1998 and 2005" before entering a sustained upward trend. Crucially, the report emphasized that this uptrend had "not shown any signs of overheating," suggesting the move was durable and not yet extended — an important technical caveat that positioned gold's outperformance as a multi-year structural theme rather than a short-term spike.

**2012** carried this thesis forward into the report's introductory framing, generalizing it from a purely technical observation into a broader macro narrative. Here the relative strength of gold against both equities and Treasuries was measured through ratio charts, and the report noted that these ratios were "still intact" in their downward trajectory. The description became more precise and chart-driven: "Both ratios have been setting lower lows and lower highs and are thus locked in a downward trend," a classic technical-analysis characterization of a persistent trend. By moving this observation into the introduction, the 2012 report signaled that gold's relative outperformance against both major asset classes had graduated from a technical footnote into a headline argument supporting the overall bullish gold thesis for the year.

Taken together, the two chapters trace a natural progression: the 2011 report established the historical origin and technical validity of the trend (dating its inception to 1998–2005 and confirming it was not overextended), while the 2012 report reaffirmed and reinforced the trend's persistence, using it as an opening pillar of the year's broader case for gold. The consistent use of ratio-based falling trend lines — first as a standalone technical observation, later as evidence marshaled at the very start of the report — illustrates how this concept evolved from a chart pattern into a recurring strategic argument for gold's relative value proposition versus stocks and bonds.
