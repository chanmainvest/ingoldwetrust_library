# Bretton Woods III / New Monetary Order

Bretton Woods III describes a proposed epochal shift in the global monetary architecture: away from a dollar- and Treasury-centered system reliant on "inside money," and toward a system anchored by "outside money" — gold bullion and other hard commodities — increasingly championed by Eastern economies. The concept, drawn from Zoltan Pozsar's framework, matters because it reframes gold not as a peripheral hedge but as the potential foundation of a successor world monetary order, one born out of strains in the Eurodollar system and resurgent Western inflation.

## How the argument evolved

**2023** introduced the Bretton Woods III framework directly, presenting it as a live, unfolding phenomenon rather than a theoretical possibility: "We are witnessing the birth of Bretton Woods III – a new world (monetary) order centered around commodity-based currencies in the East." The report laid out a three-stage historical arc of monetary evolution — from the original Bretton Woods system "backed by gold bullion," through Bretton Woods II "backed by inside money" (the dollar-Treasury complex that dominated the postwar and post-1971 eras), to the emerging Bretton Woods III "backed by outside money (gold bullion and other commodities)." Crucially, this shift was tied to a specific mechanism: a commodity-backed monetary realignment in the East was framed as a force that weakens the Eurodollar system while simultaneously fueling inflationary pressure in the West. This gave the concept both a geopolitical dimension (East versus West) and a macroeconomic one (currency-system stress translating into price instability).

**2024** carried the thesis forward largely intact, restating the same historical progression — gold-backed Bretton Woods, inside-money Bretton Woods II, and outside-money Bretton Woods III — but sharpened the tone from observation to inevitability. Where the 2023 language spoke of "witnessing the birth" of a new order, the 2024 treatment declared that "we are irrevocably on the road to a new global (monetary) order," signaling a hardening of conviction that the transition is not merely underway but unstoppable. The continued attribution to Pozsar's thesis anchors the argument in a specific, named analytical lineage, lending it intellectual continuity across the two editions rather than treating it as a one-off narrative device. Together, the two years form a single continuous argument: 2023 establishes the diagnosis and the causal story (Eastern commodity-backing undermining Eurodollar dominance and stoking inflation), while 2024 reaffirms and intensifies the conclusion, treating the move toward a gold- and commodity-anchored order as a settled trajectory rather than an open question.
