# Remonetisation of Gold

Remonetisation of gold refers to the thesis that gold is progressively reclaiming its historical role as monetary asset — not merely a commodity or a crisis hedge, but a recognized store of value and yardstick for currency quality. As confidence in paper money erodes, the In Gold We Trust reports argue that gold's monetary function is being restored both in market behavior and in political-institutional attitudes, marking this as a structural signal of where the broader gold bull market stands.

## How the argument evolved

**2010** introduces remonetisation as a foundational thesis rather than a speculative aside. The report frames gold's rise in the context of eroding trust in fiat currencies, asserting that "the re[monetisation] — Gold is an excellent measure of the quality of paper money." Crucially, this edition marks the moment the authors declare the process underway in real time, stating "we believe that the remonetisation of gold has now finally begun." At this stage, remonetisation is presented as a beginning — a turning point rather than a mature trend — tied directly to gold's function as a barometer of monetary debasement.

**2012** builds on this foundation by reframing remonetisation as evidence of market maturation rather than mere onset. The report now treats remonetisation as a marker of cyclical progression, arguing that "remonetisation indicates that bull market has entered a new phase." This shift is significant: rather than simply announcing that gold is regaining monetary status, the 2012 text uses remonetisation as a diagnostic tool to locate the bull market's stage of development. Equally important is the introduction of a political and cultural dimension absent from the 2010 framing — gold is described as becoming "politically correct" again, suggesting that remonetisation is not only a market phenomenon but also a shift in institutional and public perception. The report characterizes this as part of a "gradual reversal of that former attitude, with gold coming back into fashion," implying a slow, multi-year rehabilitation of gold's reputation among policymakers and mainstream commentators who had previously dismissed its monetary relevance.

Taken together, the two chapters trace an arc: 2010 establishes remonetisation as a fresh, almost prophetic claim rooted in monetary quality concerns, while 2012 treats it as a confirmed and advancing process with political legitimacy attached. The concept evolves from a diagnostic statement about currency debasement into a broader narrative about gold's rehabilitation within both markets and institutions — a shift from "has begun" to "has entered a new phase."
