# Gold Stock-to-Flow Ratio

The gold stock-to-flow ratio compares the enormous existing above-ground stock of gold to its comparatively small annual production (the "flow"), producing a ratio measured in decades rather than the months typical of ordinary commodities. This extreme abundance-relative-to-production is treated across the In Gold We Trust reports as the foundational reason gold behaves differently from other commodities and monetary assets — shaping its pricing behavior, its resistance to market squeezes, and its credentials as a scarce, low-inflation store of value.

## How the argument evolved

**2013** introduced the concept in a narrowly technical context, using it to explain an anomaly in futures pricing rather than as a standalone theme. The report argued that gold's very high stock-to-flow ratio compared to other commodities like wheat means the metal "should never trade in backwardation," since the vast existing stockpile relative to yearly output leaves no realistic scenario of physical scarcity forcing spot prices above futures. Here the ratio functioned as a diagnostic tool for sentiment and market-structure analysis, helping explain why anomalies in the gold market signaled unusual conditions.

By **2022**, the concept had matured into a core pillar of the report's supply-and-demand framework. The chapter on gold's fundamentals elevated stock-to-flow from a technical footnote to a defining characteristic of the metal, stating that gold "has a greater stock to flow than almost any other commodity," with a ratio "measured in decades (compared to months for ordinary commodities)." This abundance relative to any single year's mining output was used to explain why gold is uniquely resistant to manipulation or squeeze dynamics: the "massive amount of it relative to any one market participant" means no single buyer or seller can meaningfully distort the market the way they might with a commodity whose stock is small relative to annual flow. The ratio thus became central to understanding gold's supply-side fundamentals as distinct from industrial commodities.

**2025** carried the concept into the monetary and macroeconomic domain, explicitly linking stock-to-flow to gold's role as a hedge against currency debasement and inflation. In the chapter on central bank gold repatriation, the report defined the "total stock-to-flow ratio (SFR) for gold" as the comparison of "the current stock of a commodity against the flow of new production," and drew a direct line from a high ratio to monetary scarcity: "A high ratio, typical for gold or Bitcoin, indicates scarcity and signals lower inflationary pressure." This framing situates gold alongside Bitcoin as an asset class defined by structural scarcity, reinforcing the report's broader narrative about central banks repatriating and revaluing gold as a hedge against fiat currency depreciation. Over the twelve years spanned by these notes, the stock-to-flow ratio evolved from a niche explanation of futures-market behavior into a foundational monetary concept underpinning gold's case as a scarce store of value.
