# Gold Mining Shares as Leading Indicator

Within the In Gold We Trust report's technical analysis toolkit, gold mining equities are treated not merely as a leveraged play on bullion but as a forward-looking signal for the metal's own price direction. The core thesis is that the relative strength or weakness of mining shares versus gold often anticipates turning points in the gold price before they become visible on gold's own chart, making the sector a valuable confirmation tool for assessing the durability of gold price trends.

## How the argument evolved

**2017** introduced the concept through the technical analysis chapter's use of the GDX gold mining ETF as a proxy for sector sentiment and trend. The report explains that "a trend following signal based on the price chart of the gold mining ETF GDX as well as the associated sentiment is used for this purpose," establishing GDX-based analysis as a formal component of the technical toolkit rather than a mere aside. Crucially, the report states plainly that "gold stocks often tend to lead the gold price," framing miners as a barometer worth watching in their own right. At the time, however, the signal was flashing caution: mining shares were "currently underperforming" gold, a divergence noted without extensive elaboration but planting the seed of the leading-indicator framework for subsequent editions.

**2018** built directly on this foundation, restating the principle more assertively — "the mining shares are often leading indicators of the gold price" — and, importantly, operationalizing it as an analytical test applied to real-time conditions. Rather than simply observing the relationship, the chapter used it diagnostically: with mining shares "outperforming the gold price by a slight degree," the report inferred that this "possibly indicates the fact that the ongoing gold sell-off is not sustainable." This marks a meaningful evolution from 2017's observational tone to a more applied, interpretive use of the indicator — relative performance of miners versus bullion becomes a lens through which the sustainability of a prevailing gold price trend (in this case, a downtrend) can be judged. Where 2017 registered underperformance as a note of caution, 2018 flipped the signal, treating relative outperformance as evidence against the persistence of a bearish move, thereby completing the indicator's function as a two-way gauge of trend health rather than a one-directional warning sign.

Across the two years, the throughline is consistent: gold mining shares, whether tracked through GDX or through direct comparative performance against the gold price, are positioned as an early-warning and confirmation mechanism. The report's technical analysis chapters treat divergences between miners and bullion — whether the miners lag or lead — as meaningful information about where the gold price itself may be headed next, cementing this relative-performance analysis as a recurring fixture of the annual technical outlook.
