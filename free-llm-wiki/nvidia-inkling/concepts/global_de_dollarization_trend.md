# Global De-Dollarization Trend

The global de-dollarization trend captures the accelerating retreat from US dollar dependence across international trade, central-bank reserves, and energy settlement—a shift the In Gold We Trust reports treat as a structural rupture in post-war monetary order. Once assumed permanent, the greenback’s reserve-currency dominance is now described as coming to an end, with countries actively turning away from dollar usage and threatening the foundation of American financial hegemony. As this erosion unfolds, it elevates gold and alternative settlement mechanisms back into strategic focus, making de-dollarization not merely a currency story but a defining macroeconomic realignment.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** — The report frames the shift as an endgame for American monetary supremacy, declaring that "The US Dollar Storyline is coming to an end." The authors observe that "Many countries in the world began turning away from using the dollar," documenting reduced reliance not only in trade invoicing but also in official reserves and energy transactions. They caution that the "dollar’s reserve currency status will continue to give ground," portraying the decline as sustained and multidimensional rather than abrupt. By labeling the chapter "The Endgame Has Begun," the 2020 analysis establishes de-dollarization as a central, ongoing threat to the global reserve architecture.

**2021** — The narrative moves from structural diagnosis to active strategic confrontation, identifying specific actors and instruments driving the retreat. The report notes that "it is a stated goal of China to use the e-yuan to slowly undermine the dominance of the US dollar," linking digital-currency innovation directly to settlement rivalry. Beijing aims to "challenge the dominance of the US dollar in international trade settlement," opening what the authors call a digital front in the currency wars. The geographic scope widens dramatically: "Fifty years after the Nixon shock, even Saudi Arabia is openly turning toward China," tying energy markets—historically the bedrock of dollar demand—into the de-dollarization arc. Meanwhile, Europe’s gold accumulation, signaled in the chapter title, reinforces that reserve managers are diversifying away from fiat exposure as the dollar’s monopoly frays.

Read chronologically, the reports construct a clear progression: **2020** establishes that the dollar’s storyline is ending and that "Many countries in the world began turning away from using the dollar" across trade, reserves, and energy; **2021** then shows how that endgame is being executed—through China’s e-yuan, Saudi Arabia’s geopolitical pivot, and Europe’s renewed gold buying—transforming a gradual erosion into an open, multi-front challenge to US reserve-currency dominance.
