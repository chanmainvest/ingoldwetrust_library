# Technical Analysis Independence

Technical Analysis Independence is the principle that price action and market structure can be evaluated on their own objective terms, separate from the macroeconomic or monetary narratives that typically drive fundamental analysis. Within the *In Gold We Trust* corpus, this idea serves as a methodological safeguard: it insists that an analyst must be able to "focus exclusively on technical conditions in terms of price and market structure" without allowing prior convictions about inflation, policy, or supply-demand dynamics to color the reading of the chart. The significance of this separation lies in its potential to overturn consensus expectations—when technical and fundamental signals conflict, the technical picture retains its own evidentiary authority, sometimes producing conclusions that run counter to the prevailing fundamental story.

## How the argument evolved

**2016** establishes the foundational separation between technical and fundamental reasoning. The report assesses technical conditions in price and market structure objectively, stressing that this evaluation must remain "independent of one's view of the fundamentals." At this stage, the argument is primarily definitional: technical analysis is presented as a distinct lens that can "focus exclusively on technical conditions in terms of price and market structure," yielding an "objective assessment of those factors independent of one's view of the fundamentals." The chapter introduces the possibility of divergence cautiously, observing that "Sometimes technical analysis can lead an analyst to completely different conclusions." Here, independence is framed as a capacity—the analyst is able to step outside fundamental assumptions and let price structure speak for itself, even if that voice contradicts the broader economic narrative.

By **2017**, the concept has hardened into a stricter disciplinary rule. The report again directs the analyst to "focus exclusively on technical conditions and market structure," but now pairs this focus with an explicit mandate to "analyze the situation objectively and independent of one's fundamental opinions." The shift in language is subtle but important: where the earlier chapter spoke of assessment independent of one's view, the later chapter demands analysis independent of one's opinions, tightening the ethical and methodological boundary between the two domains. The consequence of this stricter independence is also amplified. Rather than merely reaching different conclusions, the analyst may now "arrive at an entirely different conclusion based on his analysis of the technical picture." The technical image is no longer just an alternative input; it becomes a self-sufficient basis for judgment capable of overriding fundamental expectations entirely. Taken together, the two years trace an arc from methodological openness—allowing technical factors to be heard separately—to analytical sovereignty, in which the technical picture commands its own independent verdict.
