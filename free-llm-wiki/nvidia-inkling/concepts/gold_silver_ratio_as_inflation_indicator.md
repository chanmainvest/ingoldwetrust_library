# Gold-Silver Ratio as Inflation Indicator

The gold-silver ratio operates as a macroeconomic gauge that captures the tension between inflationary and deflationary forces through the relative pricing of the two metals. Within the *In Gold We Trust* reports, it is characterized as an "excellent indicator of the interaction between inflation and deflation," serving both as an early warning system and as a correlation tool for market-based inflation expectations. Its analytical value stems from translating spread dynamics into monetary-condition signals, allowing investors to distinguish between disinflationary warnings and bullish gold-market confirmations.

## How the argument evolved

**2015** — The report's 2015 treatment in "Gold And Inflation" establishes the conceptual foundation by presenting the gold-silver ratio as a structural barometer of inflation-deflation interaction. Here, the spread is interpreted defensively: "A rising G/S ratio is thus a warning signal for gold investors," indicating that widening valuations between gold and silver reflect disinflationary pressure rather than strength in the monetary metals complex. The analysis also embeds a long-horizon perspective, observing that "reversion to the mean has to take place over the long term," which frames the ratio as a cyclical, slow-moving indicator rather than a reactive trading metric.

**2020** — By 2020, the discussion in "Status Quo Of Inflation Dynamics" transforms this defensive framing into an active predictive framework linked to derivative markets. The chapter investigates "how inflation swaps are correlated with the Gold/Silver ratio," explicitly connecting precious-metal spreads to institutional inflation pricing. Directional interpretation inverts the earlier warning logic: "A falling G/S ratio significantly increases the probability of a bull market in gold," so that contraction in the spread becomes a bullish confirmation rather than a deflationary alarm. The report reinforces this shift with concrete price action, noting that a "reversal from 125 to 100 took place, which confirms our bullish outlook," using the observed compression to validate an optimistic stance on gold amid changing inflation dynamics.

Together, the chapters trace an evolution from theoretical macro indicator to empirically anchored trading signal: the 2015 framework defines the ratio's defensive meaning and long-cycle mean-reversion properties, while the 2020 update integrates inflation-swap correlations, assigns predictive power to falling spreads, and cites a specific reversal to corroborate a bullish outlook.
