# Gold and Real Yield Correlation

The report treats the correlation between gold prices and real yields on inflation-linked bonds as a core macro-financial linkage, using Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) and break-even rates to decode how gold responds to shifts in inflation expectations. Rather than viewing gold in isolation, the analysis frames the metal’s price action as tightly coupled to the real return dynamics embedded in government debt, making the relationship a practical gauge for monetary conditions. Over successive editions, this view evolves from an empirical observation into a durable diagnostic that tracks gold through both deeply negative and rising real-yield regimes. Its significance lies in offering investors a structured lens—grounded in observable bond-market data—through which to interpret gold’s behavior as inflation dynamics change.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** established the empirical bedrock. The chapter asserted that "Yields on inflation-linked bonds show an extremely high correlation with the gold price," presenting the linkage not as theoretical but as visibly present in market data. To operationalize the comparison, the report directed attention to the real yield of 5-year inflation-linked US government bonds (TIPS), framing the exercise precisely: "If we compare the gold price with the real yield of 5-year inflation-linked US government bonds (TIPS)" the linkage becomes evident. At that stage of the cycle, the analysis highlighted a critical inflection point: "Since the low in TIPS of 0.14% on 19 March, inflation concerns have risen slightly again." This observation tied gold’s trajectory to a rebound from extreme lows in real yields, suggesting that even modest recoveries in TIPS yields could coincide with renewed inflation anxiety and corresponding price action in gold.

By **2022**, the framework had matured into a persistent structural feature rather than a temporary coincidence. The report reaffirmed that "Typically, there is a correlation between the gold price and inflation-linked bond yields," signaling that the relationship held across changing macro conditions. What had changed was the magnitude of inflation expectations: the report observed that the break-even measure "has rallied to almost 3% – its highest level in two decades," reflecting anticipated inflation at a twenty-year high. Concurrently, "The expected real yield rose recently and is now even slightly positive," marking a departure from the deeply negative real-rate environment that had previously prevailed. Together, these updates demonstrated that the gold–real-yield correlation remained intact even as the yield curve transitioned from historic lows toward positive territory, confirming the framework’s utility across both falling and rising real-yield regimes.
