# Gold and Real Yield Correlation

The correlation between gold and real yields on inflation-linked government bonds—chiefly U.S. Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)—provides a direct lens on how the metal tracks inflation expectations and the real return on sovereign debt. By following break-even rates and the embedded real yield of inflation-linked paper, the report reads gold’s price action as a referendum on purchasing-power erosion and monetary conditions. This linkage anchors bullion’s valuation to the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold against government debt that adjusts for inflation, making the relationship a durable pillar of macro analysis.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** established the framework with striking clarity. The chapter noted that "Yields on inflation-linked bonds show an extremely high correlation with the gold price," directing the comparison specifically toward the real yield of 5-year U.S. government bonds (TIPS). "If we compare the gold price with the real yield of 5-year inflation-linked US government bonds (TIPS)," the report revealed an extreme compression in real returns, with yields falling to a low of 0.14% on 19 March. From that trough, the narrative observed that "Since the low in TIPS of 0.14% on 19 March, inflation concerns have risen slightly again," suggesting that as real yields collapsed, gold’s sensitivity to inflation expectations intensified. In this environment, gold functioned less like a static commodity and more like a mirror of deeply negative or compressed real-rate conditions.

By **2022**, the correlation remained intact, but the macroeconomic backdrop had inverted in key respects. The report reaffirmed that "Typically, there is a correlation between the gold price and inflation-linked bond yields," yet the data now reflected surging inflation expectations rather than collapsing real returns. U.S. break-even rates "has rallied to almost 3% – its highest level in two decades," signaling that markets were pricing sustained price pressures unseen in a generation. Concurrently, "The expected real yield rose recently and is now even slightly positive," a marked departure from the near-zero or negative territory documented two years prior. The evolution is therefore chronological and thematic: in 2020, gold tracked real yields downward into extreme compression, amplifying inflation concerns as TIPS bottomed; in 2022, gold continued to move in relation to these same inflation-linked instruments, but now against spiking break-even rates and a return to positive real yields. Across both periods, the report treats the gold–real-yield nexus as an adaptive analytical constant—one that holds whether inflation dynamics manifest through crushed real returns or through surging nominal break-evens.
