# Central Bank Gold Accumulation

Central bank gold accumulation denotes the sustained, large-scale purchase of physical gold by monetary authorities to diversify foreign-exchange reserves and diminish reliance on fiat currencies—above all the US dollar. Across the In Gold We Trust reports, this behavior evolves from a notable post-crisis rebound into a structural, record-breaking pillar of reserve strategy led increasingly by Eastern and non-Western economies. The reports treat gold not as a speculative commodity but as a neutral strategic asset, with accumulation serving as both a hedge and an explicit instrument of de-dollarization.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** opens the narrative with a historic benchmark: central banks "bought more gold within one year than they have in any year since 1971," acquiring "In 2018 alone they bought 651 tonnes of gold." The report frames this surge as a deliberate geopolitical pivot, declaring that "The answer is de-dollarization," and presents gold as the neutral reserve asset through which authorities can reduce dollar exposure without fully committing to rival currencies.

**2020** reinforces the magnitude of the 2018 breakout by noting that "central banks bought more gold in 2018 than at any other time since the gold standard ended in 1971." The strategic logic is broadened beyond a single-year spike: authorities are "switching from dollars to other currencies, and from currencies collectively to gold," suggesting that accumulation is embedded in a wider restructuring of reserve portfolios rather than a temporary tactical trade.

**2021** demonstrates that the trend survived global disruption. "Central banks remained net buyers of gold in 2020," with diversification efforts visible in Russia, Poland, and euro-area states. Concrete portfolio shifts appear in the data: one authority "now holds around 30% of its reserves in euros – and 22% each in gold and US dollars." The motivation is quantified in sentiment as well: "36% of central bankers openly stated that de-dollarization is of at least “marginal” relevance when buying gold," confirming that gold purchases are policy-level responses to currency risk rather than passive market behavior.

**2023** escalates both scale and geography. The report establishes that "Central banks as a group have been net buyers of gold every year since 2009," but emphasizes an unprecedented acceleration: "In 2022, central banks bought the most gold on record, 1,136 tonnes." The geographic divide sharpens with the observation that "East is stacking gold like there is no tomorrow," signaling that Asian and non-Western nations are now the dominant structural buyers.

**2024** shows the momentum extending into new record territory. "2023 was another year of blistering central bank buying," and "Central bank gold demand set a new record for a first quarter" in Q1/2024. The East remains the engine: "Central bank demand was supported from the global East." By this point, accumulation is no longer an emerging de-dollarization tactic but a sustained, quantifiable feature of global monetary architecture, with central banks treating gold as an indispensable reserve anchor amid persistent inflationary and geopolitical uncertainty.
