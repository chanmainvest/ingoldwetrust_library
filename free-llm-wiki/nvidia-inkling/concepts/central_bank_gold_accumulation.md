# Central Bank Gold Accumulation

Central bank gold accumulation describes the sustained, large-scale acquisition of bullion by monetary authorities to reduce dependence on fiat reserve currencies—above all the US dollar—and to secure neutral, sovereign wealth. Across the *In Gold We Trust* reports, this behavior evolves from a post-1971 record-breaking event into a structural, East-led pillar of global de-dollarization, with authorities explicitly treating gold as both a strategic reserve and an inflation hedge. The narrative traces how official buying shifted from cyclical diversification to an open, geographically polarized endgame in which Eastern central banks drive record volumes year after year.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** opened the narrative with a stark historical comparison: central banks “bought more gold within one year than they have in any year since 1971”, with the report noting that “In 2018 alone they bought 651 tonnes of gold”. At this stage the accumulation was already framed as strategic rather than tactical, because “The answer is de-dollarization”—gold was being embraced as a neutral reserve asset while European authorities began joining the movement away from dollar-centric holdings.

By **2020**, the report had elevated the trend to an “endgame” thesis. It repeated that “central banks bought more gold in 2018 than at any other time since the gold standard ended in 1971”, but added a structural interpretation: monetary authorities were “switching from dollars to other currencies, and from currencies collectively to gold”. The message was that accumulation had become a deliberate, multilateral exit strategy from dollar dominance, not a temporary hedge.

In **2021**, the argument gained institutional specificity. “Central banks remained net buyers of gold in 2020”, with Russia, Poland, and euro-area states reshaping reserve books—one profile now holding “around 30% of its reserves in euros – and 22% each in gold and US dollars”. The report also captured the explicit motivation behind the buying: “36% of central bankers openly stated that de-dollarization is of at least “marginal” relevance when buying gold”, confirming that reserve diversification was openly acknowledged as a driver of gold demand.

The **2023** chapter transformed the narrative from steady buying into a record-breaking, geographically lopsided surge. “Central banks as a group have been net buyers of gold every year since 2009”, yet “In 2022, central banks bought the most gold on record, 1,136 tonnes”. The report underscored the Eastern leadership of this wave with the vivid observation that “East is stacking gold like there is no tomorrow”, framing Asian and non-Western accumulation as the dominant structural force.

Finally, **2024** tied the accumulation directly to the inflationary present. “2023 was another year of blistering central bank buying”, and “Central bank gold demand set a new record for a first quarter” in early 2024. The report emphasized that “Central bank demand was supported from the global East”, linking unprecedented official-sector purchasing to the “Status Quo Of Inflation” and reinforcing the view that gold remains the preferred reserve anchor when monetary stability is in question.
