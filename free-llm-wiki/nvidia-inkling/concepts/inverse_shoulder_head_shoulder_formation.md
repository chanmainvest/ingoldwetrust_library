# Inverse Shoulder-Head-Shoulder Formation

The inverse shoulder-head-shoulder formation is a bullish reversal chart pattern in which gold carves a central trough— the “head”— flanked by two shallower troughs— the “shoulders”— beneath a shared neckline resistance. Within the In Gold We Trust reports, this structure serves as a multi-year technical framework that frames gold’s consolidation and projects a decisive upward resolution. Its significance rests on the prolonged failure to breach the neckline near USD 1,360 to 1,400 and the corresponding breakout target near USD 1,800 that analysts assign to a confirmed penetration.

## How the argument evolved

**2018** introduces the pattern as an already visible, large-scale structure. The report notes the "establishment of an impressive inverse shoulder-head-shoulder formation" in gold, yet emphasizes that the market has not cleared its ceiling: "gold has failed to break through the neckline a few times in the resistance region of USD 1,360 to 1,400". Even with these repeated rejections, the technical outlook remains constructive, because "If the gold price were to break through this resistance zone, the next target would be USD 1,800". In this first treatment, the formation is presented as a mature setup— impressive in scale, clearly bounded, and carrying an ambitious price objective—while the neckline acts as a stubborn gate that has not yet yielded.

**2019** extends the narrative backward in time and forward in implication. Rather than treating the pattern as a recent development, the report observes that "an impressive inverse shoulder-head-shoulder formation has been forming since 2013", underscoring a half-decade of consolidation compressed into the same resistance band. The price action has not improved its record at the boundary; indeed, "the price has already failed several times on the neck line in the resistance range of USD 1,360-1,400". What changes is the sense of accumulated potential: the setup "could explosively resolve upwards". By tracing the formation from 2013 through repeated failures at the USD 1,360–1,400 neckline, the 2019 analysis transforms the pattern from a static observation into a coiled technical spring, with the USD 1,800 target now representing the explosive release of years of compressed price action.

Read together, the two years construct a continuous technical story. The 2018 chapter establishes the impressive formation, defines the resistance region, and sets the USD 1,800 target; the 2019 chapter deepens the historical context, confirms the persistence of neckline failures, and heightens the expectation of an explosive upward resolution.
