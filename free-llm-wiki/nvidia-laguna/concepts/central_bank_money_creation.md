# Central Bank Money Creation

Central bank money creation refers to the process by which central banks generate new currency through mechanisms like quantitative easing, significantly expanding the monetary supply. This practice has become a cornerstone of modern monetary policy, particularly evident during periods of economic crisis. The concept's significance lies in its profound impact on global financial markets, influencing everything from interest rates to asset prices, and raising critical questions about the nature of money itself.

## How the argument evolved

The discourse around central bank money creation has intensified over recent years, with investment reports highlighting its unprecedented scale. **2015** marked a pivotal moment, as the report emphasized that "On a global level, central banks will create more irredeemable money this year than ever before." This statement underscored a fundamental shift in monetary policy, where central banks were increasingly resorting to creating money without the traditional backing of gold or other commodities. The term "irredeemable money" captured the essence of this transformation—currency being printed not to finance specific projects, but to pump liquidity into the financial system.

**2017** saw this trend not only continue but accelerate, with the report detailing how "largest central banks created the equivalent of almost USD 1,000 bn" through quantitative easing. This massive injection of funds was explicitly described as being used to "purchase financial securities," highlighting how central banks were directly shaping financial markets by buying assets rather than lending to governments or banks. The distinction was crucial: the fresh currency was not "used to fund philanthropic projects but to purchase financial securities," a practice that fundamentally altered the relationship between money creation and economic activity. This evolution from general money printing to targeted asset purchases represented a more sophisticated, yet equally controversial, approach to monetary intervention.
