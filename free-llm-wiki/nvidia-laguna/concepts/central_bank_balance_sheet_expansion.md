# Central Bank Balance Sheet Expansion

Central bank balance sheet expansion refers to the process by which monetary authorities increase the size of their assets through quantitative easing and other unconventional policies. This practice has become a defining feature of modern monetary policy, particularly following the 2008 financial crisis, as central banks sought to stimulate economies and prevent deflation. The expansion of central bank balance sheets represents a significant shift in monetary policy tools and has profound implications for financial markets, currency values, and the broader economy.

## How the argument evolved

The concept of central bank balance sheet expansion has been a recurring theme in the In Gold We Trust reports, with each edition providing updated analysis of the growing scale of monetary intervention. **2016** marked a pivotal point in the narrative, as the report highlighted how central banks had dramatically expanded their balance sheets in the aftermath of the financial crisis. The analysis specifically pointed to the Bank of Japan as a leader in this regard, noting that "BoJs leverage ratio and the size of its balance sheet relative to GDP has reached unprecedented levels." This observation underscored how monetary policy had evolved beyond traditional interest rate manipulation into direct asset purchases and other unconventional measures.

The 2016 report also documented the expansion from 2007 to 2015, illustrating the dramatic transformation of central bank balance sheets over nearly a decade of crisis response. This historical perspective set the stage for understanding the magnitude of monetary intervention that would follow.

**2020** brought even more dramatic developments to the narrative, as the global pandemic triggered unprecedented monetary responses. The report documented how central bank balance sheets had expanded from $0.9 trillion in 2008 to an astonishing $7 trillion during the pandemic period. The Federal Reserve's balance sheet served as a prime example, growing from USD 0.9trn to 4.5trn between 2008 and 2015, and then experiencing further explosive growth following the initial round of Federal Reserve measures to combat the catastrophic economic effects of Covid-19. The report described this as a "breathtaking" expansion that represented an extraordinary commitment of central bank resources to stabilize financial markets and support economic activity during an unprecedented crisis.
