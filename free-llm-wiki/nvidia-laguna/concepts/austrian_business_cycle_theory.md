# Austrian Business Cycle Theory

The Austrian Business Cycle Theory (ABCT), pioneered by Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich August von Hayek, explains how excessive liquidity in financial markets triggers artificial booms that inevitably lead to painful corrections. This economic framework identifies the relationship between monetary expansion, asset price inflation, and subsequent consumer price inflation, providing a lens through which to understand the propagation of economic cycles. The theory's significance lies in its prediction that capital misallocation and asset bubbles, driven by central bank policies, will eventually manifest as broader inflationary pressures and economic instability.

## How the argument evolved

**2014** established the foundational mechanism of ABCT by documenting how asset price inflation precedes consumer price inflation, with "prices of capital goods (= asset price inflation) are the first to increase" while "consumer price inflation only sets in at a later stage." This early formulation emphasized the temporal sequence of inflationary pressures across different price levels.

**2016** provided concrete historical validation by identifying the "disproportionate growth rate of M3 in the time period before 2008" as "the root cause of various bubbles and misallocations of capital," concluding that this pattern "represents a textbook example of Austrian business cycle theory (ABCT)." The report connected monetary expansion directly to observable market distortions.

**2020** confirmed the theory's continued relevance by observing that "The present development corresponds exactly to the textbook description of the ABCT," noting how "asset prices rise first (= asset price inflation) and only then does consumer price inflation (= inflation) set in." This reinforced the predictive power of the temporal sequence described years earlier.

**2023** synthesized the theory's core insight by explaining how "flooding the markets with liquidity triggers an artificial boom," directly linking central bank monetary interventions to the cyclical distortions that ABCT predicts. The report positioned ABCT as an essential framework for understanding contemporary monetary policy consequences.
