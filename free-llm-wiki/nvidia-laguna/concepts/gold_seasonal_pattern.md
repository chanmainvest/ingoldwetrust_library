# Gold Seasonal Pattern

The Gold Seasonal Pattern refers to the recurring tendency of gold prices to rise during specific months of the year, particularly from July through February, with the majority of annual gains concentrated in this period. This phenomenon has been observed consistently across multiple years and is attributed to a combination of seasonal demand factors, including increased jewelry and investment buying during holiday seasons, as well as institutional rebalancing activities. Understanding this pattern is significant for investors seeking to time their gold purchases strategically, as it offers insights into potential price movements based on historical trends.

## How the argument evolved

**2018** marked the foundational year for understanding the Gold Seasonal Pattern in the In Gold We Trust reports, establishing a clear timeline for when gold typically appreciates. The analysis revealed that "Gold typically rises from the beginning of July to the end of February," with "Almost the entire average annual gain tends to occur during that period." This observation set the stage for deeper exploration into the mechanics behind seasonal gold movements, highlighting a nearly eight-month window of positive momentum that consistently outperformed other periods of the year.

Building on this foundation, **2019** introduced a critical refinement by examining how election cycles influence seasonal patterns. The report noted that "The following chart shows the annual development of gold in pre-election years," revealing that "It can be seen that the seasonal tailwind is particularly positive in the second half of the year." This discovery added a new dimension to the seasonal analysis, suggesting that political uncertainty and increased market volatility during election years amplify the traditional seasonal gains, making the July-to-February window even more pronounced in pre-election contexts.

The evolution continued in **2021**, which shifted focus to post-election years and identified distinct monthly variations within the broader seasonal framework. The analysis observed that "seasonal headwinds set in from the end of May but reverse again at the beginning of July," while noting that "June is – after January – the second weakest month in post-election years." This nuanced understanding demonstrated that while the overall seasonal pattern persists, the specific timing and strength of monthly movements can vary significantly depending on the electoral calendar, requiring investors to adapt their strategies accordingly.
