# Gold Seasonal Pattern

The Gold Seasonal Pattern refers to the recurring tendency of gold prices to rise during specific months of the year, particularly from July through February, with the majority of annual gains occurring within this window. This phenomenon has been observed consistently across multiple years and is attributed to a combination of seasonal demand factors, including increased jewelry and investment buying during holiday periods, as well as institutional rebalancing activities. Understanding this pattern is significant for investors seeking to time their gold purchases strategically, as it offers insights into potential price movements based on historical trends rather than short-term market volatility.

## How the argument evolved

**2018** marked the foundational recognition of the Gold Seasonal Pattern in the In Gold We Trust reports, establishing a clear timeline for when gold typically appreciates. The analysis revealed that "Gold typically rises from the beginning of July to the end of February," with "Almost the entire average annual gain tends to occur during that period." This observation set the stage for understanding how seasonal cycles influence gold's performance, highlighting a nearly eight-month window of upward momentum that consistently delivered strong returns.

Building on this foundation, **2019** introduced a critical refinement by examining how election cycles interact with seasonal patterns. The report noted that "The following chart shows the annual development of gold in pre-election years," revealing that "It can be seen that the seasonal tailwind is particularly positive in the second half of the year." This discovery added a layer of complexity, suggesting that political calendar events amplify the natural seasonal tendencies, with pre-election years showing even more pronounced gains during the traditional seasonal window.

The evolution continued in **2021**, which shifted focus to post-election years and identified nuanced variations within the established pattern. The analysis observed that "seasonal headwinds set in from the end of May but reverse again at the beginning of July," while noting that "June is – after January – the second weakest month in post-election years." This finding demonstrated that while the overarching seasonal pattern persists, its expression varies depending on the electoral context, with specific months showing distinct weakness that investors should account for when developing timing strategies.
