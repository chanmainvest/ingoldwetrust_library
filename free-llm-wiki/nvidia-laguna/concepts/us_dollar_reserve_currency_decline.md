# US Dollar Reserve Currency Decline

The US dollar's position as the world's primary reserve currency is eroding, with its global share falling from 73% in 2001 to 58% in 2022. This decline reflects a fundamental shift in the international monetary system, driven by the weaponization of sanctions and the recognition that the dollar's dominance is no longer sustainable. The transition signals a potential reordering of global financial architecture, with implications for monetary policy, trade, and investment strategies worldwide.

## How the argument evolved

**2017** marked an early acknowledgment within official circles that the dollar's reserve currency status was inherently temporary. The report noted that "Central banks and governments have long been aware that the dollar has a sell-by date as a reserve currency," emphasizing that this decline was not merely a theoretical concern but an unfolding reality. It highlighted a pivotal change in global power dynamics, observing that "the decisive new global reality was the appearance on the world scene of America as simultaneously the richest and militarily the most powerful player. During the latter part of the 20th century no other power even came close. That era is now ending." This framing suggested that the dollar's hegemony was tied to US unipolar dominance, which was beginning to wane.

By **2023**, the narrative had crystallized into a more urgent assessment of de-dollarization as an inevitable "Final Showdown." The report documented concrete data showing that "the US dollar's share as a global reserve currency fell" and specifically noted that "the US dollar's share of global currency reserves dropped to only 58% in 2022." This represented a significant contraction from the 73% peak in 2001, with the analysis pointing to sanctions as a key accelerant of the trend. The documentation of an "11% market share loss since 2016" underscored that the decline was not gradual but accelerating, reflecting growing international efforts to diversify away from dollar-denominated assets and transactions.
