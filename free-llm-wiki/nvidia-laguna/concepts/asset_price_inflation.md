# Asset Price Inflation

Asset price inflation refers to the sustained increase in the prices of assets such as real estate, stocks, and bonds, distinct from and often occurring alongside consumer price inflation. This phenomenon has become a critical focus in understanding modern monetary policy, as central banks' expansive measures can inflate asset values even when traditional price indicators remain subdued. The significance lies in its implications for wealth distribution, financial stability, and the effectiveness of monetary policy in reflecting true economic conditions.

## How the argument evolved

**2016** — The 2016 report "The Holy Grail Of Monetary Policy" highlighted how the divergence between robust money supply growth and relatively low consumer price inflation had primarily become manifest in the form of asset price inflation. The report emphasized that this dynamic particularly affected real estate and bonds, noting that "asset price inflation amounted to 7.8% last year, the highest level since the statistics were first compiled." This marked a pivotal recognition that conventional inflation metrics were failing to capture the full scope of monetary expansion, as the excess liquidity was flowing into financial assets rather than consumer goods.

**2018** — By 2018, the analysis in "Inflation Vs Deflation The Big Showdown" deepened the understanding of this phenomenon. The report underscored that monetary inflation does not only affect consumer prices but also asset prices, which are underrepresented in public debate. It pointed out that "rising share prices tend to be seen as positive," revealing a cognitive bias in how markets interpret inflationary pressures. This evolution showed a growing awareness that asset price inflation, while often perceived as beneficial, represents a form of monetary distortion that can lead to systemic risks when asset bubbles form.

The progression from 2016 to 2018 reflects an increasing sophistication in analyzing how monetary policy's unintended consequences manifest through asset markets, with the reports collectively arguing that the true inflationary impact is hidden in the rising values of financial and real assets rather than in everyday consumer prices.
