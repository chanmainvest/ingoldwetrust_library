# Monetary Tectonics

Monetary tectonics is the conceptual framework used to describe the ongoing struggle between disinflationary and inflationary forces within global financial systems, and it holds significant implications for investment strategy. This dynamic tension shapes monetary policy decisions, asset allocation, and market volatility, making it a critical lens through which investors analyze economic risks and opportunities.

## How the argument evolved

**2015** marked the initial articulation of monetary tectonics as a central theme in the investment discourse. The report emphasized that "we have in this context extensively discussed the struggle between deflationary and inflationary forces," establishing the foundational premise that these opposing economic pressures create a complex, ever-shifting landscape for investors. The concept was presented as a key determinant of market outcomes, with the report asserting that "This conflict, which we have dubbed 'monetary tectonics', is very important for investors." At this stage, the focus was on identifying the structural forces at play and their direct impact on investment decisions.

**2018** deepened the analysis by highlighting the inherent instability within this system. The report described the "interdependencies of inflationary and deflationary forces as monetary tectonics," underscoring that these relationships are not static but rather characterized by "potential instability." This evolution in thinking revealed that the very nature of monetary tectonics introduces systemic risk, as the balance between forces can shift rapidly. The report cautioned that "the risk of a deflationary shock is always around the corner," suggesting that investors must remain vigilant to the precarious equilibrium that defines this monetary landscape. The 2018 perspective thus framed monetary tectonics not merely as a descriptive term but as a warning about the fragility of the global economic system.
