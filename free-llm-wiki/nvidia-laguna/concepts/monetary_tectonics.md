# Monetary Tectonics

Monetary tectonics is the conceptual framework used to describe the ongoing struggle between disinflationary and inflationary forces within global financial systems, and it holds significant implications for investment strategy. This dynamic tension shapes monetary policy decisions and market behavior, making it a critical lens for understanding economic risks and opportunities.

## How the argument evolved

**2015** marked the initial articulation of monetary tectonics as a central theme in the investment discourse. The report explicitly introduced the term to describe the "struggle between deflationary and inflationary forces," emphasizing that "This conflict, which we have dubbed 'monetary tectonics', is very important for investors." At this stage, the concept was positioned as a fundamental analytical tool for navigating the complex interplay of economic pressures.

**2018** saw the refinement and deepening of the monetary tectonics framework, particularly in the context of inflation versus deflation dynamics. The report expanded on the concept by highlighting the "interdependencies of inflationary and deflationary forces as monetary tectonics," noting that these relationships create "potential instability" within the system. A key development in this iteration was the recognition of persistent downside risk, with the report cautioning that "the risk of a deflationary shock is always around the corner." This evolution demonstrated how monetary tectonics had matured from a descriptive framework to a predictive model for anticipating systemic vulnerabilities.
