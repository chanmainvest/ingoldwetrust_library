# China's Currency Internationalization

China's currency internationalization represents a strategic effort by the Chinese government to elevate the global role of the renminbi (yuan) as a reserve and transaction currency, thereby reducing dependence on the US dollar. This initiative is a cornerstone of China's broader economic diplomacy and financial de-dollarization strategy, aiming to reshape international monetary structures and enhance Beijing's influence in global finance.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** marked a pivotal phase in the report's analysis, highlighting China's multi-pronged approach to renminbi internationalization. The document emphasized that "China has been working on various fronts for many years to reduce the influence of the US dollar and to promote the use of the renminbi." Key strategies included pricing commodities like oil in renminbi and developing domestic financial markets to attract foreign capital. A significant milestone noted was that "Around 14% of oil trading on major stock exchanges now takes place" in the currency, signaling tangible progress in embedding the renminbi into global trade flows. The report also suggested that China could advance its goals "without the accumulation of US government bonds," indicating a shift toward financial autonomy.

By **2024**, the narrative had evolved to underscore diplomatic and geopolitical dimensions of this strategy. The analysis highlighted that "China has made significant efforts to forward the goal of renminbi internationalization," particularly through high-level agreements with major oil producers. The report specifically pointed to "forging key agreements with Saudi Arabia and the UAE" as critical steps toward establishing the renminbi as a viable alternative in energy markets. Additionally, the expansion of the Belt and Road Initiative to include new countries and projects was presented as a mechanism to deepen economic ties and incentivize the use of the renminbi across participating nations. This evolution reflects a transition from market-based development to strategic geopolitical alignment, reinforcing China's vision of a more multipolar global financial system.
