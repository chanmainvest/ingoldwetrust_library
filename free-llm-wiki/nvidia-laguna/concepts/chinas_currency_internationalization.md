# China's Currency Internationalization

China's currency internationalization represents a strategic effort by the Chinese government to elevate the global role of the renminbi (yuan) as a reserve and transaction currency, thereby reducing dependence on the US dollar and enhancing Beijing's influence in the international monetary system. This initiative is a cornerstone of China's broader economic diplomacy and financial de-dollarization strategy, aiming to reshape global trade dynamics and financial architecture.

## How the argument evolved

**2020** marked a pivotal phase in the report's analysis, highlighting China's multi-pronged approach to renminbi internationalization. The report noted that "China has been working on various fronts for many years to reduce the influence of the US dollar and to promote the use of the renminbi." Key strategies included pricing oil in renminbi and developing domestic financial markets. A significant milestone was the expansion of oil trading in yuan, with "around 14% of oil trading on major stock exchanges now taking place" in the currency. The report also emphasized China's potential to achieve this without accumulating US government bonds, suggesting a parallel financial architecture.

**2024** saw the narrative evolve to underscore concrete diplomatic and economic breakthroughs. The report stated that "China has made significant efforts to forward the goal of renminbi internationalization," particularly through high-level agreements with major oil producers. Specifically, "forging key agreements with Saudi Arabia and the UAE" was highlighted as a critical step toward pricing oil in renminbi, which could dramatically increase its global usage. Simultaneously, the expansion of the Belt and Road Initiative to include new countries and projects was presented as a vehicle for embedding the renminbi deeper into international trade and investment networks. These developments signaled a maturation of China's strategy, moving from gradual market development to securing landmark partnerships that could accelerate the renminbi's global adoption.
