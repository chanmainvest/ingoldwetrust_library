# Gold as Monetary Asset

Gold has long held a dual identity—both as a tangible commodity and as a form of money. Over time, the In Gold We Trust reports have increasingly emphasized its role as a monetary asset, highlighting its unique status as a reserve asset and currency code (XAU). This shift reflects a growing recognition that gold’s intrinsic value and historical function as money make it distinct from other commodities, especially in times of economic uncertainty.

## How the argument evolved

**2011** marked the beginning of a clear thematic shift in how gold was framed within the report. The introduction explicitly stated that “gold has been more and more regarded as monetary,” signaling a departure from viewing gold solely as a commodity. This perspective underscored the “monetary character of gold,” positioning it not just as a tradable metal but as a foundational element of global financial systems. The report emphasized gold’s role as a key reserve asset, noting its formal recognition through the currency code XAU, which further solidified its legitimacy in international finance.

By **2015**, the argument had matured and expanded. The introduction made a deliberate distinction: “We analyze gold primarily as a monetary asset and not as a commodity.” This framing was not merely semantic—it reflected a deeper analytical approach that considered gold’s competitive advantages in a deteriorating monetary landscape. The report observed that “the competitive position of gold relative to paper money and other asset classes has improved considerably,” suggesting that declining confidence in fiat currencies and traditional financial instruments was driving investors toward gold’s inherent stability. This evolution from commodity to monetary asset was not abrupt but rather a gradual revaluation of gold’s role in preserving value amid systemic risks.
