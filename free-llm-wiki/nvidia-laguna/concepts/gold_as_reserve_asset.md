# Gold as Reserve Asset

Gold has long served as a cornerstone of monetary systems, but its role as a reserve asset has evolved significantly in response to shifting global economic dynamics. From central banks diversifying away from the US dollar to individuals seeking protection against energy-driven inflation, gold’s unique properties as both a financial asset and a commodity make it an increasingly vital component of international reserves and personal wealth preservation strategies.

## How the argument evolved

**2018** marked a pivotal moment in understanding gold’s role in the modern financial architecture, particularly as central banks began reevaluating their reserve allocations. The report emphasized that "Gold reserve has been a key part of countries' diversified international reserves," highlighting its dual nature as an asset blending financial stability with commodity characteristics. This perspective was reinforced by the observation that "As a special asset with properties of financial assets and commodities," gold occupies a unique space in portfolios designed to hedge against systemic risks. The dramatic example of Russia tripling its gold reserves over a decade underscored how geopolitical tensions and currency instability could accelerate central bank adoption of gold as a strategic hedge.

**2022** brought a starkly different context—globalization unraveling under the pressure of energy wars and surging commodity prices. In this environment, the report argued that "With globalization breaking down and the rise in energy prices, gold will become increasingly important on the sovereign level and on the individual level." The logic was clear: as traditional monetary anchors weaken and input costs spiral, investors require assets that can "rise in price with the rise in the prices of energy and commodities and other inputs." This necessity spans both institutional and retail spheres, with the report concluding that "That is why you need a reserve asset like gold and at least hold some of it on the sovereign level and on the individual level to preserve your purchasing power."
