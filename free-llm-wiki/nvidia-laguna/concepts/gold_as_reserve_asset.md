# Gold as Reserve Asset

Gold has long served as a cornerstone of monetary systems, functioning not merely as a commodity but as a critical reserve asset for nations seeking financial stability and diversification. Its unique duality—possessing both financial and physical commodity characteristics—positions it distinctly from traditional fiat currencies and other assets. Over time, the role of gold as a reserve asset has evolved in response to shifting global economic dynamics, with investment reports like *In Gold We Trust* chronicling its resurgence amid geopolitical instability and monetary uncertainty.

## How the argument evolved

**2018** marked a pivotal discussion around the de-dollarization trend and the growing importance of gold in international reserves. The report emphasized that "Gold reserve has been a key part of countries' diversified international reserves," highlighting its dual nature as "a special asset with properties of financial assets and commodities." This framing underscored why nations like Russia were actively increasing their gold holdings, with the report noting, "Why has Russia tripled its gold reserves in the last 10 years?" The narrative positioned gold not just as a store of value but as a strategic tool for economic sovereignty amid changing global financial architectures.

By **2022**, the conversation had shifted in response to the fragmentation of globalization and surging energy prices. In an exclusive interview with Luke Gromen, the report argued that "With globalization breaking down and the rise in energy prices, gold will become increasingly important on the sovereign level and on the individual level." This evolution in thinking reflected a broader recognition that gold’s ability to preserve purchasing power extends beyond mere scarcity—it also responds to the real-world costs of energy and production inputs. As Gromen stated, "You need a reserve asset that has the ability to rise in price with the rise in the prices of energy and commodities and other inputs." Thus, the 2022 edition reinforced gold’s role as a resilient hedge against systemic economic disruption, advocating for its retention not only by central banks but also by individuals navigating an increasingly volatile financial landscape.
