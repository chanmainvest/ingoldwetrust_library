# Russia's De-Dollarization Strategy

Russia's de-dollarization strategy represents a systematic effort to reduce dependence on the US dollar in international trade and reserve holdings, driven by geopolitical tensions and economic sanctions. This approach has evolved from gradual diversification to rapid structural change, particularly accelerated by Western sanctions following geopolitical conflicts. The strategy encompasses shifting trade settlements to national currencies, reallocating sovereign wealth reserves, and strengthening economic ties with non-Western partners through alternative financial architectures.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** marked the initial documentation of Russia's de-dollarization efforts, with reports highlighting the country's active movement away from the US dollar in both trade and reserves. The narrative captured a pivotal moment where Russia was not simply abandoning the dollar, but rather responding to what one observer noted: "We aren't ditching the dollar, the dollar is ditching us." At this stage, Russia had sold off US Treasuries worth just over USD 100bn and was switching its reserves into euros and yuan, signaling a strategic reallocation of foreign assets toward currencies of emerging alternative economic centers.

**2024** presented a dramatically accelerated timeline, with the strategy having undergone a fundamental transformation following Western sanctions. The report documented that Russia had dramatically reduced dollar usage in trade, shifting decisively to rubles and renminbi. The data revealed a seismic shift: "Until 2022, the US dollar was used for around half of Russia's trade" but "this figure has now plummeted to just 13%." The 2024 analysis emphasized that "the majority of payments, over 90%, are made in national currencies," representing not just a policy shift but a complete restructuring of Russia's international economic engagement model. This evolution from gradual diversification to near-complete replacement of dollar transactions reflects how sanctions acted as a catalyst, transforming de-dollarization from strategic hedging into urgent necessity.
