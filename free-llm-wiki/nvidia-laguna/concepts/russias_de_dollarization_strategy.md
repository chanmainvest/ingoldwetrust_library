# Russia's De-Dollarization Strategy

Russia's de-dollarization strategy represents a systematic effort to reduce dependence on the US dollar in international trade and reserve holdings, driven by geopolitical tensions and economic sanctions. This shift reflects broader concerns about dollar dominance and has significant implications for global financial architecture, particularly as Russia pivots toward alternative currencies and gold-backed systems.

## How the argument evolved

**2019** marked the beginning of documented evidence showing Russia actively moving away from the US dollar in both trade and reserves. The report captured the sentiment that "We aren't ditching the dollar, the dollar is ditching us," highlighting how external pressures were accelerating this transition. At that time, Russia had sold off US Treasuries worth just over USD 100bn and was switching its reserves into euros and yuan, signaling a clear diversification strategy away from Western financial instruments.

**2024** provided a dramatic update on how this strategy had evolved under the pressure of full-scale sanctions. The report revealed that Russia had dramatically reduced dollar usage in trade, shifting decisively to rubles and renminbi. Most significantly, "the majority of payments, over 90%, are made in national currencies," representing a complete reversal from just two years prior when "the US dollar was used for around half of Russia's trade." This figure has now plummeted to just 13%, demonstrating the rapid and successful implementation of de-dollarization measures following the 2022 sanctions.

The evolution from gradual reserve diversification to complete trade currency transformation illustrates how Russia's de-dollarization strategy matured from a cautious hedge into an urgent necessity, ultimately achieving what was once considered a long-term strategic goal in just a few short years.
