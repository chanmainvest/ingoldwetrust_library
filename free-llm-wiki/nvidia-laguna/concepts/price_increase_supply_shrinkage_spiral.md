# Price-Increase Supply-Shrinkage Spiral

The Price-Increase Supply-Shrinkage Spiral is a self-reinforcing economic dynamic where rising costs force firms to exit the market, reducing supply and further driving up prices. This mechanism is particularly relevant in inflationary environments where production costs are climbing and monetary policy is accommodative. The concept has been a key theme in the In Gold We Trust reports, with the 2022 and 2023 analyses highlighting how this spiral can become entrenched, creating persistent upward pressure on prices and complicating central bank efforts to achieve price stability.

## How the argument evolved

**2022** — The 2022 report first articulated the spiral as a direct consequence of rising production costs. It explained that "The (sharp) rise in production costs forces companies out of the market" and that this "causing supply to shrink, which further fuels the upward pressure on prices." This framing positioned the spiral as a supply-side phenomenon driven by cost pressures rather than demand excess.

**2023** — By 2023, the analysis had evolved to incorporate the role of monetary policy, linking the spiral more explicitly to central bank actions. The report noted that "higher interest rates can have an inflationary effect in the short term" and emphasized that "current inflation is mainly the result of substantial budget deficits monetized by central banks." It then connected these factors through the mechanism that "Higher borrowing costs lead to higher production costs for the same quantity of goods," suggesting that even traditionally "tight" monetary policy could inadvertently feed the spiral through increased financing costs.
