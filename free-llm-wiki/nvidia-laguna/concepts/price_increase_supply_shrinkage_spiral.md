# Price-Increase Supply-Shrinkage Spiral

The Price-Increase Supply-Shrinkage Spiral is a self-reinforcing economic dynamic where rising costs push companies out of the market, reducing supply and further driving up prices. This mechanism amplifies inflationary pressures and complicates monetary policy responses. The concept has been a key focus in the annual "In Gold We Trust" reports, with the 2022 and 2023 editions offering complementary analyses that trace how production costs, interest rates, and fiscal policy interact to create persistent price pressures.

## How the argument evolved

**2022** — The 2022 report framed the spiral as originating in rising production costs, which directly force companies out of the market. The analysis emphasized that this cost pressure "causing supply to shrink, which further fuels the upward pressure on prices," creating a feedback loop where declining supply exacerbates inflation. This dynamic was presented as a core driver of persistent inflation trends, with the report noting that the sharp rise in production costs was not merely transitory but structural.

**2023** — By 2023, the report expanded the narrative to include monetary policy as a catalyst. It argued that "higher interest rates can have an inflationary effect in the short term," introducing a new layer to the spiral. The mechanism now involved higher borrowing costs leading to "higher production costs for the same quantity of goods," which then pressured firms to reduce output or exit the market. Simultaneously, the report attributed "current inflation [to] substantial budget deficits monetized by central banks," suggesting that fiscal expansion and monetary accommodation had set the stage for the spiral to take hold. The 2023 analysis thus integrated interest rate dynamics with supply-side constraints, portraying the spiral as both demand-driven (via monetary policy) and supply-driven (via cost pressures).
