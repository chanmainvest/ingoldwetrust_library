# De-dollarization Trend

The de-dollarization trend represents a growing global shift away from the US dollar as the primary reserve and transaction currency, driven by concerns over dollar dominance and its associated geopolitical risks. This movement reflects increasing efforts by major economies to diversify away from dollar dependency through alternative trade arrangements, reserve allocations, and payment systems. The significance of this trend lies in its potential to reshape the global financial architecture and reduce the United States' monetary influence worldwide.

## How the argument evolved

**2017** marked the initial recognition of de-dollarization as a tangible phenomenon, with the report noting that "The trend becomes ever more tangible and can be described by the following term: de-dollarization." The analysis highlighted that since 1973, the dollar standard had been fundamentally based on usage demand—"they were needed"—but this foundation was beginning to erode as "The world is looking for alternatives to the dollar – and finds them more and more often."

By **2019**, the report documented concrete actions by major players, stating that "More and more countries are looking for alternatives to the US dollar" and identifying specific actors: "Major players such as China, Russia and now also Europe are taking steps to undermine the US dollar system." This represented a significant evolution from theoretical concern to active policy implementation, with Europe joining established efforts by China and Russia to challenge dollar hegemony.

The **2023** perspective emphasized the strategic response of emerging markets, observing that "emerging-market countries have taken careful note of the militarization of money" and are "trying to reduce their dependence on the US dollar" through "increasing efforts to develop alternative trade currencies and payment systems." This iteration showed how the trend had matured into a coordinated, multi-regional movement with institutional and systemic implications.
