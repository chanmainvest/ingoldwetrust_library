# Seasonal Election Year Patterns

Seasonal election year patterns refer to recurring price movements in gold and silver that align with the political calendar of U.S. elections, particularly midterm and presidential years. These patterns manifest as predictable periods of weakness in precious metals markets, driven by a confluence of investor behavior, portfolio rebalancing, and seasonal trading dynamics. Understanding these cycles is critical for anticipating market direction and positioning within gold-centric investment strategies.

## How the argument evolved

**2022** marked an early articulation of this phenomenon, identifying that gold faces seasonal headwinds from late May in midterm election years, reversing in early July, with June being the second weakest month. The report noted that "seasonal headwinds set in from the end of May, but reverse again at the beginning of July," highlighting a distinct seasonal rhythm that diverges from typical market behavior. It further emphasized that "June is, after May, the second weakest month in election years," underscoring the predictability of this weakness. The analysis cautioned that "from a seasonal perspective, further headwinds can therefore be expected in the coming weeks," suggesting that these patterns are not merely historical but actionable for near-term positioning.

By **2024**, the framework had expanded to encompass presidential election years, broadening the scope beyond midterms. The report observed that gold and silver exhibit seasonal weakness from July to November in U.S. presidential election years, with silver showing a particularly pronounced decline from August to the end of November. This evolution reflects a maturation of the thesis—from a focus on midterm-specific dynamics to a more comprehensive understanding of how election cycles across different years influence precious metals. The consistent thread is the identification of a seasonal trough that aligns with the summer-to-early-fall period, regardless of the type of election, reinforcing the reliability of these patterns over time.
