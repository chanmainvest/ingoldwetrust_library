# De-dollarization Movement

The de-dollarization movement represents a systemic shift away from the US dollar's dominance as the global reserve and trade currency, driven by major economies seeking monetary multipolarity and reduced exposure to American financial hegemony. This transformation, gaining momentum over the past decade, reflects growing dissatisfaction with dollar-centric systems among key global players and signals a potential restructuring of international monetary arrangements. The movement manifests through coordinated efforts to increase gold holdings, develop alternative payment systems, and conduct trade in non-dollar currencies.

## How the argument evolved

**2017** marked the initial recognition of de-dollarization as an accelerating reality, with the report noting that "statements of Trump, Pence, Greenspan and Zoellick are compatible with the above discussed process of de-dollarization." The analysis highlighted "steps toward a multi-polar future" and observed that "de-dollarization are in the meantime coming thick and fast," suggesting the process was already underway rather than theoretical.

By **2018**, the focus shifted to concrete actions, with countries moving away from using the US dollar as the primary reserve and trade currency. The report emphasized that "many of the world's countries... no longer respect the US" and acknowledged that "the process of de-dollarization is likely to take decades," framing it as a long-term structural change rather than a short-term trend.

**2020** brought urgency to the narrative, declaring "the endgame has begun" as major economies reduced dollar dependence through gold purchases, alternative currencies, and non-dollar trade arrangements. The analysis noted "the gradual de-dollarization of the world" and observed that "Russia and China have presented the USA with a difficult choice," while "many countries in the world began turning away from using the dollar as a reserve currency."

In **2021**, the report expanded the scope to include Europe, Russia, China, and Iran, all seeking alternatives to US dollar dominance. A key development was the "dissatisfaction of Europe, Russia, China and Iran with having to trade oil in US dollars," coupled with Europe's recognition that "We need to reduce sanctions risks by bolstering our technological independence, by switching to payments in our national currencies."

**2023** marked a pivotal transition from "rather passive preparations to active reorientation of the East – led by China." The report explicitly named this phenomenon "de-dollarization – the resistance of countries like China, Russia, the rest of the BRICS," and highlighted "the resistance of countries like China, Russia, the rest of the BRICS, parts of Europe and the Middle East to the dominant position of the US dollar."

**2024** demonstrated accelerating momentum, with "significant progress by players such as China and Russia to undermine the greenback's dominance" and the entry of "crucial players like Saudi Arabia and the UAE" into the movement. The report concluded that "The rallying cry to escape the dollar system is spreading to new ears," indicating the movement's expanding reach beyond traditional adopters.
