### Key Takeaways 

- Broad adoption of innovations that occurred in the gold mining industry over the recent years is a response to mounting operational constraints such as declining ore grades, water scarcity, higher fuel/ energy costs, and decarbonization pressure. 

   - Exploration is getting faster and cheaper through better sensing and drilling control: UAV geophysics (drones) for early screening and mapping, fiber-optic DAS for borehole seismic monitoring, and directional drilling and rotary steerable systems. 

- Decarbonization is reshaping mine planning and haulage via battery-electric vehicles, trolley assist, hydrogen pilots, and in-pit crushing and conveying (IPCC), aiming to cut diesel use and emissions as well as some ventilation needs, but often with higher upfront CAPEX or infrastructure requirements. 

- Processing and waste-management innovations such as thiosulfate leaching or dry-stack tailings target energy, water, toxicity, and risk reduction.

### More Electricity, Less Carbon 

**Electrification in transportation has been a common trend over the last decade, and it has also impacted the gold mining industry.** The adoption of electric vehicles is important for several reasons. They not only reduce emissions, which helps mining companies meet their ESG targets, they also significantly reduce operating costs, as electricity is usually cheaper than diesel. Electric trucks provide instant torque, and during downhill hauls a large amount of energy can be recovered. A study by IDTechEx claims that a 150-tonne electric haul truck can save over USD 5.5mn in energy costs over its lifetime. Another advantage is a lower number of moving parts in electromotors, which means fewer breakdowns and lower maintenance costs. 

## **_„Any conversation I have about innovation starts with the ultimate goal._** Sergey Brin 

**Moreover, using emission-free vehicles also limits the need for extensive ventilation systems in underground mines.** This further reduces operating costs and also CAPEX costs. The electric vehicles also improve working conditions in the mines, as they generate less noise, heat, and emissions. Of course, there are also disadvantages. The battery packs needed to fuel the trucks are large, which limits payload capacity. Recharging the batteries takes more time than simply filling the tank with diesel. Moreover, the capacity of the batteries may deteriorate rapidly in harsh winter conditions, which makes the BEVs unsuitable for Arctic or Subarctic regions, especially in open-pit mines. There are additional costs to build the infrastructure, such as charging bays, a reinforced electric grid, or even a new power plant. Moreover, the electric trucks alone are more expensive than their diesel counterparts. So while reducing operating costs, they increase capital costs. Nevertheless, according to a study conducted by Grand View Research, the USD 3.05bn electric mining equipment market should grow by a CAGR of 15.2% and exceed USD 10.5bn by 2033.

Another technology reducing fuel consumption and the carbon footprint of mining operations is trolley-assist systems. They help haulage trucks, both diesel and electric, to ride more quickly on uphill climbs. This results in reduced fuel consumption, a decreased carbon footprint, prolonged truck engine life, and shorter turnaround times. A study of the Copper Mountain mine shows that the trolley-assist system increased the climbing speed of haul trucks by 59%, reduced the operating cost per tonne of moved material by 16%, and lowered carbon dioxide emissions by 42%.<sup>3</sup> A further evolutionary step are the dynamic-charging battery trolleys that enable recharging of batteries while the trucks are hauling, eliminating the need for charging pauses. 

- **_„If you don’t innovate fast, disrupt your industry, disrupt yourself, you’ll be left behind._** John Chambers 

Electrification of mining vehicles is not the only way to reduce fuel costs and pollution. Another potential way was shown by Anglo American in 2022, with its introduction of a 2MW hydrogen-battery hybrid haulage truck at its Mogalakwena PGM mine. According to Anglo American, it was the largest hydrogen-powered haulage truck to date. Technically, it is a Komatsu 930E truck, with its diesel engine replaced by hydrogen fuel cells and a battery pack. It weighs 220 tonnes and can carry up to 290 tonnes of material (which equals 50 elephants or 220 cars). According to the company, full adoption of this type of truck could reduce the volume of diesel emissions at its open-pit mines by approximately 80%. 

Unfortunately, although the truck has been serving in the mine for a year, the project cannot be marked as a success. It turned out that the development of on-site hydrogen infrastructure takes longer and is more expensive than originally expected. Following a change of priorities, Anglo American stopped financing First Mode, the company that stood behind the trucks, and it went bankrupt. However, the idea of using hydrogen to power mining equipment is not dead, as in February 2025, Komatsu started testing its HD785 truck equipped with a hydrogen combustion engine. However, this is a different hydrogen-based technology.

**The target of reducing carbon emissions has also resulted in the introduction, or more exactly the reintroduction, of  in-pit crushing and conveying (IPCC) systems.** The concept is not new; it dates back to the middle of the 20th century. The ore is crushed directly in the pit and subsequently transported to the processing facility by conveyor belts. This eliminates the need for hauling trucks and reduces fuel consumption and volume of emissions, as the conveyors are powered by electricity. The biggest innovation in this system is the adoption of gearless conveyors. They use high-power electric motors that drive the pulley directly, without using a gearbox. The gearless systems are suitable for long distances, as well as for steep climbs. Moreover, the elimination of gearboxes means a reduction of moving parts, with fewer breakages, longer maintenance intervals, and lower operating costs. On the other hand, gearless conveyor systems are significantly more expensive than the classic ones. Therefore, their adoption is especially efficient for large-tonnage long-life mines. Among the biggest gearless conveyor producers are ABB and Siemens. According to ABB, the gearless conveyors have a lifetime more than 10 years longer than standard geared conveyors, and they are also less noisy (73 dB vs. 85 dB) and reduce energy consumption and CO2 footprint by 6–10%.

### Innovating the Exploration Process 

**The exploration process has enjoyed numerous innovations, too. One of the main advances in this area, especially in its very early phases, is the adoption of drones to perform the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) geophysics.** The drone platforms can carry all the equipment needed to perform geomagnetic surveys. In comparison to classical aircraft with a human crew, the drones are much cheaper, and the logistics are easier. They can also operate in more problematic areas and worse weather conditions, without endangering the operators. High-definition satellite imagery combined with AI, provided by companies like CAP2, Terra AI, or Earth AI, is also a major innovation.<sup>4</sup> 

Another innovation is distributed acoustic sensing (DAS). This technology uses optical fiber cables to create a string of acoustic sensors that can be used for vertical seismic profiling in drill holes, in place of classical geophones. The DAS systems have numerous advantages. They enable continuous monitoring over the whole length of the drill hole. Moreover, they are much more durable, and their usage is cheaper. In theory, the fiber cable can remain in the drill hole for decades and still be functional. A huge advantage is much quicker and easier installation. Moreover, the DAS geophones are more sensitive and can better capture weak signals. 

**Several innovations have also been introduced to the exploration drilling area.** While directional diamond drilling is not a new idea, it has been supplemented by numerous technological improvements over the recent years. Directional diamond drilling enables leading the drill hole along a specific path that deviates from a simple vertical trajectory. This enables not only horizontal drilling but also the drilling of numerous branches from a single main drill hole, which may significantly reduce the amount of drilling needed to outline a deposit. ### Advances in Ore Processing and Metallurgy 

###### **Numerous innovations have also been adopted in the metallurgy and ore process-** 

**ing areas over recent years.** The high-pressure grinding rolls (HPGR) are an example. Although this is not a new technology, it found a more widespread adoption in the gold mining industry only over the recent years. Traditionally, the gold mines used SAG mills, where the ore is ground using steel grinding balls. In HPGR, the ore is cracked between two rotating steel rolls. This turns the ore into flakes that are much easier to further process. Compared to SAG mills, HPGR is more energy efficient, as it not only breaks the ore but also creates microfractures that enable better leaching and higher recoveries. Moreover, due to the lower water and energy consumption, less noise and dust, the overall environmental footprint is smaller. On the other hand, HPGR units are more expensive, if the ore is too hard, the rolls can wear relatively quickly, and they are more complex to operate.

Compared to classic flotation technologies, CPF enables the flotation of up to 5x bigger ore particles.** CPF uses fluidized beds, high-energy bubble generation, and low-turbulence zones to keep coarse particles suspended and attached to air bubbles. This technology requires much less ore grinding, which reduces energy consumption considerably. Moreover,

An important step towards sustainable mining is the substitution of cyanides by less toxic alternatives. An example is thiosulfate leaching. According to SGS Minerals Services, thiosulfates are less toxic and also more efficient than cyanides, especially for carbonaceous materials. The main components of thiosulphates are ammonium sulphate and ammonium thiosulphate, which are commonly used as fertilizers. 

The disadvantage of thiosulphate leaching is its higher chemical complexity compared to cyanide leaching. Moreover, although thiosulphates are significantly cheaper than cyanides, their consumption in the leaching process is significantly higher. Another disadvantage is that the common carbon-in-pulp and carbon-in-leach processes of gold recovery are inefficient with thiosulphates. A pioneer in the commercial-scale adoption of thiosulphate leaching is Barrick Mining. The company decided to use this method to process refractory ore at its Goldstrike mine. Laboratory testing started in 2009, a demonstration plant followed a few years later, and the full-scale facility was completed in 2015. Since then, thiosulphate leaching has also found its place in other mining operations, including in urban mining for the extraction of gold, silver, and other metals from electronic waste.<sup>5</sup> 

### New Ways in Tailings and Dam Management 

- **_„Everybody wants to save the earth; nobody wants to help Mom do the dishes._** P.J. O’Rourke 

Recent tailings dam failures, as well as growing water scarcity, have stimulated wider adoption of dry-stack tailings, in which tailings are filtered to eliminate most water. The resulting material, filtered tailings, contains more than 80% solids compared to less than 25% in common tailings and can be placed in a pile and compacted. This reduces the need for embankments and dams; moreover, it eliminates the risk of dam failures and subsequent huge ecological damage. The majority of the filtered water can be recycled, which significantly reduces water consumption. In addition, the dry stacks are much easier to rehabilitate and revegetate. The disadvantages are high capital costs to build the filtration plants, and operating costs are usually higher, too. Another problem is that while dry stacking is suitable for dry climates, in wet climates it can encounter some limitations.

A modern method in mine site reclamation is geomorphic landform design. This process is based on replicating natural landforms, slopes, and drainage networks. Systems like GeoFluv use data regarding local weather patterns and climate conditions, as well as geology and vegetation, to guarantee better long-term slope stability and significantly reduced disruption of the surrounding landscape. 

**An exciting way of dealing with old tailings is phytoremediation, which uses hyperaccumulator plants for phytoextraction and phytostabilisation.** In the tailings area, plants like sunflowers or Indian mustard are planted. These plants are able to absorb metals such as gold or platinum. They are subsequently harvested and dried, and from the resulting material, the metals can be extracted. In the case of phytostabilisation, the aim is to limit the mobility of toxic materials in the tailings. For this purpose, plants such as vetiver grass are usually used. They bind the contaminants and limit the extent of their leakage. The positives of phytoremediation are clear. The downside is that it is a long-term process. An older study shows that Indian mustard, one of the most efficient hyperaccumulator plants, can remove only 10–20% of gold contained in the substrate in a single cycle. Moreover, the employment of phytoremediation is heavily dependent on the suitability of the local conditions for the plants.

The techniques are not new, but drones represent a relatively cheap and efficient way of applying them in remote and hard-to-access locations. ### Conclusion 

- **_„The biggest risk is not taking any risk. In a world that’s changing really quickly, the only strategy that is guaranteed to fail is not taking risks._** Mark Zuckerberg 

For a modern mining company, it is inevitably necessary to innovate to overcome growing operational, regulatory, and environmental pressures. Innovations adopted in various stages of the production cycle enable miners to deal with challenges such as declining ore grades, growing energy and labor costs, and increasing water scarcity. The list of the most prominent innovators includes the gold and silver mining heavyweights Agnico Eagle Mines, Barrick Mining, Equinox Gold, Newmont, and Pan American Silver. 

However, despite the advantages, there are also limitations to the adoption of innovations. There are usually high upfront capital needs, integration into existing mining operations may be technically difficult, and the workforce may have insufficient skills. Indeed, it is the human factor that may turn out to be the main obstacle in the innovation process. According to a study by Craig Tindale, the western mining companies are facing a shortage of skilled workers due to the demographic crisis. Mining engineers are becoming a scarce commodity. The young generation is not particularly interested in this kind of jobs, and the older generation is usually more conservative and less open to new technologies. This may slow down the adoption of some innovations, especially the more disruptive ones, quite a lot. 

**However, investors want more efficiency and higher profits, and the public wants safer mines with lower environmental impacts.** Governments usually want both. Therefore, the mining industry will keep on innovating. Ironically, while the demographic crisis may slow down the adoption of some innovations, we are nearing a point where the solution for this crisis may be innovation itself. According to a Streetwise Reports article, in the USA, more than half of the current mining workforce is expected to retire by 2029. Will they be replaced by modern technologies — self-driving haul trucks, autonomous drills, robotic ore-graders? Almost certainly. **The only open question is whether the technology will arrive fast enough to bridge the workforce gap.** 

- **_„You have to be prepared to make your innovation obsolete._** Peter Drucker

Advanced monitoring systems will take care of the safety and environmental sustainability of the operations. Exploration and grade control will fully rely on real-time data from drones, satellites, and fiber optic sensors.
