#### Key Takeaways 

- AI can improve the profits of gold mining operations by improving recoveries, reducing plant downtimes, and reducing fuel consumption. 

   - In a not-so-distant future, AI agents will be able to optimize the whole production chain and run the whole mine autonomously. 

- The AI-based technologies can improve the safety at the mine site and reduce the environmental impacts, significantly reducing operating and permitting risks. 

- The main hurdles in full AI adoption are the quality and quantity of required data, and the security/ethical question of how much the mining operation relies on the AI and where the human factor should be employed in the decision process.

### **_AI will not replace humans, but those who use AI will replace those who don’t._** 

Ginni Rometty 

**oldminers are facing a problem. The low-hanging fruits have been harvested and they must dig deeper;** they must dig in more remote locations; **G** and they must dig lower-grade ore. But this is where the knight on a white horse comes to the rescue. His name is AI (artificial Intelligence), and he helps the mining companies to fight inefficiencies and high costs. He can find more ore with less drilling; he can fine-tune the mine sequencing and ore processing; he can improve mine site safety and lower the environmental burden. It is quite possible that 20 years from now, he will have full or nearly full control over all the operations at the mine site. 

**_„The rise of powerful AI will either be the best or the worst thing ever to happen to humanity. We do not yet know which._** Stephen Hawking 

**One of the major issues the gold mining industry has to face is declining ore grades.** The miners are forced to mine and process ore that would have been considered unprofitable only several years ago. The low-grade ore certainly leads to higher unit production costs. The recent steep gold price growth has helped to cover this issue, as almost every gold mine should be profitable at gold prices over USD 4,000 per ounce, but this situation might not last forever. Therefore, the cost savings that may be generated by applying AI in various stages of the production cycle may be invaluable. Moreover, AI may also be useful when the mining activities stop and it is needed to reclaim the area and secure the safety of the old mine site.

#### How AI lifts exploration success rates 

**The whole story starts at the exploration level. It would be nice to just start digging anywhere and always find some gold.** Unfortunately, while this might work in some fairy tales, in real life, years of exploration, drilling, and various studies precede the mining itself. And the problem is that the biggest and richest deposits situated in the most accessible locations have already been discovered and extracted. Therefore, the explorers must search for new deposits in more remote areas, which means more technical difficulties, longer timelines, and higher costs. All three problems can be tackled by employing AI. AI can make the interpretation of exploration data much more efficient, as it is able to process and interpret huge volumes of data quickly. This leads to quicker and more successful identification of exploration targets. According to Discovery Alert, AI can reduce exploration drilling requirements by 25–30% and discovery time by 40%. 

- **_„The gold price has to rise to stimulate more exploration and make projects more economic, or we have to be smarter and more efficient at exploration._** Richard Schodde 

The AI and machine learning algorithms use geophysical, geochemical, and remote sensing data to identify patterns indicating the presence of a potentially economically interesting mineralization. For example, a company called Fleet Space uses nanosatellites and satellite-enabled geophysics, along with AI-powered analytics, to identify new exploration targets quickly, efficiently, and with notably limited environmental impacts. The company has even provided its services to Barrick Gold, in exploration of Reko Diq, one of the biggest undeveloped copper-gold deposits in the world. 

**Another AI exploration pioneer, Terra AI, developed an AI application for mineral** 

**and reservoir exploration.** Terra AI claims that its technology enables its customers to reduce drilling time and cost by 40% and increase initial under-cover targeting accuracy by 90%. It helps to prioritise drill targets and optimize the drill sequencing and number of drill rigs used in a campaign. As new drill results keep coming, the AI incorporates them and provides updated recommendations. 

- **_„We haven’t replaced the gold that we’ve mined since 2000. So, we are in a precarious position as an industry and the gold grade is now half of what it was in 2000._**

The AI-based technology turned out to be highly successful, as Opawica encountered gold in each hole drilled in its initial drill program.

#### How AI improves the efficiency of mining operations 

- **_„Tech-enabled transformation opens up a whole new horizon of possibilities. It equips companies with novel levers, tools, and opportunities to improve safety, productivity, and ways of working. The rewards will be far-reaching for companies that commit to it and approach it wisely._** McKinsey 

But the fight of the AI knight doesn’t end at the exploration stage. He has even more work to do directly in the mining process. He can tackle a broad spectrum of issues, by finding the best metallurgical solutions and the best and most efficient haulage routes, by predicting potential equipment failures, or by modelling the whole production process in the form of a digital twin. But the ultimate goal is to improve efficiency. 

##### Metallurgy 

**It is one thing to outline a gold deposit, possibly even a large one, another thing to mine the ore, and a completely different thing to extract the gold from the ore.** If the metallurgy of the deposit is too complex and the recoveries are too low, it may be simply unprofitable to build a mine. However, the AI can evaluate various aspects of the assay results, such as the gold grade; the grain size; the hardness of the ore; the clay, carbon, or sulfide content, etc.; and the metallurgical test results, and model expected grindability, leach kinetics, and recoveries for each ore block separately. This information can be used in mine planning to better optimise the mine sequencing and ore blending. 

The final outcome should be lower consumption of energy and reagents and higher gold recoveries. Even slightly improved recoveries can have a significant impact on the overall economics of the project. Let’s say that a mine processes 5mn tonnes of ore per annum, and the ore grade is 1 g/t gold. At a 75% recovery rate, the mine will produce approximately 120,600 ounces of gold. However, at an 80% recovery rate it will be 128,600 ounces of gold. At a gold price of USD 4,000 per ounce, the difference means USD 32mn that goes directly to the bottom line.

It is also responsible for a significant portion of operating costs. The AI can evaluate real-time data regarding traffic, weather conditions, terrain profile, etc., and find the best

##### Production chain 

**But AI can also help to optimize the whole production chain, not just its separate parts. This is where digital twin technologies come into play.** Digital twins enable the miner to simulate various scenarios of plant operations under changing conditions. The AI can provide tailor-made solutions for different ore types, which enables a more optimal energy, water, and chemicals consumption. This technique enables the mine operator to simulate various alternatives of process configuration without taking on real-world environmental or financial risks. The digital twin can be applied to the whole operation or to a specific process. An example is Newmont’s development of an AIbased metallurgical twin for its Lihir gold mine. It used Metso’s Geminex platform to optimize material flows and metallurgical processes and to better manage ore feed variability. Metso states that its Geminex twin technology helps to improve operational efficiency and recoveries, reduce risks, and improve sustainability. Moreover, it also helps with the training of operators. 

**Another highly promising innovation is the AI-based predictive maintenance system.** By combining machine learning with real-time data from sensors embedded directly in the machinery, it can predict equipment failures before they occur. Sensors collect data regarding the performance, energy consumption, or heat levels of a specific piece of equipment; and the AI uses historical data to identify anomalies and detect an increased probability of failure. The mine operator can subsequently adopt measures to prevent the failure or minimize its impacts. The mine operator can better schedule cash flows and secure funds for replacing expensive equipment, and more importantly, completely avoid excessive production disruptions. This helps not only to significantly reduce unplanned downtimes but also to minimize safety risks. 

What is important is that as the system learns from previous failures, its efficiency improves over time, delivering higher and higher benefits. **According to a study by McKinsey, AI-driven predictive maintenance systems can reduce equipment downtime by up to 50%. In a mid-sized gold mine, this may mean that the negative impacts on revenues and cash flows are smaller by several tens of millions of dollars, depending on the nature of the failed equipment.** A study by Deloitte states that improper maintenance strategies may reduce the productive capacity of a plant by 5–20%. This number further confirms the potential benefits of AI in predictive maintenance systems. The same study concludes that on average, predictive maintenance increases productivity by 25%, reduces breakdowns by 70%, and lowers maintenance costs by 25%.

**In tailings management, AI can help to better anticipate, and therefore also prevent, dam collapses and tailings leakages.** AI can identify or even predict micro-movements or deformation trends that a human eye can miss. Preventing dam failures means lower environmental impacts and higher safety. The miners collect data about temperature, rainfall, soil moisture, or seismic shifts. AI can process this data along with satellite images or data collected by drones more efficiently than humans and identify the most critical areas. It can even evaluate the data continuously, 24/7. AI can also help to optimize water management and optimize beach slopes, extending the operating life of the tailings. 

AI can also be used to improve the overall safety of mining operations in numerous ways. The AI can monitor live video streams to identify potentially dangerous shifts in topography, increases of water levels, or even the appearance of suspicious persons in restricted areas. The AI can also identify the presence of persons in dangerous areas (for example, too close to heavy machinery) and provide a warning to the person, as well as to the machine operator.

**An example of AI improving the security at the mine site is the Mine Safety DX system developed by Almonty Industries and Korea Telecom.** Smart tags equipped in bands and helmets monitor the biological functions of the miners, and AI evaluates the level of their fatigue, so that they can be warned or removed from hazardous situations. Moreover, the tags also track the position of the miners and the AI issues a warning if they enter a dangerous area. 

#### How AI manages post-mining obligations 

**The work of the AI knight is hard, and it doesn’t end even after mining operations have ceased.** This is the time when the mine site needs to be reclaimed. The task usually includes actions such as removing the equipment and infrastructure, filling the open pits or turning them into lakes, reshaping the hills and mounds to support natural drainage and reduce erosion, replacing the topsoil with fertile ground, planting native species of grass and trees, etc. The final aim is to make the old mine site virtually disappear and restore the rule of nature. The AI can help with these processes. It can process satellite images to identify signs of plant degradation, soil erosion, or abnormal water accumulation, so that these issues can be eliminated before they get out of control. It can also help in identifying specific pollution sources, in the case that elevated levels of pollution are encountered. 

**_„To build may have to be the slow and laborious task of years. To destroy can be the thoughtless act of a single day._** Winston Churchill

#### Conclusion 

**All of these practices are already in use, but often only separately. In the future, though, there is the potential to combine all these processes together under agentic AI. Agentic AI is the next generation of AI, which should be able to function autonomously and 24/7.** In other words, it is quite possible that in a not-so-distant future, agentic AI will be non-stop evaluating huge amounts of live data from every possible part of the mine production chain, and will be optimizing mining operations in real time. For example, if ore grades start to decline unexpectedly, or if the metallurgy in a block that is being mined is more complex than predicted, the AI agent may change the mine sequencing, in order to feed the mill with a more optimal ore blend, with the aim of minimizing the negative impacts on production volumes. Or if the predictive maintenance algorithm starts indicating an incipient failure of a piece of equipment, the AI agent may order a new piece of equipment and at the same time modify the production chain in order to ease the load on the endangered equipment to reduce the risk of its failure before the replacement arrives. 

To sum up, there is the potential that in 20 years, and possibly even sooner, the mine will have eyes thanks to ever more sensors, it will have ears – acoustic sensors –, and it will have its own brain – various AI applications and AI agents – that will conduct a highly efficient, safe, and environmentally friendly mining operation. The mine of the future probably won’t be full of human-like robots. It will be a highly efficient, fully automated operation, with only sporadic oversight by a human operator. 

- **_„With artificial intelligence we are summoning the demon._** Elon Musk 

**However, there are also obstacles that need to be overcome.** To fully implement AI agents into a mining operation may be expensive and technically challenging, especially when we are talking about an already operating mine. In the case of a mine that is to be newly built, the process may be easier and slightly less expensive. Another problem is that the mining companies will need teams of experts who are able to create and operate tailor-made AI agents for each mine separately. Especially in the early years, it is hard to expect a one-for-all solution that is adaptive enough to suit every mining operation well.
