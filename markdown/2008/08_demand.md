## **_Robust growth of demand in all three areas_** 

The demand side of the gold market has shown a clear upward trend in the past decade. Each one of the three most important areas, i.e. jewellery, industry, and investment, has recorded substantial gains. The tendency towards diversification among investors, the sharply rising demand from emerging markets, and the rising number of industrial uses of gold have been primarily responsible for this scenario. The price shock last year, when gold increased by more than 45%, did cause demand from the jewellery industry to shrink, but failed to result in a major slump of any sort. Besides, this decline was largely compensated by the substantial increase in investment demand. 

Another factor of the soaring gold demand is the high internal and external deficit of the USA that can only be sorted out through the adequate depreciation of the US dollar. Especially Asian nations, posting high current account surpluses and holding the majority of their foreign exchange reserves in dollar-denominated assets, have gradually increased the gold portion in their portfolios. 

## **_Exchange Traded Funds with substantially stepped-up volumes_** 

Physical demand over the past few years has mainly been driven by emerging economies in Asia, the Arabic region, and Russia, while speculative demand has benefited mainly from the success of exchange traded funds (ETFs). Physical gold held by ETFs increased by almost 50% between November 2006 and 2007. Institutional investors were the main buyers, whereas demand from retail investors edged up only by a bit.
