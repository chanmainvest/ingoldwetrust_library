# Special Report Gold 

Japan currently hold 23% of the USD 6.3 trillion of external debt of the USA. According to statements made by Hou Huimin from the China Gold Association, plans were to build reserves totalling 5,000 tonnes. China thus clearly implies that it does not trust the value preservation of the US dollar anymore. 

**Only 1.6 % gold coverage – China will continue to accumulate gold** 

1,000 tonnes are, however, not a lot when compared to the foreign exchange reserves of USD 1.95 trillion. Currently they account for about 1.6% of total reserves and are valued at USD 30bn (at a gold price of approximately USD 900/ounce). Although China has stepped up the number of tonnes, the share of gold in terms of total reserves has still declined. This means that the nation with the biggest foreign exchange reserves has also the lowest gold coverage ratio. The international average is 10%. We believe that this is a clear indicator that China will continue to increase its holdings. If China were to buy the 3,217 tonnes of the IMF, they would be looking at about USD 100bn (at USD 1,000 /ounce), which would still only account for 5.2% of total reserves (about USD 2 trillion).

## **Central Bank Gold Agreements** 

**Will the IMF sales be processed within CBGA3?** 

The International Monetary Fund currently holds more than 3,200 tonnes of gold. The Fund has repeatedly pointed out that it wishes to sell 403 tonnes (i.e. 13 million ounces) and fill its empty coffers with the proceeds. But due to the global economic crisis the IMF has gained importance again – it granted almost USD 50bn worth of emergency loans to Iceland, the Ukraine, Pakistan, and Romania. The interest income would make the announced gold sales obsolete.
