# Gold as Store of Value

Gold is fundamentally presented within the corpus as a reliable store of value, offering a tangible hedge against the inherent instability of fiat currencies. This concept posits that while paper money is inherently fragile, gold maintains its intrinsic worth across economic cycles, thereby serving as a critical protector of assets.

## How the argument evolved

The foundational argument regarding gold's role as a store of value was clearly established in **2015**. At this time, the report strongly contrasted gold against paper currency, asserting that "paper money couldn't possibly be regarded as a safe store of value." In this earlier framing, gold was championed as a bulwark against systemic financial erosion, specifically noting that "Gold stands as a protector of property rights."

By **2021**, the focus on this concept had matured, shifting from a purely defensive stance against inflation to an examination of global monetary shifts. While the core belief in gold's enduring quality remained, the context broadened to include international monetary policy. The report continued to emphasize the "history of gold as a long-term store of value," but now viewed through the lens of geopolitical and central banking activity. The narrative evolved to highlight that major global players, such as central bankers, continue to recognize this quality, viewing gold as "the ‘most reserve’ of reserve assets." This suggests a transition from simply defending against domestic currency weakness to recognizing gold's role in the architecture of international finance as currencies undergo processes of dollarization.
