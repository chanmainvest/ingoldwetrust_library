# Central Bank Gold Buying Trend

The trend of central banks becoming net buyers of gold signifies a profound structural shift within the global financial landscape, suggesting a sustained institutional commitment to gold as a store of value. This consistent buying activity signals a potential continuation of a bull market driven by institutional accumulation.

## How the argument evolved

The recognition of central banks as active participants in the gold market began to solidify around **2010**. At this juncture, the reports noted that "the central banks turned into net buyers for the first time in 20 years," framing this as a "structural shift" that heralded a "new phase of the bull market." This trend was quantified in **2011**, when it was noted that "The central bank sector joined the list of net buyers in 2010," with these entities "acquiring a total of 90 tonnes of gold last year."

The momentum behind this trend accelerated significantly by **2012**. The reports highlighted a "Renaissance Of Central Bank Buying," noting that "Official sector continues to be net buyer – highest volume since 1964." The scale of this accumulation was immense, as "Total purchases amounted to 455 tonnes," a volume unmatched since 1964.

Fast forward to **2024**, and the narrative continues with sustained activity. The reports describe this as "blistering central bank buying," with the latest data showing that "Central bank gold demand set a new record for a first quarter." Furthermore, the global sourcing of this demand was noted, as "Central bank demand was supported from the global East."
