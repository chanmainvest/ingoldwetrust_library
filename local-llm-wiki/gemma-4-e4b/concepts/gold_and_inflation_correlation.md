# Gold and Inflation Correlation

The relationship between gold's price and the rate of inflation is a central tenet of this corpus, positing a strong correlation, particularly when inflationary pressures become extreme. This dynamic suggests that gold serves as a critical hedge against currency debasement, making the correlation a key indicator for assessing portfolio resilience during periods of economic stress.

## How the argument evolved

The examination of this correlation began with a clear assertion of interdependence. In **2009**, the report established that "The development of the gold price is strongly correlated with inflation," noting that the monthly correlation coefficient between gold and the rate of inflation had reached 0.48. This early analysis highlighted historical patterns, observing that "When the rate of inflation was climbing to new highs in the USA and Europe at the end of the 1970s, so did the gold price."

By **2010**, the argument solidified and intensified, focusing specifically on the threshold effect of inflation. The narrative became more definitive, asserting that "The gold price correlates very strongly with inflation as soon as the latter hits extreme values." While the underlying statistical measure remained consistent—the monthly correlation coefficient of gold and the inflation rate was cited as 0.48—the emphasis shifted toward the criticality of these "extreme levels," suggesting that the protective function of gold becomes most pronounced when inflation is at its zenith.
