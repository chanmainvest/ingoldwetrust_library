# Election Year Gold Performance

The performance of gold during election years is a variable subject within the investment landscape, yet the reports suggest a discernible pattern tied to the political cycle. This concept examines how gold behaves immediately before and during presidential elections, suggesting that the timing of these political events can significantly influence annual returns. The significance of this pattern lies in its potential utility for investors seeking to time their entries and exits based on predictable market tendencies surrounding major political events.

## How the argument evolved

The initial observations regarding election year performance were made in **2012**. At that time, the analysis noted that gold's annual performance was not uniform, depending heavily on whether the year in question was an election year or the year immediately following one. The data suggested a clear distinction: the "average annual performance of only 1.3%" was contrasted against "positive performance that we tend to see in the year after the elections," where "The average performance of those years was 11.9%."

By **2015**, the focus shifted slightly, refining the observed trend to differentiate between the election year itself and the preceding year. The narrative became more nuanced, suggesting that while gold's performance during the actual election year tended to be subdued—where "gold often tends to be weak in election years (golden bar)"—the year leading up to the election showed a distinct advantage. Specifically, the analysis highlighted that "the years preceding election years exhibit a positive trend." This evolution shows a progression from simply noting year-over-year variance to pinpointing the specific directional bias associated with the immediate political context.
