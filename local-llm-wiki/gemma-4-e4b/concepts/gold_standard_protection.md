# Gold Standard Protection

The concept of Gold Standard Protection posits that adherence to a gold-backed monetary system provides a robust defense against inflationary pressures, a defense that the reports argue is historically proven. This protection is presented as a critical benchmark against which modern economic conditions should be measured, suggesting that the gold standard offers the "best protection against inflation" during periods of economic turbulence.

## How the argument evolved

The foundational argument regarding the gold standard's protective qualities was established in **2009**. This report examined historical data, asserting that "The gold standard is proven to offer the best protection against inflation." The evidence cited from the period spanning 1879 to 1914 demonstrated that "annual inflation from 1879 to 1914 was 0.2%," occurring alongside a low "volatility of only 2.2%."

The narrative continued and reinforced in **2010**. While the core assertion remained consistent—that the gold standard offers superior defense—the focus sharpened on the comparison between different economic regimes. The **2010** report reiterated that "The gold standard is proven to offer the best protection against inflation," specifically referencing the historical data set where "annual price inflation from 1879 to 1914 was 0.2%." The evolution across these two years shows a consistent reliance on the same historical data set (1879–1914) to substantiate the claim that the gold standard provides an unparalleled buffer against inflationary forces.
