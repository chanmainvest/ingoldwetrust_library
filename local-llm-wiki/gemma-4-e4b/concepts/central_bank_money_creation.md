# Central Bank Money Creation

Central Bank Money Creation refers to the process by which central monetary authorities introduce new, non-backed currency into the global financial system. This mechanism is critically significant because it represents a massive injection of liquidity and credit into the economy, fundamentally altering the monetary landscape and carrying profound implications for asset valuation.

## How the argument evolved

The reports demonstrate a clear trajectory in emphasizing the scale of this monetary phenomenon. In **2015**, the focus was on the anticipated magnitude of this activity, noting that "On a global level, central banks will create more irredeemable money this year than ever before." This framing established the concept as a growing, unprecedented trend.

By **2017**, the narrative had intensified, moving from anticipation to documented reality. The reports highlighted the sheer volume of this activity, noting that "large central banks created the equivalent of almost USD 1,000 bn" during the first quarter alone. The language became more emphatic, describing this influx as a "flood of central bank money" and even suggesting the creation of this liquidity was akin to "central bank money ex nihilo." This evolution shows a shift from merely predicting an increase in irredeemable money to quantifying the massive, ongoing injection of it into the system.
