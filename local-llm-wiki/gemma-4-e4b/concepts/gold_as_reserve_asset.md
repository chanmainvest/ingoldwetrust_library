# Gold as Reserve Asset

Gold is consistently framed across the reports as a universal reserve asset, trusted by diverse global actors—including central banks, investors, and private individuals—because it maintains its purchasing power over extended periods of time. This concept posits gold not merely as a speculative commodity, but as a foundational store of value necessary for navigating complex and often unstable global monetary systems.

## How the argument evolved

The initial framing of gold as a trusted store of value was established in **2019**, where it was defined as "the universal reserve asset to which central banks, investors, and private individuals" maintain its purchasing power over long periods of time. As geopolitical tensions began to heighten, the focus sharpened in **2020**, where the discussion around "gold as a reserve asset" became more urgent, positioning it as "the effective reference point" in various scenarios of global financial flux.

The narrative gained significant weight in **2022** as structural inflation became a dominant theme. Here, gold was argued to be a necessary safeguard against rising costs, with the assertion that "you need a reserve asset that has the ability to rise in price with the rise in the prices of energy," making it clear that "That is why you need a reserve asset like gold."

By **2023**, the scope of gold's role broadened beyond just inflation hedging; it became recognized as integral to emerging monetary structures. The reports noted the "growing importance of gold as a reserve" within "the emerging tandem of commodities, gold and oil," while also highlighting existing concentrations of wealth, such as the fact that "Europe is still sitting on the world’s largest gold reserves."

The most recent iteration in **2024** solidified gold's role as a stabilizing, neutral force in a multipolar world. The concept matured to describe gold as "Gold serving as a neutral reserve asset," suggesting that "gold can still replace USTs as the de facto reserve asset," making it clear that "the alternative is gold."
