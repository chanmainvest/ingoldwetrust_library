# Seasonal Market Patterns

Seasonal Market Patterns refer to recurring, predictable trends observed in the performance of gold and related assets across specific timeframes, particularly those tied to annual cycles or election years. These patterns are significant because they suggest that market timing, informed by historical cyclical behavior, can offer predictive insights into asset movement.

## How the argument evolved

The examination of seasonal patterns in the reports shows a clear evolution in the specificity of the observed trends between the two years. In **2024**, the focus was on establishing the existence of these predictable cycles, noting that certain timeframes, such as election years, exhibit discernible seasonal trends for both gold and silver. The **2024** analysis highlighted a specific pattern where "a seasonally weaker phase begins in July and only ends in November," and also noted that "a seasonal downward trend can also be seen in silver from August to the end of November."

By **2026**, the analysis became more granular, detailing specific rally periods and directional strengths tied to the election cycle. The **2026** report emphasized a "spectacular spring rally (February through mid-April) to a seasonal high." Furthermore, the focus broadened to include currency dynamics, noting "The seasonal weakness of the dollar starting in mid-year," while simultaneously confirming the cyclical nature of the metal itself, observing the "seasonal strength of gold starting in July/August." Thus, the narrative moved from identifying general seasonal weakness in the latter half of the year to mapping out distinct, high-magnitude rally periods followed by predictable directional shifts.
