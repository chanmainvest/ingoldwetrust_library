# Asset Price Inflation Drivers

Asset price inflation, as analyzed within the *In Gold We Trust* corpus, describes the phenomenon where the value of non-liquid or speculative assets rises significantly faster than general consumer prices. This process is critically important because it signals underlying shifts in monetary conditions and economic structure, determining whether inflation manifests broadly across the economy or remains concentrated in specific asset classes.

## How the argument evolved

The initial framing of asset price inflation in **2014** focused heavily on the role of commodity markets as the primary transmission mechanism. The report established that the increase in asset prices was significantly influenced by rising commodity prices, noting that "prices for antiques, luxury goods, expensive wines, collector's cars and of course real estate and stocks have increased substantially." Furthermore, the document stressed that for "the transmission of monetary inflation into pronounced consumer price inflation, rising commodity prices are of decisive importance," suggesting a direct link between physical commodity appreciation and broader price movements.

By **2016**, the focus of the analysis had matured, shifting the primary driver away from immediate commodity spikes toward the structural relationship between monetary policy and asset valuation. The argument crystallized around the disparity between the growth of the money supply and the rate of consumer price increases. Specifically, the report posited that "the difference between strong money supply growth and relatively low consumer price inflation has primarily become manifest in the form of asset price inflation." This perspective quantified the phenomenon, noting that "asset price inflation amounted to 7.8% last year," thereby framing the issue less as a commodity ripple effect and more as a function of monetary expansion outpacing consumer price realization.
