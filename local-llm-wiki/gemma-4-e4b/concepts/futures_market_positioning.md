# Futures Market Positioning

Futures market positioning serves as a critical barometer for assessing market sentiment regarding gold's near-term trajectory. By analyzing the net positions held by major participants, one can gauge the degree of conviction—or lack thereof—among commercial hedgers and professional traders regarding whether gold is poised for upward continuation or faces downside risks. The evolution of this analysis across the reports demonstrates a shift in the dominant signal being read from the derivatives landscape.

## How the argument evolved

The examination of futures positioning reveals a notable shift in the prevailing market narrative between the two reports. In **2014**, the focus was on a clear trend reversal among commercial hedgers. The evidence suggested that the market was nearing a turning point, as "Commercials have sharply reduced their gross short positions," indicating a move away from bearish hedging and toward anticipation of higher gold prices. This suggested a consensus among major players that the bottoming phase was nearly complete.

However, the perspective shifted significantly by **2020**. The narrative moved away from a near-term bottoming signal and instead highlighted a sustained bearish bias within the derivatives market. The report noted that "the futures market, on the other hand, has been consistently sending out bearish signals for over a year now." This sustained negativity was underpinned by the activity of professional participants; specifically, "professional market participants such as bullion banks and market makers have cumulatively sold 278,533 futures contracts short." This contrasts sharply with the 2014 signal, suggesting that while the market may have been digesting a reversal in 2014, by 2020, the dominant signal was one of persistent selling pressure from institutional actors.
