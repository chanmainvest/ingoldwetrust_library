# Digital Currency Development

The development of digital currencies, particularly Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs), represents a significant, ongoing debate within the broader context of global monetary shifts. This concept is viewed not merely as a technological advancement but as a critical geopolitical and economic battleground, influencing the trajectory of global finance and the potential for dollarization.

## How the argument evolved

The discussion surrounding digital currencies began to crystallize around **2020** with an acknowledgment of the ongoing "debate on 'digital central bank currencies' (CBDC)." At this juncture, attention was heavily focused on the actions of major state actors, specifically noting those "concerned about the Chinese government’s moves to create a digital yuan." This early framing positioned the development of these digital assets as a key element in the larger process of de-dollarization.

By **2021**, the narrative had sharpened, moving from mere observation to a clear assessment of strategic intent. The focus remained firmly on China's advancements, which were now understood less as an experiment and more as a deliberate strategic deployment. The report noted that China was actively "advancing its digital currency," which was increasingly interpreted as a geopolitical tool. The tone became more assertive, suggesting that "China also has a digital currency in its quiver," and that the digital renminbi itself could be viewed as "another weapon" in international economic contests. This evolution shows a clear progression from recognizing the existence of the debate in **2020** to analyzing the weaponization of the technology by major powers in **2021**.
