# Gold as Monetary Asset

Gold serves a dual function within financial markets, operating simultaneously as a tangible commodity and a store of value. However, the reports within the *In Gold We Trust* corpus maintain a specific focus, treating gold primarily through the lens of its role as a monetary asset. This perspective is crucial because it frames gold's value proposition not merely in terms of physical scarcity, but in its capacity to preserve purchasing power against systemic economic pressures.

## How the argument evolved

The treatment of gold's function as a monetary asset underwent a subtle but discernible shift across the reports. In **2007**, the corpus acknowledged the inherent duality of the metal, noting that "gold is used as a monetary asset and commodity," while also recognizing that "gold is a commodity as well as a monetary asset." This early framing suggests an acceptance of gold's multifaceted nature in the market.

By **2015**, the narrative became more focused and prescriptive. The report signaled a deliberate narrowing of scope, stating clearly that "we analyze gold primarily as a monetary asset and not as a commodity." This evolution suggests a maturation of the investment thesis, moving away from a purely descriptive view toward a more targeted analytical lens. The **2015** document aimed to "analyze the gold sector in a holistic manner," but this holistic view was anchored by the primacy of its monetary function.
