# Inflationary Bias in Politics

The concept of inflationary bias in politics describes a systemic tendency within political structures to favor policies that expand the money supply. This bias is significant because it suggests that political action, particularly during times of economic uncertainty, inherently drives monetary expansion, which has profound implications for asset valuation and long-term economic stability.

## How the argument evolved

The recognition of this inflationary tendency was present in the reports across different years, though the framing of the political mechanism remained consistent. In **2012**, the reports established that political systems possess an inflationary bias, noting that "stimulus measures and campaign pledges... constitute the basic requirements for the expansion of the money supply." This early framing highlighted how political action directly translates into monetary expansion.

By **2020**, the core argument remained intact, yet the language solidified around the prerequisite nature of political action. The reports continued to assert that political systems exhibit this inflationary bias, confirming that "stimuli and election promises are the basic prerequisite for money supply expansion." The continuity of this observation was underscored by the recognition that "Bernholz also recognizes an inflationary bias in the current political system," mirroring earlier acknowledgments of this systemic issue. The underlying mechanism—that political stimuli necessitate money supply expansion—remained the central tenet across both reporting periods.
