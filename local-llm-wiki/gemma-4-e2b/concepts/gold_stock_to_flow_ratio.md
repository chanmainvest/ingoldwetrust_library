# Gold Stock-to-Flow Ratio

The Gold Stock-to-Flow Ratio is a critical metric used to assess the fundamental supply dynamics of gold, determining its relationship to market expectations regarding scarcity and inflation. This ratio measures the existing supply of gold relative to the flow of new supply, providing insight into whether gold is currently abundant or scarce, which in turn signals potential implications for its price and monetary role.

## How the argument evolved

The understanding of the Gold Stock-to-Flow Ratio evolved across the three reports, moving from a comparative commodity analysis to a statement about market abundance, and finally to an interpretation of scarcity signals. In **2013**, the initial analysis focused on comparing gold's stock-to-flow ratio against other commodities. The report established that gold's ratio is significantly higher than that of wheat, leading to the conclusion that "gold should never trade in backwardation" because "gold's stock-to-flow ratio is far higher than that of wheat."

The perspective shifted significantly by **2022**, where the focus moved inward to the characteristics of gold itself. This analysis determined that gold possesses a high stock-to-flow ratio, which directly implies abundance relative to market participants. The report asserted that "Gold has a greater stock to flow than almost any other commodity – perhaps with the exception of water." Furthermore, the high ratio was interpreted as evidence of massive existing supply, stating, "the high stock-to-flow ratio and great abundance of gold mean that there is a massive amount of it relative to any one market participant."

Finally, in **2025**, the interpretation of the ratio was framed in terms of scarcity and macroeconomic implications. The conclusion drawn was that a high stock-to-flow ratio serves as a signal of scarcity, which in turn suggests lower inflationary pressure. The report explicitly stated, "A high ratio, typical for gold or Bitcoin, indicates scarcity and signals lower inflationary pressure."
