# Federal Reserve Monetary Policy

The Federal Reserve's monetary policy is a central mechanism through which the U.S. central bank attempts to manage the economy by influencing the money supply and interest rates. This policy is significant because its actions directly impact inflation, employment levels, and the perceived stability of the U.S. dollar, which in turn affects the role of gold as a monetary asset.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of the Federal Reserve's monetary policy evolved across the two reports, shifting from a focus on standard economic goals to an examination of unconventional, crisis-driven actions. In the **2014** report, the perspective centered on the Fed's traditional mandate. The argument established that "The goal of the Fed's monetary policy was to stimulate demand for goods and services and keep unemployment low." This framing sets the baseline understanding of the policy as a tool for demand stimulation and employment management.

The narrative then progressed to the **2020** analysis, which examined the policy in the context of extraordinary circumstances. The focus moved beyond routine stimulus to the scale and motivation behind the actions taken during the Covid-19 crisis. This chapter highlighted a significant shift in the Fed's operational approach, noting that "the Federal Reserve launched the largest money printing program ever – officially to combat the consequences of the Covid-19 crisis." This observation suggests a departure from standard policy to massive quantitative easing. Furthermore, the conclusion of the **2020** chapter framed the implications of this policy, suggesting a potential consequence: "It looks as if Washington has decided in favor of option 2: the devaluation of the US dollar." Thus, the evolution moves from defining the policy's standard purpose to analyzing its extreme application during a major crisis and its potential long-term implications for the dollar's monetary status.
