# Inflation Volatility Regime

The concept of the Inflation Volatility Regime describes a shift in the macroeconomic environment where the frequency and magnitude of inflation signals change, moving from a stable pattern to one characterized by heightened uncertainty. This volatility is significant because it directly impacts the perceived risk associated with fiat currencies and, consequently, the role of gold as a store of value.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of inflation volatility has evolved to identify a distinct shift in the inflation landscape. In **2023**, the observation focused on the changing nature of the inflation data itself. The report noted that "the inflation indicator shows an increased number of signal changes, indicating a new regime of higher inflation volatility." Furthermore, it was noted that "our inflation indicator has shown an above-average number of signal changes in the past 2 years." This evidence points toward the recognition that the underlying structure of inflation signaling is changing.

This observation was further confirmed and framed in **2024**. The analysis moved beyond just the frequency of signals to characterize the resulting economic state. The report concluded that "the economy has entered a new regime characterized by greater inflation volatility and a structurally higher inflation rate." This suggests that the increased volatility is not merely a temporary fluctuation but is embedded in the economic structure, leading to a persistently higher level of inflation uncertainty.
