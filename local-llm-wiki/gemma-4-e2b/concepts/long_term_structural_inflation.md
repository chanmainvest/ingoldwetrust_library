# Long term structural inflation

Long-term structural inflation is defined by the interplay of deep, long-term economic and geopolitical forces that create persistent inflationary pressures independent of short-term monetary policy. This concept posits that inflation is not merely a function of money supply but is fundamentally driven by structural shifts in demographics and geopolitical realignment, making gold a necessary hedge against these enduring economic realities.

## How the argument evolved

The exploration of long-term structural inflation began in **2024** with an initial focus on the foundational drivers. The analysis established that these dynamics are rooted in demographic change and geopolitical bloc formation. Specifically, the concept highlights "Long term structural inflationary dynamics - Demographic change and the associated shortage of skilled workers and general labor shortage." This suggests that the underlying inflationary pressure stems from a structural shortage of necessary labor, which is a long-term feature rather than a temporary economic blip.

The argument evolved in **2025** by expanding the scope to include the role of fiscal policy within this structural framework. The analysis confirmed that the long-term dynamics encompass demographic change, geopolitical bloc formation, and fiscal policy. The core thesis remains centered on the structural nature of inflation, as evidenced by the reiteration of "Long term structural inflationary dynamics." The specific mechanisms identified include the demographic component—the "Demographic change and the associated shortage of skilled workers and general labor shortage"—and the geopolitical component, which involves "Geopolitical bloc formation." By integrating fiscal policy into this narrative, the report solidifies the view that these structural pressures are not isolated but interact with policy responses, reinforcing the thesis that these are enduring forces shaping the inflationary environment.
