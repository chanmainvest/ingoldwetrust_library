# US dollar as reserve currency

The concept of the US dollar functioning as the world's reserve currency is central to understanding global financial architecture and the future of international monetary systems. This page synthesizes the evolving arguments across recent In Gold We Trust reports regarding the sustainability, structure, and future role of the dollar in global finance.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of the dollar's reserve status has evolved over the period covered by these reports, moving from questioning its long-term viability to asserting its current resilience during periods of stress. In **2019**, the initial focus was on the sustainability of this status, with the reports explicitly questioning its permanence. The narrative suggested that "the US dollar’s status as the world’s reserve currency will not be sustainable forever," framing the idea that "the dollar’s days as the world’s most important currency are numbered."

This perspective shifted in **2023** as the analysis focused on the structural nature of the dollar's role. The argument established a unique position for the dollar, noting that "the US dollar has been the first and only world reserve currency in history without the backing of a precious metal." This established the dollar's status as a purely fiat-based system, distinct from commodity-backed reserves.

The most recent treatment in **2024** presented a counter-narrative regarding the dollar's performance during crises. While acknowledging the structural facts, the conclusion was that the dollar remains the preferred asset during volatility. The argument posits that "the U.S. dollar is the world’s reserve currency and is likely to remain so—in times of global stress, the world runs to the dollar, not away from it." This suggests a conclusion that the dollar's role is reinforced by its utility during periods of global uncertainty.
