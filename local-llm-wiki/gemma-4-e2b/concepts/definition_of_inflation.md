# Definition of Inflation

Inflation is fundamentally understood as the rate of increase in consumer prices, but the analysis within the 'In Gold We Trust' corpus expands this definition significantly to encompass monetary dynamics. The reports move beyond a narrow focus on consumer spending to establish inflation as a mechanism tied directly to the expansion of the money supply, which has broader implications for asset valuation.

## How the argument evolved

The initial treatment of inflation, as seen in **2014**, established the baseline understanding: "The term inflation is usually employed as synonymous with the rate of increase in consumer prices." This provided a standard, consumer-centric definition. However, the subsequent analysis in **2015** quickly recognized the limitations of this view. While acknowledging that "the term inflation is usually employed as a synonym for the rate at which consumer prices increase," the authors stressed the need for precision, arguing that "In order to discuss the phenomenon of monetary debasement with precision, it is crucial to differentiate between the terms inflation and rising prices (or “price inflation”)." This suggested that focusing solely on consumer prices misses the underlying cause.

The evolution culminated in a more comprehensive, monetary definition presented in **2018**. In this later perspective, inflation is defined as "monetary inflation, which is the expansion of the money supply." This shift is crucial because it broadens the scope of the phenomenon; the reports assert that "inflation originally means “monetary inflation”, i.e. the expansion of the money supply." Furthermore, this monetary view is not confined to the consumer sphere, as the analysis notes that "monetary inflation does not only affect consumer prices but also asset prices." Thus, the concept evolves from a simple measure of purchasing power to a systemic measure of monetary expansion affecting all forms of pricing.
