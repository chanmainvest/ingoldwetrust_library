# Money supply growth rate

The analysis of the money supply growth rate is central to understanding the relationship between fiat currency expansion and the perceived value of gold. This concept examines the divergence between the rate at which the money supply expands and other economic metrics, such as GDP or gold reserves, to assess potential inflationary pressures and the resulting implications for gold's role as a store of value.

## How the argument evolved

The treatment of money supply growth in the corpus began by establishing a baseline observation regarding the expansion of the money supply relative to economic growth. In the **2009** report, the focus was on the sheer magnitude of the growth, noting that "the money supply has been growing for years by more than GDP growth would have justified." This observation sets the stage by highlighting a structural imbalance where monetary expansion outpaces standard economic growth measures.

The argument evolved in the **2012** report by expanding this comparison to include gold reserves. While the **2009** chapter focused on the money supply versus GDP, the **2012** introduction introduced a comparative measure against gold. Specifically, the analysis shifted to the rate of growth of the money supply aggregates, concluding that "the money supply aggregates grow at a substantially faster rate" than gold reserves. This comparison suggests that the speed of monetary expansion is not only disproportionate to GDP but also significantly faster than the accumulation of gold reserves, implying a growing disconnect between the monetary system and traditional asset stores of value.
