# De-dollarization concept

The concept of de-dollarization explores the structural shift away from the US dollar's hegemony in the global financial system, positing that this transition is driven by geopolitical resistance and the adoption of alternative currencies. This concept is significant as it challenges the established monetary order and highlights the growing skepticism toward the dollar's universal acceptance, suggesting a future where a multipolar currency landscape emerges.

## How the argument evolved

The exploration of de-dollarization within the corpus began in **2018** with a specific focus on the mechanism of transition. The analysis centered on the idea of de-dollarization as a process involving the shift from the dollar to other major currencies. The report framed this as "De-Dollarization – From the Dollar to Gold, Via the Yuan and the Euro," detailing the pathway of this transition through alternative currencies such as the yuan and the euro.

The perspective evolved in **2023** to define the phenomenon more broadly as a geopolitical challenge to the dollar's dominance. The focus moved from the mechanics of currency substitution to the political resistance underpinning the shift. In this later analysis, de-dollarization was defined as "the resistance of nations to the US dollar's dominant position in the financial system." This resistance is explicitly linked to specific geopolitical actors, identifying the resistors as "countries like China, Russia, the rest of the BRICS, parts of Europe and the Middle East." This framing solidified the concept as a reflection of a geopolitical struggle against the current financial structure.
