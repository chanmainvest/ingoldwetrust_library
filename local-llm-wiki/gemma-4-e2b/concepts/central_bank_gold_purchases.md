# Central bank gold purchases

The concept of central bank gold purchases signifies a growing trend where sovereign central banks are actively accumulating gold, suggesting a fundamental shift in monetary policy and a move away from traditional fiat currencies like the dollar. This accumulation is significant as it signals a direct challenge to the dominance of the dollar system and the underlying assumptions of debt-based currency reserves.

## How the argument evolved

The narrative surrounding central bank gold purchases has evolved from observing a recent trend to recognizing it as a massive, accelerated shift driven by geopolitical events. In **2019**, the analysis focused on the observation that central banks have recently bought more gold than in a long period, which serves as a clear signal: "central banks have recently bought more gold within one year than they have in any year since 1971." This observation was interpreted as a clear indication that "central banks are ditching the dollar for gold."

The perspective shifted significantly by **2024**, where the argument expanded to incorporate external geopolitical pressures. The analysis now posits that the acceleration of these purchases is not merely a cyclical movement but is driven by external factors, specifically sanctions. In this later view, the argument is that "international central banks have massively accelerated their gold purchases due to sanctions." This acceleration is framed as a demonstration of the fragility of existing financial structures, as the freezing of Russian currency reserves "impressively demonstrated to the world that debt-based currency reserves are ultimately just a promise."
