# Gold Price Seasonality

The concept of Gold Price Seasonality posits that the price of gold is not purely driven by macroeconomic fundamentals but is significantly influenced by cyclical, time-based events, particularly cultural or festival-related patterns. This seasonality suggests that predictable temporal factors can cause the price to exhibit specific directional biases, which investors must account for when analyzing gold market movements.

## How the argument evolved

The recognition of gold price seasonality emerged across the analyzed reports, evolving from specific cultural observations to a more defined temporal expectation. In **2008**, the initial analysis identified a clear link between the gold price and specific cultural events, noting the "pronounced seasonality of the price" driven by factors such as the "so-called wedding season as well as the Diwali Festival in India."

This observation was further refined in **2011**, where the seasonality was explicitly linked to these festivals, leading to a specific expectation regarding price timing. The analysis concluded that "Due to the strong seasonality we expect the gold price to move sideways until the end of July."

The concept solidified in **2012**, where the conclusion was a direct prediction based on the observed pattern: "Due to the strong seasonality we believe that the gold price should be moving sideways until the end of July." This was supported by evidence showing that "The gold price has increased in September in 65% of the cases."

The view was then updated in **2024**, shifting the focus to specific monthly patterns. The analysis noted that **June** is traditionally a weak month for the gold price, and a seasonal component is not fully realized until later in the year, stating that "June in particular is traditionally an extremely weak month for the gold price." Furthermore, the seasonal component is not really well-disposed towards the gold market until mid-December. A "dark red seasonal component awaits the gold bulls in June."
