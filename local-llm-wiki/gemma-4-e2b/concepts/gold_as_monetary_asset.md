# Gold as Monetary Asset

This concept posits that gold's primary function in the financial system is not merely as a physical commodity, but fundamentally as a monetary asset. This analysis examines the evolution of gold's role, moving from a traditional currency to a commodity, and concludes that its status as a reserve asset remains central to global monetary policy.

## How the argument evolved

The treatment of gold as a monetary asset in the corpus evolves across the three reports, shifting from a recognition of its dual nature to a firm assertion of its monetary primacy. In the **2007** Full Report, the initial perspective frames gold as having transitioned from a currency to a commodity, stating plainly that "Gold is used as a monetary asset and commodity" and noting that "gold lost its significance as a currency and developed into a commodity." This sets the stage by acknowledging the historical shift.

The **2015** Introduction takes this framework further by explicitly defining the scope of analysis. The report states, "we analyze gold primarily as a monetary asset and not as a commodity," indicating a deliberate focus on the monetary function over the commodity aspect. This approach necessitates a broader view, as the authors assert, "we will therefore once again take a sober look at the big picture and analyze the gold sector in a holistic manner."

The culmination of this evolution is found in the **2017** chapter, where the argument solidifies the conclusion regarding its monetary status. The analysis moves beyond mere observation to a definitive claim: "we believe that gold must be regarded as a monetary asset." This conclusion is supported by the assertion that "gold still remains the primary monetary reserve asset of all major central banks." This final stance establishes gold's enduring role as a foundational element of global monetary reserves.
