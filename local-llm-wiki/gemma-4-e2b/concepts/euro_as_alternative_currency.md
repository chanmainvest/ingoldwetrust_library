# Euro as Alternative Currency

The concept of the Euro as an alternative currency posits that the Euro possesses the potential to serve as a significant alternative to the US Dollar in various global markets, particularly energy and trade settlements. This analysis explores the historical and geopolitical factors that have positioned the Euro as a viable, albeit perhaps underappreciated, alternative asset.

## How the argument evolved

The perspective on the Euro's alternative status has evolved across the reports, moving from a focus on market perception to recognizing its role in specific geopolitical trade arrangements. In **2019**, the argument centered on the need for the Euro to gain recognition in key commodity markets. Specifically, the analysis suggested that "the euro should finally be perceived as a serious alternative to the US dollar in (energy) markets, by the media, politicians, and voters." This initial framing focused on the necessary steps for the Euro to achieve mainstream acceptance in energy-related spheres.

The narrative developed further in **2023**, shifting the focus from mere perception to concrete, high-level geopolitical utility. By **2023**, the concept was elevated to include its role in major international trade deals. The argument now suggests that the Euro was not just a potential alternative but was actively selected by major global powers for transactional purposes. Evidence cited in this later analysis indicates that "the euro was chosen by China and Russia in early 2022 as the settlement currency for their huge gas deal." Furthermore, the 2023 view emphasized the currency's current standing relative to the dollar, noting that "the euro is the only currency that has managed to achieve significant reserve status next to the US dollar so far." This progression shows a shift from advocating for market acceptance to confirming its current, albeit niche, status in high-stakes international finance.
