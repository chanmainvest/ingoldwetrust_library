# Inflation-Sensitive Asset Allocation

This concept centers on the strategic necessity of adjusting portfolio allocations based on prevailing inflationary environments. It posits that assets sensitive to inflation, such as commodities and energy, offer the most favorable positioning when inflation is present, making the identification and management of these sensitivities crucial for capital preservation and growth.

## How the argument evolved

The discussion around inflation-sensitive asset allocation emerged from distinct but complementary perspectives across the reports. In **2016**, the focus was on the direct relationship between inflation signals and specific asset classes. The analysis established that the decision to shift capital into or out of these sensitive areas is directly tied to the inflation signal. Specifically, the report noted that "we shift allocations into or out of inflation-sensitive assets – primarily mining stocks, commodities and energy stocks." This established the mechanism: inflation serves as the primary trigger for reallocating capital into these specific commodity-linked sectors.

The perspective evolved further in **2018**, moving from identifying the assets to confirming their role as the optimal positioning strategy. The **2018** analysis solidified the view that when inflation is present, the strategy is clear: "allocation to inflation-sensitive asset classes is the best positioning for such phases." This confirms the thesis that these asset classes are superior during inflationary periods. Furthermore, the report linked this positioning to the performance of gold, noting that "The performance of gold has also been largely positive in these periods." Thus, the evolution moved from defining *what* to allocate to *why* it is the best choice, linking the inflation signal directly to favorable outcomes in both commodity-linked assets and gold.
