# Asset Price Inflation

Asset price inflation represents the initial manifestation of an inflationary process, occurring before broader consumer price increases are fully realized. This concept is significant because it identifies the earliest measurable signal of monetary expansion impacting the financial system, suggesting that asset prices serve as the primary conduit through which inflation first transmits its effects.

## How the argument evolved

The understanding of asset price inflation evolved across the five reports, establishing a clear sequence of price movements during inflationary periods. In **2014**, the concept was introduced as the starting point, asserting that "the prices of capital goods (= asset price inflation) are the first to increase in an inflationary process." This established asset price inflation as the leading indicator.

This sequence was reinforced and elaborated upon in **2015**, which specified the order of price increases. The report noted that "Asset price inflation occurs first, with capital goods prices rising before consumer prices rise." It explicitly stated, "In the course of the inflationary process, the prices of capital goods (=asset price inflation) thus rise first, and consumer prices begin to rise (=consumer price inflation) only later." This was further emphasized by the observation that "the asset price inflation that is currently underway is impossible to overlook."

The connection between monetary expansion and asset prices was solidified in **2016**. The analysis confirmed that "the expansion of the money supply affects asset prices and consumer prices," noting that "Recent decades were characterized by asset prices benefiting from a strong monetary tailwind." This provided the mechanism: the expansion of the money supply drives asset prices, which subsequently influence consumer prices.

The role of asset prices in the inflation dynamic was further clarified in **2018**. The report linked monetary inflation directly to asset valuation, stating that "monetary inflation does not only affect consumer prices but also asset prices." It further detailed the mechanism, noting that "the inflation process via so-called “asset purchasing programs” would first shift the prices of assets."

Finally, in **2020**, the concept was synthesized into a definitive sequence. The report concluded that the current dynamic corresponds to the ABCT, where "asset prices rise first before consumer price inflation sets in." This final synthesis confirms the progression: "In the course of the inflationary process, asset prices rise first (= asset price inflation) and only then does consumer price inflation (= inflation) set in."
