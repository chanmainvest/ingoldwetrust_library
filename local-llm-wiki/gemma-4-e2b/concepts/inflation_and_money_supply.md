# Inflation and Money Supply

This concept examines the fundamental relationship between inflation and the growth of the money supply, positing that sustained inflation is intrinsically linked to an expansion of the money supply. The significance lies in understanding that monetary expansion is a primary driver of inflationary pressures, suggesting that monetary policy and the growth of the money supply are central to the economic environment being analyzed.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of inflation and money supply begins with a foundational statement regarding the necessary condition for inflation. In the **2013** report, the argument establishes a direct causal link: "there is no record in the economic history of the whole world, anywhere or at any time, of a serious and prolonged inflation which has not been accompanied and made possible, if not directly caused, by a large increase in the quantity of money." This sets the baseline premise that money supply growth is a prerequisite for serious inflation.

The perspective evolves in the **2023** report, shifting focus to the contemporary context, specifically the United States. While maintaining the core thesis, the discussion connects the mechanism of inflation directly to monetary expansion within a political framework. The **2023** chapter discusses inflation resulting from "money printing and the growth of the money supply in the US." Furthermore, the analysis links this monetary phenomenon to political influence, noting that "the current political and economic crisis also has to do with the fact that the monetary system has been fully coopted by politics." Quantitatively, the report provides a specific measure of this growth, stating that "the money supply in the United States has grown by more than 38 percent." This evolution moves from a general historical observation about the necessity of money supply growth for inflation to a specific, contemporary diagnosis of how the growth of the money supply in the US is fueling current economic and political crises.
