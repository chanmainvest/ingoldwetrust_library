# Dow/Gold Ratio Analysis

The Dow/Gold ratio serves as a crucial metric for assessing the relative valuation between equity markets and gold, providing insight into the perceived relationship between these two asset classes. This ratio is significant because it can signal potential market turning points, such as when equity valuations are stretched or when gold is relatively undervalued compared to the stock market.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of the Dow/Gold ratio evolved across the two reports, shifting from a purely technical valuation perspective in **2012** to a more forward-looking, trend-based interpretation in **2020**. In the **2012** analysis, the focus was on the current level of the ratio relative to historical norms. Specifically, the report noted that "At 7.8x, the ratio is currently above the long-term median of 5.8x," which led to the conclusion that "gold is still relatively inexpensive in comparison with the Dow Jones index." This established the ratio as a measure of relative price, where a higher ratio implies gold is relatively cheap.

The perspective shifted in the **2020** introduction, broadening the significance of the ratio beyond a static valuation to a predictive tool for market peaks. In this later view, the ratio is interpreted as a signal regarding equity performance. The report stated that "the Dow/gold ratio indicates that share prices have peaked, suggesting a downward trend for gold-backed shares." Furthermore, the analysis highlighted the relationship between the ratio and gold price movements, noting that "the Dow/gold ratio is also of great interest to us, as an unsustainable equity rally is sometimes sniffed out by a strongly rising gold price." This suggests that a rising ratio, or the relationship between the two, can signal an unsustainable equity rally, which is often correlated with a rising gold price. The **2020** chapter concluded that "Since then, shares measured in gold have been on a clear downward trend."
