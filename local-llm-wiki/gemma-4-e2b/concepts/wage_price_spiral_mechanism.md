# Wage-Price Spiral Mechanism

The wage-price spiral mechanism describes the cyclical process where rising wages and increasing prices feed into one another, creating a self-fulfilling inflationary cycle. This mechanism is significant because it links monetary expansion directly to inflationary outcomes, suggesting that inflation is not merely an external event but a manifestation of internal economic adjustments.

## How the argument evolved

The conceptualization of the wage-price spiral evolved across the analyzed reports, moving from a definition of the process to an identification of its role within the inflation cycle. In **2014**, the mechanism was initially defined as the process of adjustment between wage increases and rising prices. The report described it as "The wage-price spiral set into motion by high inflation (which is a process of adjustment between wage increases and rising prices on the part of households and corporations)."

The understanding deepened in **2022** by linking the spiral directly to monetary expansion. This analysis suggested that a price-wage spiral is contingent upon monetary conditions. Specifically, it was noted that "a price-wage spiral as a typical second-round effect is only possible if the money supply has been or is being significantly expanded." Furthermore, the report connected this to central bank actions, noting that "with their ultra-expansive monetary policy, central banks have laid the foundation for the erosion of the purchasing power of money and thus provided fodder for the looming price-wage spiral."

By **2025**, the focus shifted to the relationship between the spiral and inflation itself. The mechanism was re-characterized not as a cause, but as a result. The analysis concluded that "the wage-price spiral is the manifestation of inflation, not its cause."
