# Stagflation Definition

Stagflation is fundamentally defined as an adverse economic condition characterized by the simultaneous occurrence of economic stagnation and high inflation. This concept is significant as it describes a challenging macroeconomic environment where standard policy tools are often ineffective, demanding a specific response from investors seeking real asset protection.

## How the argument evolved

The concept of stagflation has been framed across the reports, evolving from a general description of the combination of negative forces to a more quantitatively defined state. In **2014**, the initial framing established stagflation as an economic situation combining "economic stagnation (falling growth and high unemployment) combined with high inflation (rising prices)." This was later refined in **2014** when the definition was explicitly stated as a condition where "high inflation occurs alongside economic stagnation," with a historical note suggesting "stagflation — this will be the legacy of the Bernanke regime."

The definition continued to evolve in **2020**, where the focus shifted to the combination of stagnation and noticeable inflation. The report defined stagflation as "the combination of economic stagnation, i.e. low, below-potential growth and noticeable inflation coincide." This maintained the core elements but emphasized the low growth component. Finally, in **2024**, the definition became more quantitative, linking stagflation to specific metrics. The report defined stagflation as an inflation rate above 3% and growth below 1%, citing the eurozone's experience in 2023 as an example, noting that "according to our proprietary definition of stagflation – inflation rate above 3%, growth below 1%." This observation was used to conclude that the eurozone "was in stagflation in 2023 and is now sliding out of stagflation into recession."
