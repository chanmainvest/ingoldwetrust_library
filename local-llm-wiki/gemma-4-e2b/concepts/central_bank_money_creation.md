# Central Bank Money Creation

This concept examines the process by which central banks generate new money and the subsequent impact of this creation on the global monetary system. It posits that the expansion of central bank money supply, often for the purpose of purchasing financial securities, is a fundamental driver of monetary expansion and potential inflation.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of central bank money creation began in **2015** with a direct assertion regarding future monetary expansion. The report stated that "central banks will create more irredeemable money globally this year." This initial observation sets the stage by forecasting an increase in the supply of money created by central banks.

The perspective deepened in **2017** by detailing the mechanism and scale of this creation. The report focused on the purpose behind the money being created, noting that "the largest central banks created the equivalent of almost USD 1,000 bn. worth of central bank money ex nihilo." Crucially, the report clarified the use of this newly created currency, specifying that "the fresh currency was not used to fund philanthropic projects but to purchase financial securities." This distinction highlights that the creation of money is directly linked to the purchase of assets within the financial system, rather than social spending.
