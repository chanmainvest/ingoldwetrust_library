# China's Trade Surpluses

China's trade surpluses represent a significant macroeconomic factor, influencing global price dynamics and the future role of the Chinese currency in the global financial system. This concept examines how China's trade balance impacts global inflation and the implications for its status as a reserve currency.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of China's trade surpluses began by focusing on their immediate effect on global price levels. In the **2021** report, the argument established that China's large trade surpluses actively contributed to disinflationary trends worldwide. Specifically, the report noted that "China's large trade surpluses contributed to disinflationary trends by exporting price deflation globally." This mechanism was further detailed by noting that "it was effectively exporting price deflation to the rest of the world."

The perspective evolved in the **2023** analysis by shifting the focus from the immediate inflation impact to the long-term implications for the currency itself. While acknowledging the disinflationary effect, the **2023** chapter addressed the question of whether these surpluses create a barrier to the currency achieving reserve status. The argument explored the counter-narrative, noting "the fact that China is running current account surpluses does not exclude their currency from becoming a global reserve currency." This point was framed in contrast to historical precedents, referencing the fact that "the US ran surpluses post-WW2, and this led the dollar on its global reserve currency path."
