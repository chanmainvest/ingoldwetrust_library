# De-Dollarization Trend

The concept of de-dollarization describes the observable, growing global shift away from the dominance of the US dollar as the primary global reserve and trading currency. This trend is significant because it signals a fundamental restructuring of the international monetary system, moving toward a more multipolar world order where alternative currencies gain traction, challenging the established hegemony of the dollar.

## How the argument evolved

The narrative surrounding de-dollarization has evolved over the years, moving from a general observation to a description of concrete geopolitical actions. In **2017**, the concept was introduced as a clear observation that "The world is looking for alternatives to the dollar – and finds them more and more often," establishing the trend as tangible. By **2020**, the analysis deepened, linking this gradual shift to specific state actions, noting that "he has been describing the developments on the global gold and oil markets for years as a story of the gradual “de-dollarization” of the world," exemplified by "With the March 2020 action, Russia and China have presented the USA with a difficult choice." The focus then broadened in **2021** to describe the structural outcome, suggesting "a slow but steady move from a system with a single world reserve currency to a multipolar world in which the euro and yuan play supporting roles alongside the US dollar." This progression continued into **2023**, where the conclusion was drawn that "the signals point to a continuation of the trend toward de-dollarization," reflecting the collective desire for change, as "the will of a large part of the planet is there to find a new system." Finally, in **2024**, the focus sharpened on the mechanism of erosion, noting that "the slow and steady erosion of US dollar hegemony that has gripped the neck of the global monetary system" is evident through actions such as commodity pricing and trade settlements, specifically noting "Initially, Russia and China’s strategy to phase out the use of dollars for commodity pricing."
