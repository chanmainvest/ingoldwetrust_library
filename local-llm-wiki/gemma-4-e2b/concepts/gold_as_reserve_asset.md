# Gold as Reserve Asset

Gold is fundamentally positioned as a universal reserve asset, serving as a store of value for central banks, investors, and individuals across all economic strata. Its significance lies in its unique capacity to preserve purchasing power and function as a neutral store of value, making it a critical consideration in global monetary and financial structures.

## How the argument evolved

The concept of gold as a reserve asset was established early, defining it as a universal store of value. In **2019**, the initial framing presented gold as the "universal reserve asset to which central banks, investors, and private individuals from every corner of the world and of every religion and every class return again and again." This established its broad applicability across different economic actors.

The narrative evolved in **2022** by focusing on gold's relationship with inflation and commodity prices. During this period, gold was specifically identified as a reserve asset capable of rising in price alongside inflation. Evidence suggested that "you need a reserve asset that has the ability to rise in price with the rise in the prices of energy and commodities and other inputs." Furthermore, the analysis stressed that "gold will become increasingly important on the sovereign level and on the individual level in order to preserve purchasing power."

The argument gained a more aggressive stance in **2024** when the discussion shifted toward gold's potential role in challenging the dominance of the US dollar. In the context of dollarization, gold was framed as a potential replacement for the US dollar as the de facto reserve asset. This perspective argued that "gold serving as a neutral reserve asset doesn’t just fix Triffin’s Dilemma, it obviates it completely," leading to the conclusion that "gold can still replace USTs as the de facto reserve asset."

Finally, in **2025**, the focus moved to the practical and political aspects of gold as a reserve. This chapter characterized gold as a reserve asset that possesses a distinct advantage: it is "free from legal and political risks." The conclusion drawn was that "gold repatriation is much more than a set of logistical operations; it is a powerful message of central banks asserting sovereignty over their national wealth."
