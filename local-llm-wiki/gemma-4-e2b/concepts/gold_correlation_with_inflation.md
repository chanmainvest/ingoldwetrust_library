# Gold correlation with inflation

The relationship between gold prices and inflation is a central theme in the analysis of the 'In Gold We Trust' corpus, positing that gold serves as a reliable hedge against inflationary pressures. This page synthesizes the evolving perspective on this correlation across two distinct reports, showing how the emphasis shifted from a general historical relationship to a more acute, condition-based correlation.

## How the argument evolved

The initial analysis, as presented in the **2009** report, established a foundational link between gold price movements and inflationary periods. This chapter highlighted a specific historical statistical finding, noting that "The development of the gold price is strongly correlated with inflation as soon as inflation hits extreme levels." Furthermore, this chapter quantified the historical relationship, citing a specific metric: "The monthly correlation coefficient of gold and the rate of inflation has been 0.48 from 1971 to 2009." This established a baseline understanding of the correlation's existence and historical magnitude.

The perspective evolved in the **2010** report, which refined this relationship by emphasizing the intensity of the correlation. While acknowledging the previous findings, the **2010** analysis stressed the conditionality of the link, stating that "the gold price correlates very strongly with inflation as soon as the latter hits extreme values." This suggests a refinement where the correlation is not merely present during inflation, but becomes significantly stronger when inflation reaches its peak levels. In essence, the argument moved from a general correlation during inflation to a statement about a very strong correlation triggered specifically by extreme inflationary events.
