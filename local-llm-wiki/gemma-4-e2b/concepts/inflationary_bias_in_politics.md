# Inflationary Bias in Politics

The concept of inflationary bias in politics posits that the political system itself is structurally predisposed to encourage the expansion of the money supply, primarily through the requirements of stimulus measures and campaign promises. This bias suggests that political actions, rather than purely economic drivers, are the fundamental prerequisites for monetary expansion.

## How the argument evolved

The analysis of inflationary bias in the corpus evolved across two distinct reports, establishing a consistent, yet evolving, view of political influence on monetary policy. In the **2012** report, the discussion focused on the mechanism by which political requirements drive inflation. The text explicitly addresses the belief that "stimulus measures expand the money supply due to political requirements." Furthermore, it states that "stimulus measures and campaign pledges constitute the basic requirements for the expansion of the money supply." This chapter established the core recognition that "Bernholz recognises an _inflationary bias_ in the political system."

The perspective was reinforced and refined in the **2020** report. While maintaining the central thesis, the **2020** analysis framed the inflationary bias more explicitly as a systemic feature of the political environment. The argument evolved to state that "the political system has an inflationary bias where stimuli and election promises are prerequisites for money supply expansion." This view is further characterized by the assertion that "Bernholz also recognizes an inflationary bias in the current political system." The report further illustrated this mechanism by linking the political promise to monetary expansion, noting that "stimuli and election promises are the basic prerequisite for money supply expansion, according to the “free beer principle.”"
