# De-dollarization drivers

The concept of de-dollarization centers on the growing political and structural pressures that are eroding the global reliance on the US dollar as the primary global reserve and trading currency. This shift is significant because it suggests a potential long-term re-pricing of the dollar's status, moving it from an inevitable global standard to a currency facing tangible challenges from both political dissatisfaction and deliberate policy actions.

## How the argument evolved

The narrative surrounding de-dollarization evolved across the two reports, moving from a focus on underlying confidence to the observable political and governmental actions driving the change. In the **2019** analysis, the driver was framed around sentiment and perception. The report highlighted "dwindling confidence in the US as a partner and the currency is a driver for de-dollarization." This perspective suggested that the dollar's dominance might not be permanent, noting that "the dollar’s dominance may outlast the Trump era, but it is not inevitable." This established the foundational idea that sentiment regarding the US role and currency is a key catalyst.

The argument matured in the **2023** analysis by shifting the focus from abstract confidence to concrete political and governmental mechanisms. While the underlying sentiment remains relevant, the drivers are now explicitly linked to actions taken by state actors. The **2023** report identified that the process of de-dollarization is driven by "political complaints and actions aimed at reducing dependence on the US dollar." This view acknowledges that the pressure comes from both public dissatisfaction and deliberate policy shifts. The evidence cited for this is the observation of "a whole lot of complaining by politicians (and sometimes central bankers)" coupled with "many steps taken by these governments – some of them small, some big, some spectacular." This evolution shows a progression from questioning the dollar's standing to cataloging the specific political and central bank maneuvers that are actively facilitating a structural transition away from the dollar.
