# Gold as Neutral Reserve Asset

Gold is conceptualized as a neutral reserve asset capable of floating across all currencies, serving as a benchmark for inflation expectations in a new monetary system. This concept posits gold's role not as a speculative commodity, but as a fundamental, non-sovereign store of value that gains prominence during periods of systemic monetary transition or geopolitical stress.

## How the argument evolved

The perspective on gold's function as a neutral reserve asset has evolved across the analyzed reports, shifting from a structural proposal to a reaction to current geopolitical realities. In the **2020** analysis, the argument was framed around a systemic shift in global finance. The report proposed that "gold is proposed to serve as a neutral reserve asset that floats across all currencies in a new system." Furthermore, it suggested a specific application for this asset, arguing that "we should use gold as a reference point for inflation expectations for all SDR currencies." This view established gold's role as a structural, cross-currency benchmark.

The **2024** analysis builds upon this foundation by linking the theoretical role of gold to immediate, tangible external pressures. While the 2020 report focused on the mechanics of a new system, the **2024** report emphasizes the current environment driving this emergence. It posits that gold is "emerging as a neutral reserve asset due to geopolitical tensions." This chapter frames the concept within a current context, asserting that "amid one of the most challenging geopolitical tensions in decades, there is a return to gold as a neutral reserve asset." Ultimately, the synthesis suggests that the concept moves from a theoretical structural necessity in a new monetary order to a practical, immediate response to geopolitical instability, where gold benefits from the "troubling conflict between East and West" by acting as a neutral monetary reserve.
