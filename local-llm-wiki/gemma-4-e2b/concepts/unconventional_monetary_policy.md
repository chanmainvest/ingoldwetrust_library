# Unconventional Monetary Policy

Unconventional monetary policy describes the shift in central banking practices where measures previously considered outside the norm are now standard procedures, signaling a new operational reality for monetary authorities. This concept highlights the increasing reliance on tools like quantitative easing to manage inflation and stabilize financial systems, suggesting a fundamental change in how central banks interact with the money supply.

## How the argument evolved

The understanding of unconventional monetary policy evolved across the analyzed reports, moving from a description of a new procedure to a statement of its established normalcy. In **2015**, the initial framing established that "Unconventional monetary policy is now a standard procedure of central bankers," suggesting that "other central banks are now stepping into the breach." This context was immediately linked to the mechanism driving this shift: central banks utilize unconventional measures, such as quantitative easing, specifically "to ensure steady price inflation after systemic banking crises." This process involves "blowing up the amount of central bank credit, which has come to be known under the euphemism 'quantitative easing'."

By **2020**, the narrative advanced to confirm that this practice has become fully integrated into the system. The concept was re-evaluated to indicate that "unconventional monetary policy is now conventional, indicating a new normal of money printing." The analysis concluded that "QE is now conventional," ultimately asserting that "unconventional monetary policy is at best paused but can no longer be stopped."
