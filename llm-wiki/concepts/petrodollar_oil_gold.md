# Petrodollar & the oil-gold nexus

The gold/oil ratio as a purchasing-power gauge, the petrodollar recycling system
that underpins USD demand, and the geopolitical signs of its erosion.

> "One ounce of gold today buys the same amount of oil as in 1945, 1982, and 2000."
> (2008, restated 2010, 2011)

See also: [de_dollarization](de_dollarization.md), [gold_valuation](gold_valuation.md),
[voices_interviews](voices_interviews.md).

## The gold/oil ratio

The gold/oil ratio measures **how many barrels of crude oil one ounce of gold can
buy**. It is treated as a purchasing-power parity gauge for gold measured in the
world's most strategically important commodity. Oil is the commodity most tightly
bound to gold: both are priced in USD, both rise as the dollar depreciates, oil is
"one of the most important indicators of inflation," and the "peak oil" supply
constraint applies by analogy to gold.

**What extremes signal:**
- A *high* ratio (many barrels per ounce) = gold expensive relative to oil / oil
  cheap.
- A *low* ratio (few barrels per ounce) = gold cheap relative to oil.

In 2008 the ratio hit ~7x (near its all-time low) and the authors forecast "either
gold to increase sharply or oil to fall significantly." By 2010-2011 it had
normalized to **13.3-13.6x** (median ~14-15x), which they read as "gold is
certainly not excessive" — a rebuttal to the "gold bubble" thesis.

| Year | Ratio | Long-term median | Reading |
|------|-------|------------------|---------|
| 2008 | ~7x | 16.1 | Gold "very cheap" vs oil |
| 2010 | 13.3x | 14.8 | "Slightly below median" |
| 2011 | 13.6x | 14.0 | "Slightly below median" |

**Historical extremes:** All-time high ~40-42x (1973, 1986); all-time low <6x
(2008). At the 1973 ratio high of 42 and constant $100/barrel oil, gold "would
have to rise to almost $4,000/oz" — the implied-purchasing-power argument.

## The petrodollar (2012, "Silent Farewell")

US dollar hegemony is sustained by **petrodollar recycling**: global oil trade is
invoiced in USD, forcing every importer to hold dollars and recycle them into
Treasuries — creating structural demand for US paper. The 2012 chapter frames the
system as a critical-state dynamic: "When the dollar collapse comes, it will
happen two ways: gradually then suddenly" (Rickards).

**Signs of erosion (cited 2012):**
- **India paying for Iranian oil in gold**; China considering the same. China +
  India take ~40% of Iranian oil exports and are the largest gold consumers — a
  direct gold-for-oil bypass of the dollar.
- Renminbi trade invoicing rose from <1% (2010) to 8%; HSBC forecast 50% by 2015.
- The China-Japan agreement (Dec 2011) to promote yuan/yen trade — "the importance
  and symbolic power cannot be over-emphasised," since these two hold the largest
  US Treasury stocks.
- BRICS cooperation and the BRICS Bank; the SCO (members span major gold producers
  and energy exporters); ALBA's SUCRE currency in South America.

**Gold implications.** The PBoC expected to "continue gradually accumulating
gold," possibly planning gold backing for the renminbi — mirroring how US gold
reserves (29,663 t in 1953) plus military dominance originally established dollar
reserve status. Officials floated targets of 6,000-8,000 t within 3-5 years and
10,000 t within ten. Zhang Jianhua (PBoC): *"No asset is safe now. The only choice
to hedge risks is to hold hard currency — gold."*

## Notable data & quotes

- **Purchasing-power equivalence:** one ounce buys the same quantity of oil as in
  1945, 1982/83, and 2000/02 — "proof of gold's monetary stability" (2008, 2010,
  2011).
- **Ratio extremes:** 1973/42x high; 1986/~40x high; 2008/<6x low.
- **Implied gold at 1973 ratio:** ~$4,000/oz at $100/barrel oil (2008).
- **Hong Kong gold imports to China:** 118 t (2010) → 427 t (2011); +640% YoY.
- **Cicero (66 BC, epigraph):** "If those revenues are destroyed, our whole system
  of credit will come down with a crash."
- **Rickards (2012):** "gradually then suddenly… the random event occurs [in a
  critical state]."

## Nuance

The gold/oil ratio is treated as **one indicator among several** in a ratio-
analysis framework, not a standalone valuation anchor. It sits inside a multi-ratio
suite (Dow/gold, gold/silver, home-price/gold). The authors openly acknowledge the
ratio can move via *either* leg — "either gold to increase sharply or oil to fall
significantly" — so it does not lock in a directional call on gold alone. The
median itself drifts (16.1 → 14.8 → 14 across 2008/2010/2011), treated as soft
rather than fixed.

The chief limitation — that oil's own supply/demand shocks and OPEC politics can
dominate the ratio independently of gold's value — is left implicit. By 2012 the
analytical weight shifts from ratio mean-reversion to the geopolitical/structural
dollar-erosion thesis (the petrodollar argument), implying the ratio is a symptom
while the monetary-architecture thesis is the deeper driver.
