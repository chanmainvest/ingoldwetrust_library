# Monetary policy & inflation

The inflation-vs-deflation debate, the Austrian School framing, the real-interest
-rate thesis, and the "monetary tectonics" model — and why all of it is bullish
for gold. The analytical backbone of the entire corpus.

> "Liquidity during deflation, substance during inflation — gold fulfils both
> criteria… Therefore gold is the optimal investment both in deflation and
> inflation." (2010)

See also: [sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md),
[gold_mining_stocks](gold_mining_stocks.md).

## The core thesis

Gold is the optimal investment in **both** inflation and deflation because it is
simultaneously "liquidity" (cash of the highest quality: no default risk,
indestructible) and "substance" (a real asset). The Austrian School framing,
present from 2013 onward, rests on two load-bearing distinctions:

- **Monetary inflation vs. price inflation** (Mises). Cause = money-supply
  expansion; effect = rising prices. The firm argues central banks and the public
  conflate the two, hiding the monetary root.
- **Credit/deleveraging deflation as the "natural cure," not the disease.**
  Falling prices are the consequence and cure of credit booms, not their cause
  (Baader) — and governments fight this at all costs because deflation raises the
  real value of debt.

**Monetary Tectonics** (coined 2013, developed 2014) models the inflation/
deflation interplay as two tectonic plates: the deflationary plate (commercial
bank deleveraging) vs. the inflationary plate (central-bank money creation).
Visible price movements are the "earthquakes." Crucially, the deflationary plate
cannot be allowed to win politically, so the system is structurally biased toward
inflation — a bullish backdrop for gold.

## How the argument evolved

- **2010** — The open question. Post-GFC, "inflation or deflation?" is "still
  unanswered." Gold pitched as winning in both. Floats "deliberate inflation as
  the only exit strategy" from over-indebtedness.
- **2011** — **The real-rates thesis crystallizes.** Pivot from inflation-vs-
  deflation to real interest rates as the true gold driver: "Inflation has never
  been the primary driver of the gold sector on its own." Financial repression
  (negative real rates as a saver→debtor transfer) is globally in vogue.
- **2013** — Austrian reframing; monetary tectonics introduced. The belief that
  "the gold bull market is over must be accompanied by an expectation of rising
  real interest rates" — and such a rise is impossible without crashing the
  economy. Real yields below 1% prevailed in 50–64% of months in the gold-bull
  decades (1970s, 2010s) vs 11–25% in the gold-bear decades.
- **2014** — Tectonics operationalized. The Incrementum Inflation Signal
  (a "monetary seismograph") and the gold/silver ratio as the
  "deflation/reflation ratio."
- **2016** — The negative-rates era. NIRP is "a never-before-seen dimension";
  effect on gold "clearly positive." Gold becomes "a high-yielder because it
  yields zero" (Gundlach).
- **2022** — **"Stagflation 2.0."** The pivot. Inflation is no longer
  "transitory"; the 40-year Great Moderation is declared over. A proprietary
  quantitative stagflation definition (inflation >3%, growth <1%). Frames the
  episode as rhyming with the 1970s but with far higher debt.
- **2023** — The **Crack-Up Boom thesis** (Mises). Current policy paths lead
  toward a Misesian crack-up boom / currency-regime failure — but conceded
  current rates "lie far below the dangerous territory of hyperinflation."
- **2024** — Disinflation, but "inflation not defeated." Acknowledges the 2023
  disinflation. Argues declaring victory repeats the 1970s error. Maintains the
  1966–1983 analog: if it holds, US CPI peaks again near ~11% around 2027/2028.
- **2025** — "Stubbornly at a (slightly) higher level." Core inflation above 2%
  for 4+ years. Frames a tacit raising of the inflation target. Adds the Fiscal
  Theory of the Price Level (Cochrane) alongside monetarism.

## Key mechanisms

1. **Negative real interest rates = the perfect gold environment.** Gold yields
   nothing, so real rates are its opportunity cost. In quintile analysis (2011),
   gold returns +21% in the year after real rates fall below −4.3%.
2. **Monetary tectonics.** Deflationary deleveraging vs inflationary central-bank
   policy, with policy structurally biased to inflation (2013, 2014).
3. **Financial repression.** Negative real rates transfer wealth from savers to
   debtors and liquidate government debt (Reinhart/Sbrancia) (2011, 2016).
4. **Cantillon effect / Austrian credit-cycle distortion.** New money is
   distributed unequally; artificial credit expansion misallocates capital,
   producing asset-price inflation first, CPI later (2014, 2023).
5. **Austrian money-supply analysis.** Money-supply growth in excess of real GDP
   growth creates a "monetary overhang" that unleashes CPI with a lag (2022, 2024,
   2025).
6. **Stagflation (proprietary definition, 2022).** Stagnation + elevated
   inflation; monetarily caused, supply-shock-triggered. Gold, commodities, and
   mining stocks win; bonds and equities lose together.
7. **Crack-up boom (Mises, 2023).** The terminal logic of unconstrained credit
   expansion: a flight into real goods, currency failure. Gold is "the ultimate
   means of payment."
8. **Implicit target-raising / fiscal dominance (2024, 2025).** Central banks
   covertly tolerate higher inflation; fiscal policy overrides monetary
   tightening.

## Notable data & quotes

- **Real-rate thresholds (2011):** Gold returns +21% in the year after real rates
  are below −4.3%; performance only weakens significantly once real rates rise
  above 0.99%.
- **Real yields below 1% (2013):** 50% of months in the 1970s, 60% in the 2000s,
  64% in 2010–2013 — vs 11% (1980s) and 25% (1990s).
- **Gold-standard price stability (2010):** US inflation 1879–1914 averaged 0.2%
  at 2.2% volatility; post-1971 (fiat) averaged 4.6% at 2.8% volatility.
- **Stagflation 2.0 (2022):** US CPI 8.3% (April 2022); Germany 7.4% (highest
  since 1981); monetary overhang up to 21.3% (2020) vs 4.9% (1982).
- **Crack-up boom (2023):** Hyperinflation defined as 50% monthly. End-2022 CPI:
  Turkey 64%, Argentina 95%, Venezuela 156%. The euro has lost 85% vs gold since
  1999.
- **M2 contraction (2024):** First sustained M2 declines since the Great
  Depression — US shrinking since Dec 2022, eurozone since May 2023. Summers et
  al.: reconstructing CPI to include interest costs produces an ~18% peak (Nov
  2022) vs the official ~9.1%.
- **1970s analog (2024/2025):** If the 1966–1983 parallel holds, US CPI spikes to
  ~11% around 2027/2028; in the 1970s' third wave gold rose ~7x.
- **Baader (2013):** "Falling prices… are not the cause of economic and financial
  crises, but their consequences — and at the same time their cure."
- **Gundlach (2016):** Gold is "a high-yielder, because it yields zero."
- **Friedman (2022/2025):** "Inflation is always and everywhere a monetary
  phenomenon."

## Nuance — does the directional call shift?

Yes, and this is the single most important nuance:

- **2010–2016: genuinely agnostic.** The "inflation or deflation?" question is
  repeatedly "unanswered." Gold is recommended precisely because the firm hedges
  both outcomes.
- **2020: the firm takes a side.** The "Boy Who Cried Wolf" special publication
  is the first explicit, dated call for an inflationary decade.
- **2022: conviction hardens** into "Stagflation 2.0"; the deflationary plate is
  effectively dismissed as subordinate.
- **2024: a notable admission of error.** The firm concedes its model "failed to
  correctly capture the continuing disinflationary phase throughout 2023" and
  revises the Inflation Signal — an unusual internal correction — while
  reaffirming the structural call.
- **2025: deflation makes a partial comeback.** China is "exporting deflation";
  the near-term outlook is "very mixed." The firm broadens from pure monetarism
  to the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level, accommodating M2 contraction without
  deflationary collapse.

> Net: from neutral hedging (2010–2016), to a confident stagflationary call
> (2020–2022), to a conditional stance that treats deflation as a real short-term
> risk while maintaining the structural inflation thesis over 3–5 years
> (2023–2025). The Austrian framing is the constant; the directional conviction
> waxes and wanes with the data.
