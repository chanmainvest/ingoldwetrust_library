# Monetary policy & inflation

The inflation-vs-deflation debate, the Austrian School framing, the real-interest
-rate thesis, and the "monetary tectonics" model — and why all of it is bullish
for gold. The analytical backbone of the entire corpus.

> "Liquidity during deflation, substance during inflation — gold fulfils both
> criteria… Therefore gold is the optimal investment both in deflation and
> inflation." (2010)

See also: [sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md),
[gold_mining_stocks](gold_mining_stocks.md).

## The core thesis

Gold is the optimal investment in **both** inflation and deflation because it is
simultaneously "liquidity" (cash of the highest quality: no default risk,
indestructible) and "substance" (a real asset). The Austrian School framing,
present from 2013 onward, rests on two load-bearing distinctions:

- **Monetary inflation vs. price inflation** (Mises). Cause = money-supply
  expansion; effect = rising prices. The firm argues central banks and the public
  conflate the two, hiding the monetary root.
- **Credit/deleveraging deflation as the "natural cure," not the disease.**
  Falling prices are the consequence and cure of credit booms, not their cause
  (Baader) — and governments fight this at all costs because deflation raises the
  real value of debt.

**Monetary Tectonics** (coined 2013, developed 2014) models the inflation/
deflation interplay as two tectonic plates: the deflationary plate (commercial
bank deleveraging) vs. the inflationary plate (central-bank money creation).
Visible price movements are the "earthquakes." Crucially, the deflationary plate
cannot be allowed to win politically, so the system is structurally biased toward
inflation — a bullish backdrop for gold.

## How the argument evolved

- **2007–2009 — The pre-Austrian inflation framing.** The 2007 origin report
  treats gold simply as "a natural hedge against inflationary tendencies." The
  2008 report introduces the load-bearing real-interest-rate thesis (the
  "primary driver of the gold bull market, i.e. negative real interest rates"),
  the MZM money-supply gauge (since M3 was discontinued in 2006), the
  gold-vs-Misery-Index correlation, and the explicit Volcker contrast: in 1980
  the US was net-creditor with positive trade balance, so 20% nominal / 8% real
  rates were possible — conditions that no longer hold, so "an imminent rate
  increase under the current chairman seems unlikely." The 2009 *Is Gold Pricing
  in the Coming Inflation?* chapter formalizes the inflation correlation (0.48
  monthly 1971–2009; 0.76 in the 1978–1982 high-inflation window), introduces
  the gold-standard-vs-fiat stability comparison (1879–1914: 0.2% avg at 2.2%
  volatility vs 4.6% at 2.8% post-1971), seeds the Shadow-Gold-Price intuition
  ("If 25% of the US money supply M2 were to be covered by gold, this would lead
  to a fictitious gold price of USD 7,000/ounce"), and — notably — already
  frames hyperinflation as a "negligible counter indicator," citing the post-
  WWII US debt reduction from 120% to 30% of GDP "without having to resort to
  hyperinflation." Bernanke's 2002 "printing press" speech is quoted in full as
  the intellectual frame. The 2008 report is also where the "remonetisation of
  gold has begun" line first appears.
- **2010** — The open question. Post-GFC, "inflation or deflation?" is "still
  unanswered." Gold pitched as winning in both. Floats "deliberate inflation as
  the only exit strategy" from over-indebtedness.
- **2011** — **The real-rates thesis crystallizes.** Pivot from inflation-vs-
  deflation to real interest rates as the true gold driver: "Inflation has never
  been the primary driver of the gold sector on its own." Financial repression
  (negative real rates as a saver→debtor transfer) is globally in vogue.
- **2012** — **The Austrian terminology sharpened into a semantic polemic.**
  "Inflation ≠ rising prices": inflation is the *expansion of the uncovered money
  supply* (the cause); rising prices are merely the *result*. The chapter
  attributes the public's failure to grasp the cause-effect chain to "linguistic
  desensitisation," which "prevents us from recognizing the cause-effect
  relationship" and so breeds wrongheaded remedies (price controls,
  nationalisations) while "inflation continues to be fuelled." The cross-country
  M2/inflation evidence is marshalled (103 countries; Fed Minneapolis correlation
  coefficients of 0.925/0.958/0.95 for M0/M1/M2; a 1% M2 expansion → 0.9% gold-
  price increase six months later, per the World Gold Council). Tanzi's Law is
  invoked: at high inflation, real budget deficits *grow* faster because tax
  receipts lag the taxable transaction — so printing to fund deficits eventually
  fails. A separate chapter re-states the negative-real-rates thesis with the
  tipping-point data: gold's annual performance "turns negative from a positive
  real interest rate of 3–4% upwards."
- **2013** — Austrian reframing; monetary tectonics introduced. The belief that
  "the gold bull market is over must be accompanied by an expectation of rising
  real interest rates" — and such a rise is impossible without crashing the
  economy. Real yields below 1% prevailed in 50–64% of months in the gold-bull
  decades (1970s, 2010s) vs 11–25% in the gold-bear decades.
- **2014** — Tectonics operationalized. The Incrementum Inflation Signal
  (a "monetary seismograph") and the gold/silver ratio as the
  "deflation/reflation ratio." The *Conclusion: Inflation vs. Deflation* chapter
  is the period's clearest agnostic synthesis: "it cannot be stated a priori
  whether inflationary forces will prevail… when in doubt, higher price inflation
  will definitely be preferred over a deflationary adjustment," but
  "disinflationary forces should not be underestimated" — a temporary deflationary
  episode "on the road to greater inflation" is admitted as realistic. The
  Bernanke Fed's post-2011 disinflation is acknowledged, with the caveat that
  falling inflation *below* the 2–2.5% target simply hands the Fed leeway to
  counter-counter, making the long-run direction inflationary.
- **2014 (Japanese scenario)** — The European "Japanification" warning. With
  17 years of zero rates, Japan's debt service already consumed 25% of tax
  revenue; a 3-pp refinancing-cost rise would swallow the entire tax take — the
  "Keynesian endgame." Europe is judged to be following the same path ("central
  banks themselves are in danger of becoming zombie-banks," per the BIS). This
  is the deflationary counter-weight to the inflation thesis: the over-indebted
  West risks a Japanese-style stagnation that would itself be bullish for gold
  via rising Japanese gold demand (already tripled on Abenomics).
- **2016** — The negative-rates era. NIRP is "a never-before-seen dimension";
  effect on gold "clearly positive." Gold becomes "a high-yielder because it
  yields zero" (Gundlach). 2016 is the corpus's *annus horribilis*: the
  stagflation thesis is announced ("From Reflation to Stagflation"), the
  "monetary Marshmallow test" frames NIRP as a violation of positive time
  preference (originary interest "can never disappear," Mises), helicopter money
  is debated as the next instrument, and the "From Risk-Free Interest to
  Interest-Free Risk" chapter declares a "monetary Rubicon has been crossed" —
  "we have strayed unto *terra incognita*." Five currency areas had adopted
  NIRP; >USD 8 trn of government bonds traded at negative yields; 51% of all
  government bonds (USD 15 trn) showed negative *real* yields. The conclusion:
  "monetary policy is at the end of the line."
- **2022** — **"Stagflation 2.0."** The pivot. Inflation is no longer
  "transitory"; the 40-year Great Moderation is declared over. A proprietary
  quantitative stagflation definition (inflation >3%, growth <1%). Frames the
  episode as rhyming with the 1970s but with far higher debt.
- **2023** — The **Crack-Up Boom thesis** (Mises). Current policy paths lead
  toward a Misesian crack-up boom / currency-regime failure — but conceded
  current rates "lie far below the dangerous territory of hyperinflation."
- **2024** — Disinflation, but "inflation not defeated." Acknowledges the 2023
  disinflation. Argues declaring victory repeats the 1970s error. Maintains the
  1966–1983 analog: if it holds, US CPI peaks again near ~11% around 2027/2028.
- **2025** — "Stubbornly at a (slightly) higher level." Core inflation above 2%
  for 4+ years. Frames a tacit raising of the inflation target. Adds the Fiscal
  Theory of the Price Level (Cochrane) alongside monetarism.

## Key mechanisms

1. **Negative real interest rates = the perfect gold environment.** Gold yields
   nothing, so real rates are its opportunity cost. In quintile analysis (2011),
   gold returns +21% in the year after real rates fall below −4.3%.
2. **Monetary tectonics.** Deflationary deleveraging vs inflationary central-bank
   policy, with policy structurally biased to inflation (2013, 2014).
3. **Financial repression.** Negative real rates transfer wealth from savers to
   debtors and liquidate government debt (Reinhart/Sbrancia) (2011, 2016).
4. **Cantillon effect / Austrian credit-cycle distortion.** New money is
   distributed unequally; artificial credit expansion misallocates capital,
   producing asset-price inflation first, CPI later (2014, 2023).
5. **Austrian money-supply analysis.** Money-supply growth in excess of real GDP
   growth creates a "monetary overhang" that unleashes CPI with a lag (2022, 2024,
   2025).
6. **Stagflation (proprietary definition, 2022).** Stagnation + elevated
   inflation; monetarily caused, supply-shock-triggered. Gold, commodities, and
   mining stocks win; bonds and equities lose together.
7. **Crack-up boom (Mises, 2023).** The terminal logic of unconstrained credit
   expansion: a flight into real goods, currency failure. Gold is "the ultimate
   means of payment."
8. **Implicit target-raising / fiscal dominance (2024, 2025).** Central banks
   covertly tolerate higher inflation; fiscal policy overrides monetary
   tightening.
9. **Helicopter money as the next instrument (2016).** Once ZIRP, NIRP, and QE
   are exhausted, the only remaining lever is overt monetary finance ("QE for the
   people"). Four variants catalogued: QE+fiscal expansion, permanent cash
   transfers to government, cancellation of bonds held by the central bank, and
   cash transfers to households. Draghi called it "a very interesting concept";
   Praet flagged it as a last-resort "extreme instrument." Theorised as
   potentially shifting money from "credit money" to "reputation money" (Mayer).
10. **Stagflation pre-emption (2014, 2016).** The 1970s replay thesis: credit
    expansion → devaluation of the senior currency (USD) vs commodities → cost-
    push inflation + stagnation. The Phillips curve "by definition rules out
    stagflation," which is exactly why the authors take it seriously. First
    raised as one 2014 scenario; escalated in 2016 to "it is easily possible
    that the economy will slide directly into a pronounced stagflation phase" —
    the direct precursor of 2022's "Stagflation 2.0."

## Notable data & quotes

- **Real-rate thresholds (2011):** Gold returns +21% in the year after real rates
  are below −4.3%; performance only weakens significantly once real rates rise
  above 0.99%.
- **Real yields below 1% (2013):** 50% of months in the 1970s, 60% in the 2000s,
  64% in 2010–2013 — vs 11% (1980s) and 25% (1990s).
- **Gold-standard price stability (2010):** US inflation 1879–1914 averaged 0.2%
  at 2.2% volatility; post-1971 (fiat) averaged 4.6% at 2.8% volatility.
- **Stagflation 2.0 (2022):** US CPI 8.3% (April 2022); Germany 7.4% (highest
  since 1981); monetary overhang up to 21.3% (2020) vs 4.9% (1982).
- **Crack-up boom (2023):** Hyperinflation defined as 50% monthly. End-2022 CPI:
  Turkey 64%, Argentina 95%, Venezuela 156%. The euro has lost 85% vs gold since
  1999.
- **M2 contraction (2024):** First sustained M2 declines since the Great
  Depression — US shrinking since Dec 2022, eurozone since May 2023. Summers et
  al.: reconstructing CPI to include interest costs produces an ~18% peak (Nov
  2022) vs the official ~9.1%.
- **1970s analog (2024/2025):** If the 1966–1983 parallel holds, US CPI spikes to
  ~11% around 2027/2028; in the 1970s' third wave gold rose ~7x.
- **Baader (2013):** "Falling prices… are not the cause of economic and financial
  crises, but their consequences — and at the same time their cure."
- **Gundlach (2016):** Gold is "a high-yielder, because it yields zero."
- **Friedman (2022/2025):** "Inflation is always and everywhere a monetary
  phenomenon."
- **M2/inflation correlation (2012):** Across 110 countries, r(M0)=0.925,
  r(M1)=0.958, r(M2)=0.950; for 14 Latin American countries r(M2)=0.993. A 1%
  M2 expansion → 0.9% gold-price increase six months later (World Gold Council).
- **NIRP scale (2016):** Negative rates imposed in five currency areas (eurozone,
  Japan, Switzerland, Sweden, Denmark); >USD 8 trn of government bonds at
  negative nominal yields; 51% of all government bonds (USD 15 trn) at negative
  real yields; ECB/BoJ printing the value of global annual gold production every
  ~30 days. Since Lehman, 654 global rate cuts and USD 12.3 trn of central-bank
  asset purchases.
- **Time preference (2016):** Negative interest rates are framed as violating
  "positive time preference" — "as long as human beings differentiate between
  'sooner' and 'later', there can be no negative real interest rate" (Hoppe). The
  originary interest rate "can never disappear" (Mises).
- **Helicopter-money survey (2016):** Of German savers, 46.5% of a one-off
  EUR 2,000 transfer would be spent; with regular payments 30.5% would raise
  consumption — implying helicopter money "would provide a boost to inflation"
  (Gehringer/Schafföner).
- **Fekete (2014, 2016):** "At first the pendulum was swinging towards infinite
  interest… right now the pendulum is swinging to… zero interest, spelling
  hyper-deflation… Arguing which of the two forms will dominate is futile."

## Nuance — does the directional call shift?

Yes, and this is the single most important nuance:

- **2010–2014: genuinely agnostic.** The "inflation or deflation?" question is
  repeatedly "unanswered" (2014: "it cannot be stated a priori whether
  inflationary forces will prevail"). Gold is recommended precisely because the
  firm hedges both outcomes.
- **2015–2016: the directional call begins to form.** The 2015 report's forward
  look explicitly raised "rising inflationary trends" as a likely "paradigm
  change," and 2016's "From Reflation to Stagflation" escalated this to a near-
  forecast: stagflation had "moved a decisive step closer." The agnosticism is
  eroding, but the firm still frames it as *one scenario among several* rather
  than the base case — the language is warning, not prediction.
- **2020: the firm takes a side.** The "Boy Who Cried Wolf" special publication
  is the first explicit, dated call for an inflationary decade.
- **2022: conviction hardens** into "Stagflation 2.0"; the deflationary plate is
  effectively dismissed as subordinate.
- **2024: a notable admission of error.** The firm concedes its model "failed to
  correctly capture the continuing disinflationary phase throughout 2023" and
  revises the Inflation Signal — an unusual internal correction — while
  reaffirming the structural call.
- **2025: deflation makes a partial comeback.** China is "exporting deflation";
  the near-term outlook is "very mixed." The firm broadens from pure monetarism
  to the Fiscal Theory of the Price Level, accommodating M2 contraction without
  deflationary collapse.

> Net: from neutral hedging (2010–2016), to a confident stagflationary call
> (2020–2022), to a conditional stance that treats deflation as a real short-term
> risk while maintaining the structural inflation thesis over 3–5 years
> (2023–2025). The Austrian framing is the constant; the directional conviction
> waxes and wanes with the data.
