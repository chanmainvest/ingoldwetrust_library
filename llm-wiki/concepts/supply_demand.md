# Supply, demand & peak gold

Why gold cannot be analyzed like an ordinary commodity — the "peak gold" thesis,
the stock-to-flow inversion of supply, the "great fallacy of high gold demand,"
and the central-bank/ETF dominance that emerged in the 2020s.

> "There is no such thing as a glut of gold." (2022)

See also: [central_bank_gold](central_bank_gold.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md),
[india_eastern_demand](india_eastern_demand.md).

## The core thesis

**Gold cannot be analyzed like an ordinary commodity because it is not consumed,
only held.** This reframes both supply and demand in three linked moves:

- **The "peak gold" argument (2008).** The earliest framing is conventional but
  supply-constrained: annual mine output is structurally short of demand, and the
  gap is filled by central-bank sales and recycling. Mine production was argued to
  be at or past a "peak," driven by declining ore grades.
- **The stock-to-flow inversion (2014, 2022).** Because virtually all gold ever
  mined still exists, annual mine output (~1.5% of the stock) is nearly irrelevant
  to price. Gold's mean stock-to-flow ratio of 67.1 (vs 37.1 for the US dollar's
  M3) is what makes it monetary.
- **The "great fallacy of high gold demand" (2014).** Mainstream analysts err by
  treating gold demand like consumer-goods demand. For gold, "demand is not a
  quantity" — it is willingness to pay. Every holder is simultaneously a potential
  seller (reservation demand). Miners are price-takers with "minimal impact on
  price."

## How the argument evolved

- **2007 — The origin report.** The very first IGWT report is already a pure
  flow-based structural-shortage argument: ~3,600 t annual demand vs ~2,500 t
  mine output, the deficit "compensated by sales by central banks as well as by
  recycling." The peak-gold intuition is already present ("mining production
  will decline at least until 2009… the biggest, richest and most easily
  exploited mines are mostly known"), as is the lead-time argument (7–9 years
  from first drilling to first ounce) and the regional shift (South Africa 70%
  of world output in 1970 → 15% in 2007; China and Russia rising). The CBGA
  regime is treated as the swing supply source whose 2009 expiry "will
  significantly tighten the supply on the global market."
- **2008 — The peak-gold / structural-shortage era.** Flow-based framework. Mine
  output stagnant (~2,476 t), demand ~3,600 t, gap filled by CBGA central-bank
  sales and scrap. South Africa's collapse from 67.7% of world output (1970) to
  11.1% (2007) is the cautionary exhibit.
- **2009 — Investment demand explodes** (retail +396% in H2 2008); China becomes
  both largest producer and consumer.
- **2014 — The intellectual hinge.** Flow-based analysis is declared misleading;
  stock-to-flow and reservation demand become the lens.
- **2016 — Market-cap context.** Above-ground stock ~183,000 t, worth USD 7,640 bn
  — only 3.1% of US financial assets.
- **2022 — Full reframe.** The gold-basis/contango/backwardation model (Keith
  Weiner / Monetary Metals): non-declining marginal utility, abundance (high
  stock-to-flow), and gold as the "meter stick" of value.
- **2024-2026 — Empirical turn.** Central banks bought 1,082 t (2022) and 1,037 t
  (2023) — >35% of the 3,644 t mined in 2023. The marginal buyer shifted West→
  East. By 2026 investment demand surged 84% YoY to 2,175 t; ETF inflows USD 89 bn.

## Key mechanisms

1. **Peak gold / declining mine output (2008).** Ore grades falling, lead times
   5-8 years. *Later complicated:* mine output kept hitting records (3,672 t in
   2025) — but the *stock-to-flow point* survived.
2. **Recycling as price-elastic secondary supply.** Scrap was 23% of supply in
   2007; 1,370 t in 2024 (+11%). But by 2026 recycling is *muted* despite record
   prices — holders expect further gains.
3. **Jewellery as the historical demand base, now price-sensitive and Eastern.**
   63% in 2007; fell to 1,543 t (-18%) in 2025 as price crushed volume. Framed as
   just another *form* of holding gold (2022), not consumption.
4. **Investment demand's structural rise.** 2009 retail +396%; 2026 investment
   demand 2,175 t (+84%). Bars/coins + ETFs overtook jewellery as the marginal
   price driver.
5. **Central-bank demand >35% of mine supply by 2024.** The post-2022 inflection
   (Russia reserve freeze) is the key catalyst. See
   [central_bank_gold](central_bank_gold.md).
6. **Stock-to-flow: annual flow barely matters.** ~200,000 t stock vs ~3,000-3,700
   t annual flow; a 20% mining shortfall = 0.3% of stock.
7. **The basis / backwardation mechanism (2022).** Price fundamentals read from
   the gold basis, not tonnage. Backwardation = "harbinger of monetary collapse."
8. **Conviction vs. opportunistic buyers (2026).** New taxonomy: conviction
   buyers (CBs, Western ETFs — price-insensitive, set direction) vs. opportunistic
   buyers (Asian households — accumulate on dips, set the floor). Goldman Sachs:
   "Conviction buyers set the price. Opportunistic buyers set the floor."

## Notable data & quotes

- **Mine production:** ~2,476 t (2007, "lowest in eleven years") → 3,644 t (2023)
  → record **3,672 t (2025)**. Total supply 2025: 5,002 t.
- **Above-ground stock:** ~183,000 t (2016); "around 200,000 tonnes" (2022).
- **Stock-to-flow (1900-2013, 2014):** gold mean 67.1 (median YoY change 3.1%);
   US$ M3 mean 37.1 (median 30.6%).
- **South Africa:** 32 moz in 1970 (67.7% of world) → 11.1% in 2007; lowest output
   since 1922.
- **Central-bank share:** 1,037 t in 2023 = ">35% of total yearly supply" (2024);
   863 t in 2025 = USD 95.2 bn record.
- **WisdomTree (2026):** "For the first time in roughly three decades, gold
   surpassed Treasuries in central bank reserve share."
- **FOFOA (2014):** "Gold is the inverse of paper, unlimited to the upside,
   limited to the downside."
- **Blumen (2014):** "Miners are price takers… mine supply is so small compared
   to the existing gold, miners have minimal impact on price."

## Nuance — was "peak gold" (2008) vindicated?

**Partially, and the corpus quietly retreats from the strong form.** The 2008
thesis (Barrick forecasting 10-15% declines) was *not borne out by tonnage* —
mine production climbed to record 3,672 t (2025). The strong Malthusian claim was
falsified by China, Russia, West Africa, and heap-leach economics.

But the thesis survives in three softer forms:
- *Reserve-quality decline is real* — ore grades keep falling (South Africa 16 g/t
  → 4 g/t); the "peak" is better described as a peak in *grade and ease*, not
  tonnage.
- *Supply elasticity is low* — even record prices failed to elicit a proportionate
  supply response (2025, 2026).
- *It became irrelevant to price* — the 2014/2022 reframes make the point moot:
  whether mines produce 2,400 t or 3,700 t is a 0.3-0.85% perturbation against a
  200,000 t stock.

> Scarcity is redefined from *flow scarcity* (2008) to *monetary/stock scarcity*
> relative to expanding fiat and debt (2026) — which is why gold can hit record
> prices while mine output also hits records.
