# Voices: the interviews

The recurring "exclusive interview" chapters — a distinctive feature of the
corpus that brings external thinkers (FOFOA, Macleod, Gromen, Pozsar, Napier,
Tindale) into the analysis, and the major ideas that entered the corpus through
them.

> The interview chapters are where the corpus tests its theses against outside
> minds — and where several of its flagship concepts (Bretton Woods III, the
> exorbitant burden, Freegold) actually originated.

See also: [de_dollarization](de_dollarization.md), [sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md),
[monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md).

## The guests

### FOFOA (2021) — Freegold
Anonymous blogger ("Friend Of Friend Of Another"), intellectual heir to the
1990s "Another"/"FOA" writers. **Core thesis (Freegold):** the inevitable end of
paper/bullion banking — a "2nd Singularity" in which physical gold is severed
from paper gold (XAU), paper balances are cash-settled, and physical is repriced
dramatically "once, and that will be more than enough." **Distinctive
contribution:** the idea that gold is *not an inflation hedge* but a "transition
hedge / singularity hedge"; the long-standing **$55,000** reference number.

### Alasdair Macleod (2022) — sound money / gold standard
Head of Research for Goldmoney; nephew of Iain Macleod, who coined "stagflation"
(1965). **Core thesis:** the real story is the collapse of purchasing power of
paper currencies; inflation *is* the expansion of money and credit (he faults the
FOMC for never mentioning money). The "end solution" is a forced **return to a
genuine gold-coin standard** because major central banks are already in negative
equity. **Distinctive contribution:** the sound-money framing; the argument that
the 1970s Volcker escape (rates to 20%) is impossible today given mega-deficits.

### Luke Gromen (2022, 2026) — the exorbitant burden / oil-gold link
Founder of FFTT. The corpus's recurring de-dollarization voice (2018-2026).
**Core thesis:** we are in the first Western sovereign-debt bubble since WW2;
"sovereigns cannot default… this is just inflation." Freezing Russian reserves
was a "break the glass" moment. By 2026: gold has replaced Treasuries as the
primary CB reserve asset for the first time in 30+ years; China's point to the US
is "you've overvalued the dollar versus gold so laughably that if you want the
dollar to fall, let gold rise." **Distinctive contribution:** the oil-gold-dollar
nexus as the structural driver; turning de-dollarization into a mainstream IGWT
leitmotif.

### Zoltan Pozsar (2023) — Bretton Woods III
Former US Treasury (Office of Debt Management), NY Fed (2008 crisis). **Core
thesis:** reserve management is undergoing "transformative change." The world is
bifurcating into a **dollar bloc (G7)** and a **renminbi/gold bloc (Eurasia)**.
Dollars are "inside money" (freezable); gold is "outside money" (you control it
if you hold it physically). **Distinctive contribution:** the Bretton Woods III /
commodity-backed money thesis; the inside-money/outside-money distinction;
portfolio prescription **20/20/20/40** (cash/commodities/bonds/stocks) replacing
60/40.

### Russell Napier (2023) — financial repression
Author of *Anatomy of the Bear*; founder of The Library of Mistakes. **Core
thesis:** the endgame is financial repression — governments capturing both the
banking system and the savings system to depress yields while inflation runs.
"The crucial thing is the capture of the financial system, not the collapse of
the banking system." Recessions revert to 1945-1990 type (EPS decline ~11%, not a
2008-style halving). **Distinctive contribution:** the financial-repression/
credit-controls model. Title dictum: "Save like a pessimist, invest like an
optimist." **Notably dissents** from the de-dollarization consensus.

### Craig Tindale (2026) — the physical-world / materiality voice
Private investor; his white paper reportedly reached the White House and Pentagon.
**Core thesis:** "the end of infinite materiality." China refines 50-98% of
nearly every strategic metal; financial sanctions can be answered with material
sanctions. Supply-chain physics can't be toggled (an idled aluminum smelter takes
12 months and jackhammers to restart). **Distinctive contribution:** the
operational/physical-supply-chain lens; bridges commodity bottlenecks to the
gold-revaluation thesis ("a gold revaluation… probably the most probable it's
been in the last 40 years").

## Adopted vs. arm's length

**Heavily integrated (became recurring Incrementum themes):**
- **Gromen's de-dollarization / oil-gold-dollar nexus** — the most integrated;
  appears 2018-2026, woven through IGWT's own chapters.
- **Pozsar's Bretton Woods III** — the bloc framework and structural-inflation
  thesis adopted into the 2023 "Final Showdown" and beyond.
- **Napier's financial repression and "different kind of recession"** — echoes
  through IGWT's monetary-climate and fiscal-dominance framing.
- **Macleod's Austrian money-supply definition and Volcker-impossibility** —
  aligns with IGWT's own long-standing Austrian framing.

**Treated at arm's length (tail risk, not base case):**
- **Freegold (FOFOA)** — the $55K physical-only reset is presented as "one of the
  most fascinating perspectives," a thought experiment, not the base case. IGWT
  retains conventional incremental price-targeting.
- **A formal/near-term gold-standard return (Macleod)** — IGWT leans more on
  "gold as reserve asset / market revaluation" than on "governments re-peg to
  gold." See [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md).
- **FOFOA's "gold is not an inflation hedge"** — actually runs *against* IGWT's
  own marketing of gold as inflation protection; only partially conceded.

  financial system, and the paper gold market, and the reset will include the
  repricing of physical gold."

  standard. This could happen quite rapidly." / "Russia might drop a bombshell…
  'It's not 2,000 tonnes, we've got 5,000 tonnes.'"
- **Gromen (2022):** "We now have the biggest sovereign debt bubble… in 100 years."
  / "Sovereigns cannot default… This is not really credit risk, it is duration

  normalize debt/GDP.
- **Gromen (2026):** China's ~$1.2 tn trade surplus would be balanced by gold at
  **~$26,000/oz**.
- **Pozsar (2023):** "Two percent inflation and going back to the old world… I
  don't think it stands a snowball's chance in hell." / "Paul Volcker is a
  legendary policymaker, but he was lucky."
- **Napier (2023):** "The first casualty of war is the truth, and the second
  casualty of war is price stability." / **"I'm not in the de-dollarization
  camp."**
- **Tindale (2026):** "Gold at USD 25,000 isn't hard to imagine."

## Nuance — where the guests disagree

The interviews are not an echo chamber; the sharpest splits:

- **De-dollarization: the flagship split.** Pozsar and Gromen are firmly in the
  camp; **Napier explicitly dissents** ("I'm not in the de-dollarization camp"),
  noting the renminbi is held as a reserve by only one central bank in size and
  foreign RMB holdings are *falling*. He draws the reserve-vs-trade-currency
  distinction others blur.
- **Inflation's mechanism.** Macleod is a monetarist purist; Gromen and Pozsar
  locate inflation structurally (sovereign-debt imperative; commodity/labor
  intensity of rearm/reshore/energy-transition).
- **Gold's function.** FOFOA insists gold is *not* an inflation hedge (it's a
  transition hedge); the others lean on gold as an inflation/purchasing-power
  hedge — a quiet tension with FOFOA.
- **Deflation vs. inflation endgame.** Exter/Downs (the 2019 Exter's-Pyramid
  chapter) feared a *deflationary* collapse; the later interviews all resolve
  toward "inflate or die."
- **The alternative to the dollar.** Even within the Eurasian-bloc advocates:
  Pozsar emphasizes the *renminbi*; Gromen emphasizes *gold*.
