# Crisis triggers & the swan taxonomy

The white/gray/black-swan framework, the specific crisis triggers identified
across 2016-2018, and the Austrian credit-cycle "trigger of the next recession"
argument — with a candid retrospective on the predictions' accuracy.

> "There is nothing riskier than the widespread perception that there is no risk."
> — Howard Marks (2018)

See also: [monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md),
[sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md), [hyperinflation](hyperinflation.md),
[golden_constant_austrian_school](golden_constant_austrian_school.md).

## The core framework (2017)

The authors adopt Taleb's three-tier taxonomy but reframe it to indict market
complacency:

- **Black swan** — "not only highly improbable, but also ex-ante unimaginable for
  the general public." Extreme consequences; almost impossible to prepare for.
- **Gray swan** — highly unlikely and significant, but at least imaginable ex-ante
  because similar events have occurred.
- **White swan** — "events which it is possible to expect, regardless of whether
  they are relatively improbable or probable."

Their key analytical move: they argue a **US recession is properly a white swan**
(a known, cyclical, expected event), but markets were pricing it as a gray/black
swan — "the probability of an approaching recession is currently completely
disregarded" (2017). They tether this to Austrian Business Cycle Theory: boom-bust
cycles are systematic consequences of credit expansion, not random shocks. The
framework's purpose is to set up the gold thesis: most swans point to a monetary-
policy U-turn (rate cuts + renewed QE), which they expect to be bullish for gold.

## Specific triggers identified (2016-2018)

**2016** — a diffuse list: a bursting bubble in China; Greek exit; Brexit; a
failing Italian bank or major loss at Deutsche Bank; "maybe just a verbal lapse by
Janet Yellen"; an inflation surprise from oil base effects; a flattening yield
curve; a Fed policy error.

**2017** — an explicit gray-swan inventory:
- **US recession** (treated as the *primary* white swan / most important catalyst).
- **Stagflation** (gray swan).
- **Credit crisis in China** (gray swan).
- **Political crisis in the US / Trump impeachment.**
- **Geopolitical escalation** — Korea, South China Sea, Middle East.
- **Hyper-deflation** from a banking or sovereign-debt crisis.
- **Inflationary boom** and **monetary reset / partial remonetization of gold**.

Their explicit judgment: "a potential recession in the US, which would invariably
lead to a U-turn in monetary policy, represents the potentially most important
catalyst for the future trend in the gold price."

**2018** — narrows to structural/macro triggers:
- **QT ("From QE to QT — the ultimate crisis trigger?")** — the Fed shrinking its
  balance sheet by $420 bn (2018) and $600 bn (2019); the 2018 inflection where
  global central-bank liquidity turns net negative.
- **China as "Sword of Damocles"** — "the biggest credit bubble in history";
  Chinese banking assets >600% of GDP; expansion of $20 tn post-2008 vs $4 tn for
  the US.
- **Zombie companies / Minsky Moment** — Bianco Research's zombie share at a
  20-year high.
- **Volmageddon / short-vol unwind** — the Feb 2018 mini-crash as a "taster of a
  paradigm shift."
- **Stock-bond correlation inversion** threatening risk-parity portfolios.
- **Trade war** with China.

## The credit-cycle / "trigger of the next recession" argument (2016, 2017)

**Austrian Business Cycle Theory** is named explicitly as "the most cogent and
helpful explanation" of boom-bust. The **five indications** of approaching
recession (2017): (1) rising rates, (2) artificial asset-price inflation, (3)
consumer debt + slowing credit expansion, (4) duration of the upswing, (5)
stagnating tax revenues.

Key data: **16 of 19 US rate-hike cycles since 1914 were followed by recessions**
(Hoisington Quarterly Review). The Fed's tightening was dated from the 2013
"tapering," so by 2017 "we are already in the fourth year of the tightening cycle."
Business-cycle duration: the post-war average expansion is 61 months; the current
(at 96 months in June 2017) was the third-longest ever; "we believe it is unlikely
that the old record will be broken."

## Notable data & quotes

- **Fed recession probability (May 2017):** 0.68% — and "of 89 economists surveyed
  by Bloomberg, not a single one currently expects a GDP contraction in 2017, 2018
  or 2019."
- **VIX at a 27-year low** in 2017; last comparable level was 2007.
- **Shiller P/E (CAPE):** 29.1 — "only slightly exceeded by the manic figure posted
  in 1929 and the sheer lunacy of early 2000."
- **US broad money supply (TMS-2):** 4.34× its January 2000 level; +141% since
  January 2008; CPI +15% cumulative — "money supply has grown 9.4 times faster than
  CPI."
- **China M2/GDP:** ~250% in 2017 (vs 16% for Mexico 1993, 112% for Thailand 1996).
- **China bank balance sheet:** grew $20 tn post-2008 vs $4 tn for the US — "four
  times the Japanese GDP."
- **Dimon on QT (2018):** "We've never had QE like this before, we've never had
  unwinding like this before… it could be a little more disruptive than people
  think."
- **Mayer (2017):** "We are living in a fragile interim period between the Great
  Financial Crisis and another crisis that is likely to be no less severe."
- **Mikhailovich (2017):** "The record of fiat currencies through history, 100%, is
  eventual failure. The record of gold for 5,000 years, 100%, is lack of failure."

## Nuance — how accurate were the predictions?

The reports were **directionally right about fragility and the asymmetric monetary
response, but systematically early on timing and wrong on mechanism.**

**Misses:**
- The **2017 recession call** ("within 24 months") was wrong by ~3 years — the
  expansion ran to February 2020 (128 months), breaking the all-time longevity
  record, directly contradicting "it is unlikely that the old record will be
  broken."
- The **Austrian credit-cycle indicators** flashed red from 2016 but the
  malinvestment liquidation didn't crystallize until forced by an exogenous shock.
- The **China credit crisis** never detonated — the state's control of the banking
  system allowed extend-and-pretend through 2018-2020.
- The **Eurozone triggers** (Italy, Deutsche Bank, Grexit) did not fire.

**Hits:**
- **QT as a 2018 trigger:** largely correct — the Fed's balance-sheet runoff and
  December 2018 hike helped trigger the **Q4 2018 ~20% S&P selloff**, forcing the
  Fed pivot in early 2019.
- **Volmageddon (Feb 5, 2018):** correctly pre-identified; the "lower vol feeds
  into lower vol" self-reflexive loop (Chris Cole) was vindicated within months.
- **The monetary-policy-response prediction** ("rapid rate cuts and renewed QE")
  was almost exactly what happened in March 2020, and gold rallied dramatically
  (~$1,500 late 2019 → >$2,000 August 2020).
- **The inflation-surprise thesis** proved prescient with a long lag: the 2021-22
  spike matched the "sharp increases occur unexpectedly and in compressed time
  frames" formulation — though they expected it earlier and from monetary rather
  than fiscal+supply-shock causes.

> The actual 2020 trigger (COVID-19) was a genuine black swan — exogenous, ex-ante
> unimaginable — which ironically validated the taxonomy while bypassing every
> specific trigger they had enumerated. The Austrian credit-cycle indicators
> functioned as structural-risk alarm bells rather than reliable timing devices.
