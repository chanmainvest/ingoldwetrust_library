# Gold mining stocks

Gold mining stocks as an investment — their leveraged relationship to the gold
price (beta), the chronic underperformance that earned the sector its "problem
child" reputation, the "creative destruction" turnaround thesis, and the
structural superiority of royalty/streaming companies. A redemption arc spanning
2008–2026.

> Incrementum distinguishes between "safe-haven gold" (physical bullion for
> purchasing-power preservation) and "performance gold" (mining stocks for
> capital appreciation). Miners *should* outperform gold in bull markets — and
> they do, at the turn — but they have a long history of giving it back.

See also: [monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md).

## The core thesis

Gold mining stocks are **"performance gold"** — a leveraged equity play on the
gold price. Because a miner's revenues scale with the gold price while much of
its cost base is fixed, small gold-price moves translate into outsized moves in
earnings and equity value (in 2025, gold rose 64.4% while the GDM index rose
155.4% — an implied beta of ~2.4). But leverage cuts both ways: for most of the
period miners chronically underperformed gold, destroying roughly $500 bn of
shareholder value between 2003 and 2012 through top-of-cycle acquisitions,
runaway capex, and dilution. The recurring puzzle is *why* an asset that should
mechanically amplify gold instead lagged it — answered by company-specific
failures overwhelming the structural leverage.

## How the argument evolved

- **2008** — Setup and optimism. Introduces the HUI (Amex Gold Bugs / unhedged
  miners), XAU, and the leverage idea: unhedged miners "participate above
  average in the increase (and the fall) of gold prices."
- **2013** — The disaster becomes clear. Mining stocks are "a complete disaster,"
  punished for capital-intensive projects, risky takeovers, and rising costs.
  The BGMI/gold ratio hits its lowest in "more than 70 years." First endorsement
  of royalty/streaming as a superior structure; first flagging of all-in
  sustaining costs (AISC) at $941/oz in Q4 2012.
- **2015** — Systematic post-mortem. Lists the failure mechanisms: neglected
  exploration, debt-funded takeovers, rising costs, dilution. Cites Pollitt's
  "production per share" chart. BGMI/gold ratio at 0.4 vs a long-term mean of
  1.56. Ends on a hopeful "strongly asymmetrical risk/reward" note.
- **2016** — The **"creative destruction" thesis crystallizes.** ~30 CEOs
  replaced (Barrick, Goldcorp, Kinross), AISC discipline, write-downs. The
  Schumpeterian core: extended slumps force consolidation and efficiency, leaving
  survivors "leaner."
- **2019** — "After the Creative Destruction, a Bull Market?" The 2011–2015
  drawdown was 83%. M&A recovered to $12.6 bn in 2018 (Barrick–Randgold,
  Newmont–Goldcorp). The airline-industry consolidation analogy (Frank Holmes).
- **2022** — Royalty & streaming gets a dedicated chapter. Over 10 years, a
  5-company R&S index rose +90% while GDX fell -41% and GDXJ -64%.
  Franco-Nevada's 40 employees generate $32.4 mn revenue each vs Newmont's
  $0.85 mn per employee.
- **2024** — Rigorous beta/valuation. GDX-to-GLD beta ranged 1.43–2.24; GDXJ
  1.64–2.65, peaking at 2.65 in the 2015–2020 bull market. Key finding: beta is
  *highest when the gold price was declining or stagnant*, which is why miners
  underperform cumulatively. "Gold mining stocks behaved like gold on steroids…
  during the good times but also during the bad ones."
- **2026** — Two culminating chapters. "The Collapse of Commodity Beta" reframes
  gold as the portfolio "goalkeeper" with miners as the "strikers" (a barbell).
  "From Problem Child to Model Student" declares victory: 2025 was "the most
  profitable year in gold mining history" — GDX EPS quadrupled ($1.05 → $4.63),
  P/E fell 30.8x → 19.8x, FCF margin jumped 4.2% → 24.5%. "The sector has learned
  from the excesses of the past."

## Key mechanisms

1. **Operating leverage / beta to gold.** The foundational mechanism, quantified
   in 2024 (betas 1.4–2.65). Nuance: beta is unstable and highest in bad periods.
2. **Capital misallocation (the "problem child" diagnosis).** Neglected
   exploration, debt-funded mega-acquisitions at cycle tops (40–50% premiums),
   "production-at-all-costs," dilution. ~$500 bn destroyed 2003–2012 (McKinsey).
3. **Creative destruction / bear-market cleansing.** Extended slumps force
   consolidation; ~30 CEO replacements, $30 bn write-downs, net debt/EBITDA
   falling 1.6x → 1.1x.
4. **Cost discipline and the AISC revolution.** The shift to all-in sustaining
   costs as a transparency benchmark (first flagged 2013 at $941/oz). Later
   nuance: exogenous costs (oil) are hedgeable, but endogenous cost inflation
   (declining ore grades) "cannot be hedged."
5. **Royalty & streaming as structurally superior.** Limited cost-overrun risk,
   optionality on exploration, capital efficiency. R&S outperformed GDX/GDXJ
   across 1/3/5/10-year windows. Recommended 2026 weighting: 40–50% royalty/senior.
6. **M&A consolidation and reserve scarcity.** 2025 was the most active M&A year
   since 2010 ($21.2 bn across 32 deals), rooted in declining discovery rates:
   202 finds (2009 peak) → 70–90/year now; cost per discovery $50 mn → $200 mn.
7. **Valuation.** The BGMI/gold ratio (record lows in 2013/2015/2019); P/S found
   most stable, D/E most regime-sensitive (2024). The "hidden call option" (2026):
   majors value reserves at $1,700–2,200/oz vs ~$4,600 spot.

## Notable data & quotes

- **Drawdowns:** 2011–2015 = -83%; BGMI/gold ratio "lowest in 70+ years" (2013),
  at 0.4 vs mean 1.56 (2015).
- **Beta (2024):** GDX-GLD 1.43–2.24; GDXJ-GLD 1.64–2.65; GDXJ briefly topped 3.5
  in March 2020.
- **R&S outperformance (2022):** 10-year R&S index +90% vs GDX -41%, GDXJ -64%.
- **2025 profitability (2026):** GDX EPS $1.05 → $4.63 (+341%); P/E 30.8x → 19.8x;
  FCF margin 4.2% → 24.5%; EBITDA margin 37.5% → 56.7%. Top 10 producers tripled
  FCF (~$9 bn → ~$27 bn).
- **M&A (2026):** $21.2 bn across 32 deals; Coeur/New Gold ($7 bn), Gold
  Fields/Gold Road ($3.69 bn), Equinox/Calibre ($2.13 bn). Premiums now 11–39%
  vs 50–80% last cycle.
- **Costa (2024):** "Gold miners, arguably the most hated industry in today's
  markets, are also at their most undervalued state in 40 years."
- **Lassonde (Franco-Nevada, 2022):** "We get a free perpetual option on the
  discoveries made on the land… and a free perpetual option on the price of gold."

## Nuance

- **Leverage is double-edged — increasingly acknowledged.** The 2024 chapter is
  candid that beta is higher in bad periods, so cumulatively miners
  underperformed gold. The "problem child" framing is never fully retired.
- **Is the turnaround real?** The 2013/2015 optimism ("lip service or not?") was
  premature — the cleanup took until ~2019–2022 to show through. The 2024 chapter
  still calls the sector "unloved, undervalued, underowned" before the 2025
  vindication.
- **Royalty/streaming vs classical miners.** The corpus prefers R&S on a
  risk-adjusted basis but concedes lower leverage — resolved in 2026 via the
  barbell portfolio (40–50% R&S/seniors, 15–20% juniors, 5–10% explorers).
- **Active vs passive.** A strong late position: mining stocks are "anything but
  buy-and-hold investments" (2015), culminating in the proprietary Active Aurum
  Signal (active +16,279% vs passive +1,477% since 1971).
