# Demographics, generations & the commodity supercycle

Why demographics turned inflationary, the Strauss-Howe Fourth Turning / Turchin
elite-overproduction framework, and the capex/commodity-bull argument that
follows. The structural-inflation drivers behind the corpus's macro thesis.

> "The young and old (dependents) are inflationary whereas the working age
> population is disinflationary." — Juselius & Takáts (2021)

## 1. Demographics turn inflationary (2021)

The thesis (drawn from Goodhart & Pradhan's *The Great Demographic Reversal* and
Juselius & Takáts): the post-1990 "Great Moderation" of inflation was **not
primarily a monetary-policy achievement but a one-time, unrepeatable demographic
and geopolitical supply shock** that is now reversing.

**The disinflationary tailwind (1980s-2010s):**
- The opening of China and reintegration of Eastern Europe **doubled the labor
  supply** available to the advanced economies (1991-2018). Eastern Europe alone
  added 209 million working-age people.
- China's dependency ratio collapsed from ~80 (mid-1970s) to a historic low of
  **36 in 2010** — a function of the Great Leap Forward famine, the 1963-73 baby
  boom, and the One-Child Policy.
- China's undervalued yuan meant China was "effectively exporting price deflation
  to the rest of the world."

**The structural reversal (now inflationary):**
- Juselius & Takáts (22 advanced economies, 1955-2014): "the young and old are
  inflationary whereas the working age population is disinflationary." Mechanism:
  workers produce more than they consume; dependents consume but do not produce.
- China's over-60 share rises from 17% → **25% by 2030 → ~35% by mid-century**;
  dependency ratio 41% → **50% (2030) → 75% (mid-century)**. China's working-age
  population has already peaked.
- Globally, the over-80 cohort will **triple from 137 million (2017) to 425 million
  by 2050**.
- **No replacement dividend exists.** India lacks China's administrative
  coordination; Africa faces geographic barriers. The global manufacturing complex
  is "ageing together," foreclosing the Japan-style outsourcing escape valve.
- **The Japan refutation:** Japan's low inflation is explained by its being the
  chief beneficiary of the China dividend, *and* broad money growth averaged only
  2.9%/yr 2000-2020 vs 5.5% (euro) and 6.2% (US). QE alone is "at most anti-
  deflationary."

## 2. The generational / Fourth Turning argument (2021)

**Strauss-Howe Fourth Turning.** The US entered the Crisis/Fourth Turning circa
2005-2008, with resolution due in the 2020s. Strauss & Howe's 1996 prophecy:
"Sometime before the year 2025, America will pass through a great gate in history,
commensurate with the American Revolution, Civil War, and twin emergencies of the
Great Depression and World War 2." Neil Howe: "History is seasonal, and winter is
coming."

**Peter Turchin — elite overproduction.** A mismatch between real power and the
number of people *feeling entitled* to it produces counter-elites who mobilize
dispossessed commoners (Trump as archetype). The "sitting elite" attempts "bread
and circuses" via ex-nihilo money creation, which consumes capital and accelerates
the downfall. "The Fourth Turning and elite overproduction views seamlessly
substantiate each other."

**The generational handoff.** Millennials (first cohort born 1982) will be "over 40
by the 2024 election and experienced politicians and business leaders by 2028."
They carry unsustainable student debt, cannot exceed their parents' material
well-being (the Elephant Curve shows the Western middle class as the loser of
globalization), and have "a sense of great injustice in a system rigged against
them." A 2019 Gallup poll shows socialism and capitalism "equally popular" among
Millennials. "These are the forces that shape a generation of soon to be leaders."

**The "one generational leap" thesis.** The monetary order changes in discrete
generational jumps (classical gold standard → interwar gold exchange → Bretton
Woods → post-1971 fiat). The next leap is due in the 2020s. The likely destination
is **NOT a return to a rigid gold standard** but an **MMT-esque system with closed
capital accounts and managed exchange rates** — a "Bretton Woods III" — possibly
consolidating into **three geopolitical blocs** (North American/USD, European/euro,
Pacific/RMB). "A new monetary world order must be anchored to something tangible,
and that tangible asset is likely to be gold" — specifically a **gold-backed
digital currency**.

## 3. The capex / commodity-bull argument (2023)

**The setup — a decade-plus of capex suppression.** Oil & gas investments fell
**>50%, from $700 bn (2014) to ~$300 bn (2021)** (Saudi Aramco's Amin Nasser). The
Net Zero Banking Alliance tripled to 122 banks = **40% of global banking assets**,
with members halting new oil/gas financing. Result: lowest new oil & gas discoveries
in 75 years; zero new greenfield copper project approvals.

**The "age of emergencies" demand shock** — rearm, reshore, energy transition:
- **Rearm:** the Ukraine war redirected capital from discretionary tech toward
  commodities and infrastructure.
- **Reshore:** deglobalization compromises "unfettered access to critical minerals."
- **Energy transition:** the US **Inflation Reduction Act (~$370 bn)** and EU
  **REPowerEU (>€1 trn renewables + €700 bn grids by 2030)**.

**Why commodities are the beneficiaries:**
- **Copper:** the world needs **8 Escondida-sized projects in 8 years**; supply gap
  ~6 mn t/yr, with a 4.7 mn-tonne annual deficit still probable by 2030. Lead times
  up to **15 years**.
- **Battery metals:** past-5-year supply growth (Li +6%/yr) is dwarfed by IEA
  net-zero-2050 demand projections (Li +30%/yr).
- **Labor bottleneck:** 92% of US solar PV developers report difficulty finding
  qualified labor; Germany forecasts a **5-million-worker shortage by 2030**.
- **Oil & gas resurgence:** global upstream capex **+39% in 2022 to $499 bn**
  (highest since 2014); global oil demand a record **102 mn bpd (2023)**.

**Gold's fit.** When the **S&P GSCI/Dow ratio fell below 50** (only at 1929, late
1960s, late 1990s), a major commodity bull market followed. Goehring & Rozencwajg:
a 25/25/25/25 energy/metals/precious-metals/agriculture portfolio returned **+122%
(1929-40), +400% (1970s-80), +360% (1999-2010)**. "A commodities supercycle only in
its first or second innings."

## Synthesis

The three chapters form a single causal chain: **demographics** structurally end
the disinflationary era → the **generational/Fourth-Turning handoff** supplies the
political will for a new monetary order → the **capex/commodity supercycle**
supplies the real-asset sink into which the resulting inflation and currency
debasement flows. Gold is the constant: named as the tangible anchor of the
post-crisis monetary order, and as the precious-metals leg of the commodity
portfolio that historically outperforms during such regime-change windows.
