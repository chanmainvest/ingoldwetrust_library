# Central bank gold

Central bank gold behavior — the flip from net selling to record net buying, the
physical migration of gold from West to East, and the modern repatriation wave —
is treated as a leading structural indicator of gold's slow-motion remonetization
and the erosion of the post-1971 fiat/US-dollar order.

> Central banks flipped to net buyers around 2010 after roughly 50 years as net
> sellers, and in the 2020s became the single most important demand segment for
> gold. Gold is returning "by function, not by revolution, but by evolution"
> (2026).

See also: [de_dollarization](de_dollarization.md), [china_gold](china_gold.md),
[gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md).

## The core thesis

Central bank buying signals growing distrust of the USD-denominated reserve
system, the weaponization of fiat reserves, and the absence of a trustworthy
monetary anchor. No classical gold standard is predicted — gold is regaining
monetary relevance as the ultimate reference asset because "the weaknesses of
the alternatives are becoming apparent" (2026). Central bank buying is the
structural floor under the gold price.

## How the argument evolved

- **2009** — Still the Washington Agreement / Central Bank Gold Agreement (CBGA)
  regime: Western central banks capping *sales*. China sits at just 1.6% gold
  coverage. The thesis that emerging markets need to "catch up" to a 10% coverage
  ratio (~12,799 t required) is seeded.
- **2010 — The CBGA3 signing and the de-hedging squeeze.** The third Central
  Bank Gold Agreement (Sept 2009) reset the annual sale ceiling to 400 t, and
  "for the first time since 1988, central banks were net buyers of gold again"
  — the inflection point. Simultaneously, producer de-hedging (Barrick's
  $5.6 bn closure of its hedge book) removed a structural overhang that had
  capped the price for a decade.
- **2011** — The declared inflection: central banks became net buyers in 2010
  "for the first time in two decades," acquiring 90 t; Q1 2011 alone hit 129 t.
  Mexico bought 93.3 t; Russia planned 100 t/year. CBGA sales had effectively
  dried up (only 6.2 t).
- **2012** — "Renaissance" language: "the last time central banks bought as much
  gold as they did last year was in 1964." 455 t bought in 2011 vs 77 t in 2010.
  Framed as emerging-market "catching up" to industrialized nations' allocations.
- **2020** — Cultural deepening: Asia holds gold as "strong hands"; gold is money
  in Asia (5,000-year memory), "paper gold" is a Western phenomenon. Russia at
  2,251.89 t; Turkey quadrupled reserves 2017–2019 (116 t → 412 t).
- **2024** — Quantitative crescendo: central banks bought **1,082 t (2022)** and
  **1,037 t (2023)** — >35% of total mine supply (3,644 t). Aggregate official
  gold hit an ATH of 35,927 t. Since 2010, Russia/China/Türkiye alone added 3,289
  t (62% of total purchases). The BIS 2020 paper anchors the optimal share at 10%.
- **2025** — Repatriation matures as a distinct sub-thesis: "if it's my gold, I
  want it in my country" (Invesco). The freezing of Russian FX reserves
  ($258 bn, 2022) is the defining catalyst. Buying and repatriation are now
  "inextricably linked."
- **2026** — Theoretical peak: central banks bought 863 t in 2025 worth a record
  $95.2 bn; **95% of central banks expect reserves to rise with zero expecting a
  decline** (first time ever). Gold is now an "active balance-sheet tool" —
  "silent remonetization without the political cost of debt issuance." The Six
  Vectors framework synthesizes the channels.

## Key mechanisms

1. **East-vs-West flow / "strong hands."** Gold physically migrates from Western
   selling centers (London, NYC) to price-insensitive, culturally embedded Asian
   accumulation. "Asia's strong hands won't be caught out by a rising price"
   (2020). By 2026: a "dual structure" of Western pricing and Eastern accumulation.
2. **Reserve diversification / the 10% target.** From the 2009 table (12,799 t
   to reach 10%) to the BIS-anchored 2024 logic. ANZ projects ~609 t/year by
   2030 if EMs hit 10%; China alone would need 2,643 t.
3. **Repatriation as lack-of-trust / sanctions hedge.** The definitive treatment
   in 2025. "Reserves that are not physically controlled are not reserves in an
   emergency" (2026) — the post-Feb-2022 rule. Venezuela 160 t (2011), Germany
   674 t (2013–17), Netherlands 122.5 t (2014), Austria 90 t, Poland 100 t
   (2019), Hungary 94.5 t, India 215 t (2022–24).
4. **Gold as silent balance-sheet recapitalization.** Bundesbank revaluation
   reserve hit EUR 387 bn (end-2025); Eurosystem gold reserves EUR 1,274 bn
   (2025) vs EUR 872 bn (2024). US gold booked at $42.22/oz leaves a ~$1.2 trn
   revaluation gap.
5. **"Conviction buyers" vs "opportunistic buyers" (2026).** A new analytical
   frame: central banks + Western ETF investors are price-insensitive conviction
   buyers who set direction; EM households are price-sensitive opportunistic
   buyers who set the floor. "Conviction buyers set the price. Opportunistic
   buyers set the floor" (Goldman Sachs).
6. **The "six vectors of remonetization" (2026).** (I) reserves/sanction-resistance
   + petrogold; (II) private/institutional (pension funds <2% gold); (III)
   balance-sheet recapitalization; (IV) gold-backed bonds (Judy Shelton's
   Treasury Trust Bonds, targeted July 4, 2026); (V) Western CBs turn buyers
   (Canada = "monetary self-neglect"; Czech Republic 9x since 2022); (VI)
   digitalization/tokenization.
7. **Signaling economic might.** NBER's "Central Banks and Gold Puzzles": holding
   gold "signals global power." Poland's Glapiński: "Gold symbolizes the strength
   of the country."

## Notable data & quotes

- **2011:** Net buyers first time in two decades; Mexico +93.3 t, Russia +154 t.
- **2012:** 455 t bought (highest since 1964). The Bank of England "Brown bottom"
  — 395 t sold 1999–2002 at avg $275 — called the "ultimate contrary indicator."
- **2020:** Russia 2,251.89 t (7.25% of GDP vs US ~2%); Turkey 116 t → 412 t in
  three years.
- **2024:** CBs bought 1,082 t (2022) and 1,037 t (2023). PBoC +225 t in 2023
  (largest single-year since ≥1977); Nieuwenhuijs estimates the PBoC really bought
  735 t, with true holdings of 5,385 t. Poland +130 t (Apr–Nov 2023).
- **2025:** 863 t bought, worth record $95.2 bn. Poland +102 t; 95% of CBs expect
  reserves to rise, 0% a decline.
- **Repatriation (2025):** Share of global official gold NOT held at the Fed/BoE
  rose from 51% (1972) to 78% (2024) — "a proxy for the West's decline in
  financial dominance."
- **Glapiński (NBP, 2026):** "We will keep buying gold until we reach 700 tons…
  A country that borders a war zone is subject to special scrutiny."
- **Currie/Goldman (2026):** "US dollar recycling is increasingly becoming gold
  recycling."

## Nuance

- **Russia turns seller (2025–2026).** A decades-long buyer became a net seller
  (-6 t in 2025, then -15 t in early 2026) to finance the war — a wrinkle on the
  "CBs only accumulate" narrative.
- **China cannot buy openly.** If China signaled a push to 10%, it would drive
  the price against itself and signal USD no-confidence, so buying must be covert
  (2012, 2020). Disclosure gaps mean much of the data is estimated.
- **"Strong hands" vs price-sensitivity (2020 vs 2026).** The 2020 "anticyclical
  buyer" claim is later corrected: EM households *are* price-sensitive (jewelry
  demand fell ~25% in 2025). The 2026 opportunistic-buyer frame formalizes this.
