# War on cash & CBDCs

The war on cash, deep negative interest rates, and programmable digital money —
and why abolishing cash is the prerequisite for unconstrained financial
repression. Gold (and Bitcoin) as the escape valves.

> "The war on cash is over and the government won." — Jim Rickards (epigraph,
> 2016 & 2017)

See also: [sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md),
[monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md),
[bitcoin_vs_gold](bitcoin_vs_gold.md), [gold_storage_custody](gold_storage_custody.md).

## The core thesis

**Cash is the last exit from the fiat-money system, and abolishing it is the
prerequisite for deep negative interest rates and unconstrained financial
repression.** Once cash is gone, savers cannot escape negative rates by
withdrawing banknotes, so the state/banking cartel can push the deposit rate as
far negative as it wants (2016). The €500 note abolition is treated as the first
concrete step (2016, 2017).

The mechanism is stated directly: "an effective implementation of negative
interest rates would require a comprehensive cash ban affecting all banknotes"
(2016). The IMF's "Macroeconomics of De-cashing" paper concedes de-cashing would
be "critical for the efficiency" of negative-rate policy (2017).

Gold is positioned throughout as the asset immune to state manipulation, with a
stock-to-flow ratio (~1:54) that makes its supply expand at ~1.75%/yr — matching
long-run productivity (2020). Bitcoin enters in 2017 as a complementary escape
asset.

## How the argument evolved

- **2016 — The foundational tactical case.** Catalogs the lobby groups
  (Better-Than-Cash-Alliance), Davos statements, and false pretexts (crime,
  germs, cost). Identifies negative interest rates as "the most important reason
  for the abolition of cash" and the "closing the emergency exit" logic.
- **2017 — "Enters the next round."** Adds quantitative Sweden data (only 2% of
  payments in cash; 900 of 1,600 bank branches hold no cash), the IMF de-cashing
  paper, and the "salami tactics" framing. Debunks the crime/terror pretext
  empirically.
- **2020a — "Monetary endgame ahead."** Zooms out to the systemic endgame:
  centralized money → falling growth → rising debt → "new feudalism" of debt
  serfs → inevitable covert default via inflation/QE-for-the-people. Frames
  negative rates and QE as absurdities that took <10 years to become
  "conventional."
- **2020b — "From money to gold."** The theoretical interlude (via Argentarius):
  money is an abstract *right* to goods, not a thing; fiat creation is
  cloakroom-token fraud; the Cantillon effect is the redistribution mechanism.
- **2021 — "How bankers turned money into 'Σ 0 ∞ € ¥'."** The deepest mechanism
  chapter. The pivotal claim: **commercial banks, not central banks, create ~97%
  of money** (Bank of England), and did so via ever-more-abstract formats until
  2007 broke the chain of "promises of promises." Post-Covid, politicians entered
  via guaranteed lending. This explains why QE did not cause hyperinflation.
- **2022 — "Why does fiat money seemingly work?"** The theoretical defense: fiat
  works via coercion (legal-tender laws + tax-payment demand) and propaganda,
  reinforced by the fact that debtors (the majority) prefer inflation.
  "Everybody is happy." Traces the lineage from Roman coin-clipping → tally sticks
  → John Law → modern central banking. "Every fiat currency system in history has
  failed, except the current one, which has not failed yet."

## Key mechanisms

1. **Cash as the escape hatch from negative rates.** Without cash, savers cannot
   withdraw to avoid negative deposit rates. Rogoff: "the existence of cash gums
   up the works."
2. **Salami tactics / incremental de-cashing.** Abolition by degrees
   (high-denomination note removal, cash-payment caps, transaction fees).
3. **Crime/terror pretexts are empirically false.** 75% of European jihadist
   attacks cost <$10,000; a cash ban cuts illicit employment only ~10% and
   organized crime <5% (Schneider). The real beneficiaries are payment-
   processors and banks.
4. **Closing the emergency exit / bank-run prevention.** Fractional-reserve banks
   are in "permanent latent insolvency"; cash abolition removes the bank-run
   threat and the pre-crisis cash-demand warning signal (as before Lehman 2008).
5. **The Cantillon effect.** New money redistributes wealth to early receivers,
   creating a "reverse redistribution" and "new feudalism" of creditors vs. debt
   serfs.
6. **Commercial banks create ~97% of money, not central banks** (2021). The
   eurodollar grew from 15% of M1 (1969) to 175% (1979); money became repo-
   collateral, then Gaussian-copula mathematics. This explains the missing
   post-QE inflation.
7. **Fiat persists via coercion + propaganda** (2022). Two pillars: legal-tender
   laws and tax-payment demand. Gold is pushed out of circulation by Gresham's law
   but returns when fiat fails.

## Notable data & quotes

- **Sweden (2017):** only **2% of payments in cash**; Riksbank projects **0.5% by
  end of decade**; **900 of 1,600 bank branches hold no cash**; the "Swish" app
  downloaded 5.5 million times.
- **Purchasing-power loss (2020):** the USD lost **96.5% of purchasing power,
  1913–2019**; destined to lose another 34% over 20 years at the 2% target.
- **Gold stock-to-flow (2020):** ~3,560 t annual mine output vs ~193,000 t
  above-ground stock = **1:54**.
- **Debt supercycle (2020):** **$1.4 debt → $4.3 debt per $1 of GDP** (1971 →
  2020).
- **Eurodollar scale (2021):** Friedman's 1969 estimate $30 bn (15% of M1) → FRBNY
  1979 estimate $900 bn (175% of M1).
- **The "Σ 0 ∞ € ¥" framing (2021):** money's metamorphosis traced in four stages
  — 'Mon€y' → 'M 0 n € ¥' → 'Σ 0 n € ¥' → 'Σ 0 ∞ € ¥'. Greenspan (FOMC, 2000):
  "we cannot… locate money. And that has become an increasingly dubious
  proposition."
- **Rogoff (2017):** "the existence of cash gums up the works."
- **Keynes (1920, cited 2020):** "There is no subtler, no surer means of
  overturning the existing basis of society than to debauch the currency."
- **Friedman (cited 2022):** "Inflation is the one form of taxation that can be
  imposed without legislation."

## Nuance

- **Digital payments genuinely reduce transaction costs.** The 2017 chapter
  concedes part of cash's decline is organic market choice, not just repression —
  the Swish app is a real convenience.
- **The IMF arguments are engaged on their merits before being rebutted** (2017) —
  it is a debate, not a strawman.
- **The "everybody is happy" concession (2022)** is the sharpest internal tension:
  the authors admit fiat "works" because banks, politicians, debtors, and welfare
  dependents all profit from it. The system has real political staying power,
  undercutting any simple "imminent collapse" claim.
- **CBDCs are notably not named explicitly in these chapters.** The
  programmable/controllable-money theme is built *implicitly* through the
  de-cashing + negative-rates + government-guaranteed-lending logic. (The
  tokenization/CBDC-by-name material lives in the 2026
  [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md) / six-vectors chapters.)
