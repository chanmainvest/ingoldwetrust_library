# ESG & responsible mining

ESG (environmental, social, governance) as a material financial factor for the
gold mining sector — a capital-allocation gatekeeper, not a soft ethical add-on —
and the counter-intuitive "gold as ESG-positive" thesis.

> ESG is "three words worth more than USD 20 trillion." (2019)

See also: [gold_mining_stocks](gold_mining_stocks.md),
[monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md).

## The core thesis

**ESG now determines which gold miners survive**, via three pillars:

1. **Access to capital and license to operate as the binding constraint.** Because
   mining is capital-intensive and underfinanced, ESG scores function as a
   gatekeeper to cheap capital, credit ratings, and project permits. "As ESG
   scores become an important part of their credit ratings, it becomes essential
   that mining companies make ESG compliance a top priority" (2019). Bank of
   America: "70% of US assets can't be analyzed without using ESG now" (2020).
2. **The tension between ESG demands and rising gold demand.** Gold demand is
   rising (central-bank buying, inflation hedging), but new supply is harder to
   find — ore grades have halved since 2000 (Bristow, 2026); exploration takes
   ~40% longer than 15 years ago (Accenture, 2026).
3. **Gold itself as ESG-positive — the "gold as a savior" framing.** Gold is "the
   most sustainable mineral on Earth": infinitely recyclable, virtually all
   190,000 t ever mined still accessible, near-zero Scope 3 emissions. Adding
   10-20% gold to a portfolio cuts its carbon intensity by 7-17% (WGC, 2022).

## How the argument evolved

- **2019 — ESG as an emerging trend/opportunity.** Argues gold miners — currently
   poorly ESG-rated — have the most *upside* from improving ESG. Companion chapter
   on disruptive innovation (AI, drones, blockchain traceability). *Caveat:*
   "ESG compliance ratings may be subjective, and the system clearly has flaws;
   but since it is now mandatory, companies will have to comply."
- **2020 — ESG and financial stability.** Pivots to central banks and regulators:
   TCFD, EU Taxonomy, green bonds as financing routes. Introduces the WGC
   Responsible Gold Mining Principles (RGMPs) in depth (Terry Heymann
   interview).
- **2022 — "Gold as a Savior."** COP26 aftermath. Introduces the "All-In Emissions
   Cost" (AIEC) metric and argues gold is a portfolio decarbonizer. The most
   explicitly pro-gold ESG framing — gold as a hedge *against* the inflationary
   costs of the green transition itself.
- **2023 — Standards + the "S" of ESG.** Deliberately rebalances from the
   over-emphasized "E" to the social pillar. Introduces resource nationalism and
   the Lassonde-curve life cycle of a mining project.
- **2024 — The innovation frontier (asteroid & deep-sea mining).** Asks whether
   new sources threaten the gold price — concludes they largely do not (gold is
   not the priority for either).
- **2026 — AI in mining + "Innovate or Terminate."** ESG is now embedded *inside*
   an innovation imperative: AI, electrification, dry-stack tailings, thiosulfate
   leaching. The framing shifts from "ESG as constraint" to "ESG as the forcing
   function for technological transformation."

## Key mechanisms

1. **ESG as capital-allocation gatekeeper.** ESG scores feed credit ratings, index
   inclusion, and fund flows; high-ESG borrowers are ~half as likely to default
   (2022).
2. **WGC Responsible Gold Mining Principles (RGMPs).** Released September 2019;
   cover governance, environment, and social pillars with independent assurance.
3. **Cyanide/mercury in artisanal mining.** A reputational drag; conflict gold
   <1% of total supply (Heymann, 2020). The industry's own remediation:
   thiosulfate leaching (Barrick's Goldstrike, 2015) and dry-stack tailings.
4. **Gold in clean-tech / as a portfolio decarbonizer.** Scope 3 near-zero; zero
   holding emissions — "potentially the only truly neutral emissions asset"
   (2022).
5. **Life cycle of a mining project (Lassonde curve).** Only ~10% of deposits
   warrant development, <0.1% of prospected sites reach production. Discovery cost
   rose from $45 mn (1980s) to $178 mn (2010s).
6. **Innovation/automation to offset declining ore grades.** A continuous thread:
   IoT/autonomous trucks (2019) → agentic AI running mines autonomously (2026).
   Predictive maintenance cuts downtime up to 50% (McKinsey).
7. **Traceability / blockchain provenance.** Track gold from mine to vault; enables
   conflict-free, ESG-screened gold and a 3-5% price premium.

## Notable data & quotes

- **ESG AUM:** ">USD 20 trn AUM worldwide" (2019); "USD 30 trn ESG-compliant"
  (2020); BofA forecast of "another USD 20 trn into ESG funds over the next two
  decades — a 'tsunami of assets'" (2020). UNPRI: ~4,000 signatories, USD 120 trn
  AUM (2022).
- **Gold's emissions advantage:** <1% of supply-chain emissions are downstream
  (Heymann, 2020); gold generates USD 2,500+ of value per tonne CO2 vs USD 900 for
  the S&P 500 (2019); 10% gold allocation cuts portfolio carbon intensity 7%, 20%
  cuts it 17% (WGC, 2022).
- **Ore grades:** "now half of what it was in 2000" (Bristow, 2026).
- **AI in mining (2026):** AI-in-mining market USD 29.94 bn (2024) → USD 685.61 bn
  (2033), 41.87% CAGR. AI can cut exploration drilling 25-30%, discovery time 40%.
- **Asteroid/deep-sea (2024):** 16 Psyche asteroid popularly valued at "USD
  10,000 quadrillion" (flagged as unreliable); deep-sea commercialization
  expected ~2030. Conclusion: gold price "largely insulated" from both.
- **Steen Jakobson (2022):** "the ESG and green transformation is simply the single
  largest policy bet ever undertaken, and the main consequences will be inflation
  and ever lower real rates."

## Nuance — genuine or greenwashing?

The reports treat ESG as **genuine and financially material rather than
greenwashing** — but they are not naive:

- Ratings flaws are conceded (2019): Vale held a *top-tier* ESG rating through
  the 2015 Mariana dam disaster and was only downgraded after the 2019 Brumadinho
  collapse.
- The 2020 chapter explicitly warns the EU Taxonomy "will also lead to partial
  greenwashing of projects."
- Miner decarbonization is driven "not solely to reduce emissions but mainly for
  commercial reasons" (Heymann, 2020) — presented as a *strength* (it makes the
  change durable), not a critique.

**Tension with the gold-bull thesis is real.** The reports are structurally
pro-gold-mining-investment, yet ESG divestment pressures could "make it harder
for institutional players — especially pension funds — to invest in gold"
(Stöferle, 2020). The resolution: by 2022 ESG is reframed as a *bull case* for
gold (the portfolio-decarbonizer argument), and by 2026 it is subsumed into the
innovation narrative. The broadly libertarian, anti-nationalization leanings
surface in 2023 (Hayek's *Road to Serfdom* is quoted) — a preference for
community part-ownership and market-based solutions over top-down ESG mandates.
