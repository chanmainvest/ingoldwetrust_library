# China & gold

China's systematic accumulation of gold — openly via the Shanghai Gold Exchange
(SGE) and covertly via the PBoC — as a strategic pillar of its rise. The cultural
"love affair," the East-vs-West physical flow, and China's role in the
multipolar monetary order.

> "Gold goes where the money is" (James Steel). China's domestic gold never
> leaves the country — the world's largest producer is also a net importer.

See also: [de_dollarization](de_dollarization.md),
[central_bank_gold](central_bank_gold.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md).

## The core thesis

China is accumulating gold as a strategic pillar, motivated by (a) a deep
cultural affinity, (b) the desire to diversify away from and ultimately challenge
US dollar hegemony, and (c) the ambition to internationalize the renminbi and
anchor a multipolar monetary order. Gold flows West-to-East. The SGE is framed as
the institutional mechanism that "reopened the gold window" closed in 1949,
creating physical price discovery that constrains Western paper-market selloffs.
The PBoC is portrayed as buying in secret, dipping anti-cyclically, and
underreporting — accumulating far more than its disclosed tonnage.

## How the argument evolved

- **2008** — Almost entirely supply-side. China overtakes South Africa as the
  world's largest gold miner (276 t vs 272 t in 2007). The SGE (founded 2002) is
  a deregulation milestone; private ownership was illegal 1949–1982. Key claim:
  China and Russia are net importers *despite* being top producers.
- **2009** — Pivot to reserve/monetary framing. Wen Jiabao and Zhou Xiaochuan
  publicly criticize the USD. China discloses reserves of **1,054 t** (up from
  600 t), admitting covert purchases over six years. First "post-dollar era"
  language.
- **2010** — Adds the cultural/strategic-depth layer. Target of 6,000 t in 3–5
  years and 10,000 t in 10 years (Ji Xianonan). Cheng Siwei's famous "China is
  buying the dips" quote. First mention of a "Beijing put" on gold.
- **2012** — The demand-side paradigm shift: emerging markets = 70% of physical
  demand, China + India = >50%. The income-affordability argument: in CNY/INR
  terms gold is ~80% cheaper than in 1980. The "Chindia" structural bid.
- **2014** — Sharpens the reserve-currency/gold-backing argument. PBoC's Zhang
  Jianhua: "The only choice to hedge risks is to hold hard currency — gold."
  Estimate: China secretly holds 4,000–6,000 t. Nuance: China is **not** pursuing
  a gold standard, only hedging USD reserves.
- **2019** — The magisterial / civilizational treatment. Deep historical
  digression: paper money under Kublai Khan (gold-backed), the silver standard
  (~500 years), the Opium Wars and "unequal treaties." The SGE detailed as a
  physical-price-discovery corrective to the LBMA. BullionStar estimate: ~23,000
  t total Chinese gold.
- **2024** — Crisis / Japanification frame. Real estate collapse, demographics.
  But private demand booms: 1,000 t+ bought in 2023. SGE delivers a record 271 t
  in Jan 2024. Total Chinese gold "probably more than 30,000 t." First explicit
  framing of private gold as a latent state resource ("China holds gold through
  its people").
- **2025** — System-rivalry climax. China now conducts >50% of trade in RMB (vs
  3.7% in 2012). Gold share of China-bloc reserves rose ~1% → 7%. Nieuwenhuijs
  estimate: actual PBoC reserves ~5,000 t (buys ~3x what it reports).

## Key mechanisms

1. **Cultural affinity & "strong hands" (countercyclical buying).** Centuries of
   tradition; when Western paper prices fall, Shanghai physical demand surges,
   putting a floor under selloffs. Made in 2008, 2010, 2012, 2019, 2024.
2. **SGE = reopened gold window / physical price discovery.** Founded 2002;
   higher purity (99.99%), gram-denominated, mostly physical delivery. Functions
   as a corrective on LBMA/COMEX paper markets (2008, 2019, 2024).
3. **Covert PBoC accumulation.** Buys from non-SGE sources (400 oz bars),
   delays/withholds disclosure. 2009 disclosure of 454 t covertly bought; 2014
   estimate of 4,000–6,000 t; 2025 Nieuwenhuijs estimate of ~5,000 t actual with
   ~3x underreporting.
4. **Reserve diversification / hedge against USD.** China's share of US
  Treasuries fell from 27% (Mar 2007) to 11% (early 2009); gold as a hedge against
   the enormous USD reserve stock (2009, 2014, 2025).
5. **Capital controls limit RMB internationalization — gold as the workaround.**
   Closed capital account; full convertibility "inconceivable anytime soon"
   (2019). Physical outflows from London spike whenever CNY/USD nears 7.0.
6. **Petroyuan / de-dollarization.** CNY oil futures in Shanghai, BRI
   (CNY-denominated trade). RMB share of China's trade 3.7% (2012) → >50% (2024).
7. **Belt & Road as a gold-acquisition vehicle.** BRI region holds ~half of global
   gold production/consumption; Chinese miners active across Russia, Africa,
   Central Asia. The Silk Road Gold Fund (2019).
8. **Private gold as latent state resource.** The export ban persists; in crisis
   the state can call in private gold (2024, 2025). "China holds gold through its
   people" (PBoC, 2015).

## Notable data & quotes

- **2008:** China 276 t production (2007) — first time #1; demand +20% to 326 t.
- **2009:** Official reserves **1,054 t** (+76% since 2003). "China now owns more
  gold than Switzerland."
- **2010:** Per-capita 0.26 g (vs ~1 g USA/Japan); if 1.3 bn Chinese bought 1 g
  each = 1,300 t. Ji Xianonan target: **10,000 t in 10 years**.
- **2012:** Greater China demand 811 t (23.5% global) vs 293 t (8.8%) in 2000
  (+177%).
- **2014:** *"No asset is safe now. The only choice to hedge risks is to hold
  hard currency — gold."* — Zhang Jianhua (PBoC).
- **2019:** BullionStar estimate: **~23,000 t total Chinese gold**; per-capita
  still <0.5 oz vs Germany's 3 oz. Jewelry demand 3-yr high of 652 t (2018).
- **2024:** PBoC bought ~225 t in 2023; 18 consecutive months of buying. SGE
  withdrawals 1,205 t (2017) → 2,055 t (2023); **record 271 t in Jan 2024**.
  Charles Gave: "the price of gold is going to tell you who is going to win that
  effort to dedollarize Asia."
- **2025:** Official reserves **2,292 t** (Q1 2025), more than doubled since Q1
  2012. Nieuwenhuijs: actual reserves **~5,000 t** (world #2).

## Nuance

- **Walk-back on the yuan-as-reserve-currency timeline.** Early chapters are
  bullish on imminent RMB ascendancy; 2014 tempers it ("change in the currency
  system is a long-term process"); 2019 is blunt: "it is inconceivable that the
  yuan will replace the US dollar… anytime soon"; 2025 concedes the RMB "remains
  a marginal player." The thesis survives as "serious alternative / multipolar,"
  not "replacement."
- **No gold standard coming.** 2014 explicitly walks back the gold-bug
  expectation: China increases gold "to build up a hedge against its enormous
  dollar reserves," not to introduce a gold standard that would sacrifice monetary
  flexibility.
- **Price-sensitivity, with a 2023 anomaly.** Chinese demand is usually
  countercyclical, but in 2023 they "bought into rising prices" — a crisis-driven
  behavioral shift (real-estate collapse driving diversification). 2025 confirms
  price sensitivity at the margin: jewelry demand fell ~25% on high prices even
  as bar/coin demand rose 20%.
- **Gold as a vote of no confidence (2025).** The latest chapter reframes private
  hoarding as a *negative* signal — dwindling confidence in Beijing's economic
  management — and raises the sovereignty question over private holdings.
- **The "30,000 t" vs "5,000 t official" ambiguity.** The corpus distinguishes
  (a) disclosed PBoC reserves (~2,292 t), (b) estimated actual PBoC reserves
  (~5,000 t), and (c) total Chinese gold including private (23,000 t in 2019;
  30,000 t+ in 2024). Conflation risk is acknowledged.
