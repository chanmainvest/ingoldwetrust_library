# Hyperinflation & the crack-up boom

Hyperinflation as a distinct monetary regime (not just "high inflation"), the
Misesian crack-up boom as its terminal logic, the historical case studies
(Weimar, Zimbabwe, Venezuela), and why the authors treat it as a tail risk /
analytical framework rather than an imminent Western forecast.

> "Despite the recent surge in awareness of the potential for a crack-up boom,
> current inflation rates, albeit elevated, lie far below the dangerous territory
> of what is considered hyperinflation." (2023)

See also: [monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md),
[sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md).

## The core thesis

**Hyperinflation is a qualitatively different regime from elevated inflation**,
marked by a loss of confidence and a flight from the currency — not merely a
higher rate of the same phenomenon. The reports anchor on the **Cagan/Hanke
threshold**: inflation qualifies as hyperinflation when the *monthly* rate
exceeds 50% (≈12,975% annualized), sustained for at least 30 consecutive days
(Hanke's refinement). The 2019 chapter (guest-authored by Steve Hanke) stresses
that casual press usage ("Iran is hyperinflating") is almost always wrong by
this rigorous standard.

**The cause is fiscal/confidence breakdown, not "too much money chasing goods."**
Hyperinflation erupts when *all conventional funding sources wither away and the
central bank's printing press becomes the sole remaining funder of government
spending* (Hanke, 2019). Reinforced by the **Tanzi Effect**: collection lags mean
elevated inflation destroys the real value of tax receipts — a doom loop. In
Yugoslavia (Jan 1994), 95% of government expenditures were financed by the
printing press.

**Gold's role.** Gold is framed as the ultimate reference point — the "monetary
constant" against which currency failure is measured. Charts consistently price
currencies in grams/milligrams of gold. Greenspan: gold is "the ultimate means of
payment... an element of stability."

## Historical cases

- **Weimar Germany (1919-1923).** The most extensively treated case (its 100-year
  anniversary in 2023). Gold price in marks: **170 Mark/oz (Jan 1919) → 87
  trillion Mark/oz (Nov 1923)**. The exchange rate went from 4.20 RM/USD (1914)
  to 4.2 billion RM/USD (Nov 1923). The German stock index rose from ~100 (1918)
  to **26,890,000,000,000** (end-1923) — yet *lost real value measured in gold*
  until the terminal phase. Reichsbank director Havenstein's August 1923 address:
  the printing press running "absolutely at capacity." Peak monthly inflation Oct
  1923: 29,500% (prices doubled every 3.7 days) — ranks only **5th-worst** ever.
- **Hungary (July 1946).** The worst ever: monthly rate **4.19 × 10¹⁶%**, prices
  doubled every **15 hours**. #1 on the Hanke-Krus table.
- **Zimbabwe.** Nov 2008 peak: 7.96 × 10¹⁰% monthly, doubling every 24.7 hours —
  #2 all-time (Hanke & Kwok). A second episode in Oct 2017 (the "58th episode").
- **Venezuela.** The sole active hyperinflation at the time of the 2019 chapter;
  29-month duration, annual rate 131,870%. Hanke's measured actual for 2018 =
  80,000% — vs. the IMF's absurd 2,500,000% "forecast."
- **Yugoslavia (Jan 1994).** #3 all-time: 313,000,000% monthly, doubling every
  1.41 days. Hanke advised the post-communist government and became a target of
  Milosevic's propaganda.
- **Argentina (Jul 1989).** 197% monthly (doubles every 19.4 days). The Tanzi
  Effect was originally derived from 1970s Argentina.
- **Lebanon (Jun 2020).** 170% monthly — a newer entry.

The **Hanke-Krus World Hyperinflation Table** catalogues 58 episodes; 21 occurred
in newly independent former-Soviet states, and almost half happened in the 1990s
from post-communist funding deficiencies.

## The crack-up boom (Mises)

The term (*Katastrophenhausse*) was popularized by Mises (an earlier use is traced
to Moriz Dub's 1922 article). Mises's definition: *"The monetary system breaks
down; all transactions in the money concerned cease; a panic makes its purchasing
power vanish altogether."*

**Mechanics — flight into real goods.** The crack-up boom is the endgame where
increasing money quantity causes a *fall in the demand for money*. People rush to
dump currency for real goods "no matter whether he needs them or not" — Weimar's
*Flucht in die Sachwerte*. Rothbard: "A frantic rush ensues to get rid of money at
all costs... prices skyrocket upward virtually to infinity."

**How it differs from ordinary inflation.** For Mises the crack-up boom is the
breakdown of the *entire economic system*, not just a price spiral. Four failure
domains: (1) loan markets seize up (Argentina 1980s: no credit >14 days); (2)
asset-price boom as a *symptom*; (3) supply chains seize; (4) consumer-price
hyperinflation — "the easiest symptom to observe," but only one of several.

## Notable data & quotes

- **Gold in marks (Weimar):** 170 Mark/oz (Jan 1919) → **87 trillion Mark/oz**
  (Nov 1923).
- **End-2022 CPI (the "below threshold" cases):** Zimbabwe 244%, Venezuela 156%,
  Argentina 95%, Turkey 64% — all *below* the 50%-monthly hyperinflation line.
- **Average lifespan of fiat currency:** ~**74 years** for currencies currently in
  circulation (2023), debunking the internet "27 years" meme. The British pound is
  longest-serving at ~1,200 years (one ounce: GBP 0.89 in 1257 vs. >GBP 1,500
  today). The euro has lost **85% vs. gold** since 1999.
- **Gold/inflation correlation (2010):** 0.48 monthly (1971-2009), rising to **0.76**
  in the high-inflation 1978-1982 window.
- **Mises (2023):** "There is no means of avoiding the final collapse of a boom
  brought about by credit expansion."
- **Greenspan (2019):** Gold is "the ultimate means of payment... Fiat money, in
  extremis, is accepted by nobody."

## Nuance — tail risk, not baseline

The authors are emphatic that Western hyperinflation is **a tail risk and an
analytical framework, not an imminent forecast**:

- The crack-up boom is deployed as Mises intended — a *trajectory that can still be
  abandoned*: "throughout the early stages, the path towards a crack-up boom can
  still be abandoned by stopping all further credit expansion."
- Hanke (2019) stresses that **no one has ever reliably forecast the magnitude or
  direction of a hyperinflation**; the IMF's Venezuela projections are ridiculed as
  unscientific "guesstimations."
- **Where they draw the line:** below the 50%-monthly threshold is "elevated
  inflation" / stagflation (the 2022 "Stagflation 2.0" thesis — a slow, corrosive,
  multi-year condition dangerous for 60/40 portfolios but categorically *not*
  hyperinflation). Hyperinflation requires near-total collapse of non-monetary
  fiscal funding plus a decisive loss of confidence — conditions the
  reserve-currency West has not remotely approached.

> Gold is positioned as the hedge that performs across the *entire* spectrum
> (deflation → elevated inflation → stagflation → tail-risk hyperinflation), which
> is why it is the reports' recurring recommendation rather than a single-regime
> bet.
