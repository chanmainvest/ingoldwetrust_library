# Bitcoin vs. gold

Bitcoin and cryptocurrencies versus gold — the "digital gold" debate, the
Hayekian competing-currencies framing, and the case for holding both as
complementary hedges against fiat debasement.

> "Bitcoin and gold are not competitors in that future — they are co-architects."
> (2026)

See also: [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md),
[monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md).

## The core thesis

Bitcoin and gold are **complementary, not competitive** — "friends, not foes"
(2018, 2019, 2022, 2026). The recurring reasoning: both cannot be inflated by a
central bank, carry no counterparty risk (no one else's liability), are easily
transferable, and represent liquid value outside the fiat system. On the "digital
gold" label: embraced cautiously early, then defended. By 2026 the firm rebuts
critics who say gold's outperformance disproves it: "This criticism confuses
similar monetary properties with identical market behavior… Two assets that share
the same monetary properties but exhibit low correlation offer genuine
diversification."

Portfolio role: gold is "a timeless constant," Bitcoin is "a growing asset"
(2018). The firm recommends combining them — low/negative correlation plus
rebalancing plus option-writing produces a superior risk-adjusted return, a thesis
they later turned into live investment products.

## How the argument evolved

- **2017** — Cautious enthusiasm. Bitcoin crosses gold's ounce price ($1,290 vs
  $1,225). Genuinely bullish: "exposure to Bitcoin is still a good decision."
  Introduces stock-to-flow (Bitcoin ~25 yrs vs gold ~64 yrs), the 21M cap, a 2–4%
  allocation. Incrementum launches a cryptofund.
- **2018** — The Hayekian framing: cryptocurrencies are a "laboratory for private
  currency competition," validating *Denationalization of Money*. Nuance from
  Hayek's nephew Zundritsch: "Hayek would still prefer gold" because of Bitcoin's
  volatility. The "Crypto: Friend or Foe?" chapter settles it: complementary.
- **2019** — Splits into stablecoins analysis and the "Stronger Together"
  portfolio strategy. Gold–Bitcoin correlation is "low and slightly positive." A
  proprietary 30/70 gold-Bitcoin rebalancing strategy with covered-call writing.
  Skeptical of gold-backed stablecoins ("all centralized… counterparty risk").
- **2020** — Full embrace of **Plan B's stock-to-flow model** as "The Holy Grail
  of Bitcoin Valuation." Forecasts $55,000 post-halving, up to $100,000 (variants).
- **2022** — "Bull market in adoption, bear market in price." The first real
  stress test: Bitcoin fell 50%+ from its Nov 2021 ~$69,000 ATH. 30-day
  Bitcoin-Nasdaq correlation hit an all-time high of 0.8. Openly questions whether
  the S2F model is "obsolete." Only 40% probability assigned to a new cycle ATH.
- **2024** — "The New Playbook." SEC approves 11 spot Bitcoin ETFs (Jan 10, 2024)
  — a "game changer" that declares victory on the legal/ban risk. Claims
  Bitcoin's S2F now exceeds gold's after the April 2024 halving, making it "the
  hardest monetary asset."
- **2026** — "Bitcoin's Role in the Monetary Future." Bitcoin is ~18, a "young
  adult." Sovereign adoption (US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, El Salvador, Iran
  paying tolls in Bitcoin), DATs (digital asset treasury companies), quantum risk
  reframed as governance risk. "The future monetary system will be anchored not in
  credit, but in collateral that cannot be printed."

## Key mechanisms

1. **Stock-to-flow / scarcity.** Gold S2F ~64; Bitcoin started ~25, projected to
   exceed gold's. Plan B formalized this (R² = 0.95, 2020). By 2024 they claim
   Bitcoin is "the hardest monetary asset." Caveat (2022, 2024, 2026): the model's
   forecasting power may be weakening as halvings have diminishing marginal impact.
2. **Correlation / diversification.** Gold–Bitcoin correlation is consistently
   "low and sometimes negative." By 2026 the 90-day correlation oscillated between
   −0.37 and +0.57, reaching **−0.88 in March 2026** — "one of its strongest
   arguments."
3. **Store of value / "no counterparty risk."** Both are "no one else's
   liability." Bitcoin's fixed 21M cap makes its supply "highly predictable." By
   2024 ~95% of all Bitcoin is mined; approaching "absolute scarcity."
4. **Hayekian competing currencies.** Cryptocurrencies realize Hayek's vision of
   private currency competition. The internet enabled a "laboratory" Hayek never
   lived to see.
5. **Stablecoins (and gold-backed stablecoins).** Promised to combine gold's
   stability with Bitcoin's digital advantages. The firm is skeptical: "Not your
   vault, not your gold."
6. **Volatility as a feature (rebalancing bonus + option premium).** Rather than
   a drawback, the 2019 strategy monetizes volatility via rule-based rebalancing
   and covered-call writing (~10–15% annualized additional return).
7. **Energy consumption.** Raised as a drawback early ("a slow energy-hogging
   database," 2017). By 2026 declared to have "lost traction" as sustainable-
   energy share rose above 50% and stranded-energy mining reframed it as
   beneficial.
8. **Adoption curve / network effects / Lindy.** S-curve framing (2022): 25% of
   US households owned Bitcoin by 2022; 90% projected ~2029. By 2026 they cite the
   USD 105 tr demographic wealth transfer (Millennials 20–25% Bitcoin ownership vs
   <5% for over-65s).

## Notable data & quotes

- **Price milestones:** March 2017 — Bitcoin ($1,290) first exceeded gold's ounce
  price ($1,225). Nov 2021 ATH ~$69,000, then halved. Oct 2025 high of
  **$126,272** (2026).
- **Plan B model targets (2020):** $55,000 (original) → $60,000–$90,000 (extended)
  → $100,000 (out-of-sample variant).
- **S2F regression (2020):** R² = 0.95; gold S2F ~63.3 (190,000 t / 3,000 t).
- **Correlation:** Gold–Bitcoin 2013–2019 range −0.3 to +0.3 (2019); Bitcoin-Nasdaq
  hit **0.8** (May 2022); gold–Bitcoin reached **−0.88** (March 2026).
- **Market cap (2017):** Bitcoin ($66 bn) was 0.5% of gold's ($7 trn).
- **Volatility:** Gold daily ~1.0–1.5%; Bitcoin 5–15% (2019). Gold annual 15–20%;
  Bitcoin 60–100% (2022).
- **ETFs (2024):** 11 spot ETFs approved Jan 10, 2024; ~$15 bn net inflows in
  first months; IBIT "fastest growing ETF in history" (Fink). By 2026, Bitcoin
  ETFs + DATs hold >14% of total supply; Strategy holds 818,334 BTC.
- **Morris (2017):** "Gold is bitcoin without electricity."
- **Dalio (2022):** "I would take the gold and sprinkle a little bitcoin on top."

## Nuance — the internal tension

There is a clear and persistent **internal tension within the gold community**,
which the firm explicitly acknowledges: "Even among gold sympathizers, crypto
proponents and critics are roughly in balance" (2018). The firm's own conviction
shows real adjustment, not a straight line:

- **Early caution (2017):** only a 2–4% allocation vs up to 20% for gold; real
  drawbacks listed (51% attack, regulation, energy, internet dependence).
- **Peak model-faith (2020):** strong S2F endorsement with specific targets.
- **The humbling (2022):** the first major recalibration. The S2F model "remained
  below expectations"; only 40% probability of a new cycle ATH; they openly list
  the possibility that "the model is obsolete." The most intellectually honest
  moment.
- **Post-ETF vindication (2024):** more confident, even self-congratulatory.
- **Sophisticated risk framing (2026):** a formal three-category risk framework
  (systemic, governance, market structure). They concede the 2025 DAT cycle was a
  speculative bubble (some DATs fell >90%) and distinguish themselves from
  maximalists.

The firm's distinctive position — neither goldbugs nor crypto-maximalists — is
consistent: they are Austrian-school monetary theorists who see both as hedges
against fiat debasement, with gold as the established anchor and Bitcoin as the
higher-volatility complement. The one genuinely unresolved tension is the S2F
model's predictive validity after 2022, which they continue to flag rather than
bury.
