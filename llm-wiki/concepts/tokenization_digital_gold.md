# Tokenization & digital gold

Gold tokenization — the promise of combining gold's stability with digital
efficiency, the counterparty-risk tension ("not your vault, not your gold"), and
the progression from early skepticism (2019) to tokenization as the "sixth vector
of remonetization" (2026).

> "Tokenization changes the plumbing, not the metal. The plumbing matters — but
> only because the metal endures." (2026)

See also: [bitcoin_vs_gold](bitcoin_vs_gold.md), [gold_as_money](gold_as_money.md),
[de_dollarization](de_dollarization.md).

## The core thesis

**Gold's singular monetary weakness is transactability and portability.** The
2026 chapter frames the paradox: gold is the oldest financial asset still in
active use, yet "the infrastructure around gold remains strikingly archaic. A
400-ounce London Good Delivery bar traded today still moves through the same
system of vaults and armored transport used in the mid-20th century. Settlement
takes days. Fractional ownership is impractical." The promise of tokenization:
instant settlement (T+0), divisibility (a ~$2 mn bar collapses to a single gram),
and frictionless global transfer.

The central tension: **"Gold's defining monetary property is the absence of
counterparty risk. Tokenization, by its very architecture, reintroduces it —
through the issuer, the custodian, the smart contract, and the legal framework."**
The recurring formulation: tokenized gold "does not create a new form of trust. It
redistributes existing trust across a different set of intermediaries."

## How the argument evolved

- **2019 — Early skepticism** (Gold vs. Bitcoin vs. Stablecoins). Gold-backed
  stablecoins treated as structurally inferior to both physical gold and ETFs. The
  verdict is blunt: "All of the gold-backed stablecoins on the market are
  centralized, which means they have counterparty risk." They openly doubt the
  value proposition: "How is a centralized gold-backed stablecoin any better than a
  gold ETF? We have still not found a suitable answer." Over 50 tokens existed;
  most predicted to fail.
- **2020 — The "glimpse into the future" survey.** A more constructive but still
  cautious tone. By March 2020, "more than 70 projects still in the race, while
  more than 30 have failed or stalled." Detailed profiles of XAUt, PAXG, PMGT, CGT.
  Notable improvement: backing has "moved towards being more trusted and
  transparent," with allocated, serialized bars viewable online. But the 2019
  caveat is restated verbatim: "no one has yet figured out how to make a truly
  decentralized gold-backed stablecoin."
- **2026 — Tokenization as the sixth vector of remonetization.** The framing
  fundamentally shifts. Tokenization is no longer a curiosity but one of six
  structural forces driving gold's remonetization (Vector VI: "Digitalization —
  Gold-Backed Tokens Mobilize Gold"). The standalone "Gold Goes Digital" chapter
  treats it as infrastructure with a three-layer model (retail, institutional,
  speculative frontier). Bullish case explicit: tokenization "begins to resolve an
  age-old dilemma" and competes directly with CBDCs (offering neutrality and
  censorship-resistance).

## The products

**Retail tokens:**
- **Tether Gold (XAUt)** — released Jan 2020; one troy ounce per token; allocated,
  serialized bars; redeemable in Switzerland. By 2026, co-leader with PAXG.
- **Pax Gold (PAXG)** — Paxos, launched Q3 2019; NYDFS-regulated; LBMA Good
  Delivery bars. Co-leader by 2026.
- **DigixDAO / Digix Gold Token (DGX)** — the 2019 leader; one gram of gold; the
  first major DAO on Ethereum. Traded just ~$243,000/day in 2019.
- **Perth Mint Gold Token (PMGT)** — the "first to be backed by government-
  guaranteed gold" (Government of Western Australia).

**Institutional/banking-sector (new in 2026):**
- **HSBC Gold Token** — launched March 2024; >$1 bn trading volume by Nov 2025;
  now third-largest globally. Represents the "established banking sector."
- **World Gold Council's Pooled Gold Interests (PGIs)** — institutional,
  permissioned, T+0 settlement within LBMA infrastructure.
- **Streamex's GLDY** — a yield-bearing tokenized security (launched Feb 2026);
  up to 4% annualized yield via gold leasing; NASDAQ-listed.

**Sovereign angle:** Zimbabwe's gold-backed ZiG currency (April 2024, "questionable
success"); gold-token settlement concepts at the BRICS / Shanghai Gold Exchange
level.

## The key tension: is it "real" gold?

The authors land on a nuanced middle position: tokenized gold is a **structured
claim**, not the metal. "What is being traded is not gold itself but a structured
claim on gold: a digital form whose price tracks the metal but whose value remains
dependent on traditional custody and enforceability" (2026).

They place tokenization in a 4,000-year continuum of abstraction (coins →
banknotes → unallocated accounts → ETFs → tokens), noting "every attempt to make
gold more convenient has simultaneously made it less gold-like." The empirical
evidence is recruited: the Harvey, Lin, Rabetti & Zhang (2026) study found PAXG/
XAUT track spot with correlations above 0.98 even under stress — "in that narrow
sense, tokenized gold works extremely well." But liquidity "remains anchored to
traditional gold market hours." The OMFIF (April 2026) report: most gold tokens
are *indirect* — "the holder has a claim on the issuer, not on the metal itself."

The honest test question they pose and leave open: "At what point does the claim
on gold become so removed from the metal that it is no longer gold?" — while
insisting physical bars "remain the ultimate settlement layer."

## Notable data

- **Market cap:** tokenized gold quintupled in 24 months, surpassing **$6 bn** in
  February 2026 — up from a few tens of millions in 2019-2020 (PAXG ~$43 mn +
  XAUt ~$22 mn in spring 2020).
- **Concentration:** XAUT and PAXG account for ~**97%** of the segment.
- **Physical backing:** more than **1.2 Moz** of physical gold backs the tokens in
  circulation.
- **Trading volume:** **$178 bn** in 2025, surpassing all major US gold ETFs
  except GLD.
- **Project attrition:** "over 50" (2019) → "more than 70 still racing, 30+
  failed" (2020) → a PAXG/XAUT duopoly plus HSBC by 2026.
- **Broader RWA context:** the tokenized real-world-asset market ~$25 bn in early
  2026 (quadrupled YoY); BlackRock's BUIDL >$2.2 bn; projections $11-16 trn by
  decade-end.

## Nuance — bullish or fractional-reserve 2.0?

The treatment is deliberately **dual-track**:

- **Bullish (broadening access):** tokenization lowers the entry barrier "from
  nearly $2 mn for a 400 oz bar to the price of a single gram," broadening reach
  "particularly in emerging markets." It makes gold usable as collateral and, via
  GLDY, an earning asset. The Six Vectors chapter treats it as a demand amplifier
  inside a positive feedback loop.
- **Risk (fractional reserve 2.0):** stated most sharply in the *speculative
  frontier* of **in-ground gold tokenization**: "In-ground tokenization is the
  fractional reserve banking of the crypto era: an elegant packaging for an old
  problem — the monetization of promises that nobody can guarantee" (2026). They
  warn that tokenizing geological estimates (NI 43-101/JORC) repackages mining-
  finance risk in digital form, invoking Bre-X as a cautionary archetype. Even for
  vaulted gold, "speed is a feature in calm markets and a vulnerability in crises."

> **Net verdict:** bullish where it digitizes *existing* vaulted gold ("digitizing
> certainty"); a risk where it tokenizes *future/uncertain* gold ("financializing
> uncertainty"). "The growth is real; the revolution is gradual."
