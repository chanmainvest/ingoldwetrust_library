# Gold as money

The theoretical case for gold as the ultimate monetary asset — the stock-to-flow
argument, the regression theorem, the significance of the 1971 Nixon shock, and
the remonetization thesis. The philosophical foundation of the entire corpus.

> "Gold still represents the ultimate form of payment in the world. Fiat money,
> in extremis, is accepted by nobody." — Greenspan (2013)

See also: [de_dollarization](de_dollarization.md),
[central_bank_gold](central_bank_gold.md), [bitcoin_vs_gold](bitcoin_vs_gold.md),
[monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md).

## The core thesis

Gold is — and is uniquely suited to be — money, by a layered, Austrian-school
case resting on four pillars:

**(a) The regression theorem (Menger/Mises).** Money is not a state invention
but the outcome of a market evolutionary process: the "most marketable good"
emerges from barter through a self-reinforcing network effect. Mises's regression
theorem (1912) resolves the circularity of money's value by introducing time:
today's purchasing power is anchored in yesterday's, regressing back to a
commodity that had pre-monetary utility — which gold and silver did. Gold was
"not selected arbitrarily by governments" (Rothbard, 2012).

**(b) The stock-to-flow argument (the centerpiece from 2013).** Unlike consumption
commodities, gold is bought to be held. Its above-ground stock (~172,000 t in
2013) dwarfs annual mine output (~2,700 t), yielding a stock-to-flow ratio of
~64 years. The decisive claim: "Gold isn't as valuable because it is so rare, but
quite the opposite… not only scarcity, but primarily the relative constancy of the
available stock is what makes gold unique." Annual production is "irrelevant to
price determination." This is why gold "does not obey the Law of Supply and
Demand."

**(c) The 1971 Nixon shock as origin of the crisis.** The closing of the gold
window on August 15, 1971 is treated as the root cause of the contemporary
debt/financial/systemic crisis. Since then, US total credit market debt rose 33x,
public debt 39x, while GDP rose only 14x; only 12% of years saw price deflation
after 1971. The dollar's purchasing power fell from 0.75 g gold to 20.7
milligrams.

**(d) Gold as ultimate insurance / reference asset.** Gold is "uncertainty
insurance" (2021) — a call option on the problem-solving capacity of all other
market actors, perfected over 5,000 years. The 2013 *Essential Features of Gold*
chapter codifies the no-counterparty-risk property that makes this insurance
unique: gold is "the only liquid investment asset that neither involves a liability
nor a creditor relationship" — every other "liquid" claim is someone else's
obligation. Yet at ~$1.1 trn it amounted to only **0.5% of global financial
assets**, a striking under-allocation given that property.

## How the argument evolved

- **2009** — *A New Gold Standard* floats the earliest monetary-base-backing
  valuation in the corpus: if the Fed's balance sheet were covered by US gold,
  one ounce would be valued at ~USD 6,500 (and ~USD 10,000 at full M2 backing).
  The remonetization intuition precedes the formal stock-to-flow vocabulary.
- **2010** — Mostly definitional and historical. "Gold is money" via Aristotle/
  Rothbard; the gold standard era (1815–1914) as the "Golden Era" of stability
  and freedom; the gold standard as "poison for governments."
- **2011 — The remonetization evidence mounts, bottom-up.** The *Gold and Silver
  as Official Means of Payment vs. Gresham's Law* chapter documents the first
  concrete legal reclassifications — Utah recognizing gold/silver coin as legal
  tender, parallel moves in Mexico and Malaysia — and works through the
  Gresham/Thiersch distinction (bad money drives good money into hoards *only at
  a fixed rate*; at floating rates the stronger currency circulates). The
  *Regression Theorem Explains Why Gold Equals Money* chapter supplies the
  Misesian theoretical scaffolding, with stock-to-flow flagged as the reason the
  regression anchor holds: the high ratio "seems to play an important role" in
  keeping gold's purchasing power stable.
- **2012** — Theoretical deepening: the regression theorem, Menger's
  marketability, Mises's circularity resolution.
- **2013** — **Stock-to-flow elevated to "the most important reason for gold's
  monetary importance"** — the signature analytical move. The remonetization
  thesis is born, anchored to OMFIF's Jan 2013 report and gold in SDRs; collateral
  acceptance (LCH Clearnet, CME); US state laws (Utah); repatriation; gold-backed
  bonds. And 1971 nailed down as the crisis's origin.
- **2014 — Stock-to-flow restated as the monetary-above-all criterion.** The
  *Gold's Monetary Importance* chapter re-anchors the case: "If a good is to
  remain money… [there can be] no sudden unstoppable increase in its supply" —
  gold's low annual stock inflation (~1.5–2%) is precisely the property fiat
  lacks, making it the only candidate that survives the monetary test across
  centuries.
- **2021** — Philosophical reframing: money as "economic water," meta-energy,
  uncertainty insurance. First serious engagement with Bitcoin as a potential
  digital disruptor. Predicts a *non*-gold-standard outcome: "more likely an
  MM-esque path than a return to a restrictive gold standard," resolving into a
  "gold-backed digital currency."
- **2026** — Full operationalization of remonetization as a multi-channel,
  self-reinforcing process via the **Six Vectors** framework. "No return to the
  gold standard — but gold is returning. Not by decree, but by function."

> Trajectory: definitional/historical (2010–12) → stock-to-flow monetarization
> (2013) → crisis-origin + remonetization (2013) → philosophical + Bitcoin debate
> (2021) → operational six-vector framework (2026).

## Key mechanisms

1. **Stock-to-flow / relative stock constancy.** Gold's ~64-year S/F (2013) means
   mine supply cannot move the price; constancy, not rarity, creates monetary
   trust. Even at 3,500 t/yr output, stock inflation would be only 1.7%.
2. **Regression theorem / Menger marketability.** Money is the emergent
   "winner-take-most" marketable good; gold won via evolutionary selection, not
   decree (2012, 2013, 2021).
3. **Gold-standard price-stability argument.** Rome/Venice centuries of stability;
   1880–1914 "Golden Era"; US inflation 1879–1914 averaged 0.2% at 2.2% volatility
   vs 4.6% at 2.8% post-1971 (2010).
4. **The 1971 break.** Nixon's shock as the exogenous origin of debt-driven
   growth, asset inflation, Cantillon effects, and the loss of self-correction.
5. **Remonetization by function, not decree.** Gold regains monetary relevance
   through collateral use, reserves, balance-sheet recapitalization, and
   settlement — explicitly *not* via a restored classical gold standard. "A
   credibility standard, not a gold standard" (2026).
6. **The sanctions/sovereignty catalyst.** Freezing Russian reserves (Feb 2022)
   redefined "risk-free reserves"; gold is "without issuer or counterparty risk."
7. **Balance-sheet recapitalization.** US gold booked at $42.22/oz (statutory
   since 1973) vs ~$4,600 market — a ~$1.2 trn revaluation gap. The Eurosystem's
   mark-to-market model is the working precedent.
8. **Digitalization / tokenization (2021, 2026).** Tokenized gold (XAUT, PAXG)
   resolves gold's historic transactability weakness; market cap quintupled to
   >$6 bn by Feb 2026. Bitcoin is treated as *complementary*, not a substitute.

- **1971 dollar erosion:** 0.75 g gold/dollar → 20.7 milligrams (2013).
- **Debt decoupling (2013):** US total credit market debt x33, public debt x39,
  GDP x14 since 1971.
- **Gold-standard stability (2010):** US inflation 1879–1914 averaged 0.2%; post-
  1971 averaged 4.6%.
- **US statutory gold price:** $42.22/oz since 1973; revaluation gap ~$1.2 trn on
  261.5 moz (2026).

## Nuance

- **No formal gold standard is predicted** — consistent across the later corpus.
  2013 (OMFIF): "doesn't recommend a return to a classical gold standard." 2021:
  "more likely an MM-esque path." 2026: "No return to the gold standard — but gold
  is returning. Not by decree, but by function." Judy Shelton's gold-convertible
  Treasury Trust Bonds are framed as a "credibility standard," not a fixed peg.
- **Gold is "not the ideal" final architecture.** The 2021 "What is Money?"
  chapter is candid: "because it is physical, gold cannot perfect the five
  properties of money." Bitcoin is presented as having "perfected the
  affordances" — resolved pragmatically in 2026: Bitcoin is "complementary, not a
  substitute."
- **The remonetization thesis is probabilistic.** The 2026 chapter lists three
  failure scenarios (real-growth debt reduction, geopolitical détente, CBDC
  breakthrough) and argues only their *combined* reversal is unlikely.
- **The "burden of proof" rhetorical move (2026).** Rather than proving
  remonetization will happen, the authors invert the burden: fiat's permanence is
  the extraordinary claim requiring proof, given 5,000 years of monetary history.
- **Tension between "money" and "reference asset."** Early chapters insist gold
  *is* money (medium of exchange); by 2026 the position softens to gold as "the
  ultimate reference asset for value, trust, and settlement" — partly why
  tokenization becomes essential to the argument.
