# The "Status Quo" framework

The recurring opening diagnostic of each annual report — a multi-pillar
triangulation of gold's position across currencies, equities, bonds, debt,
inflation, and demand. The structured "situation report" that sets up the rest of
the report.

> "At least three reference points are needed to determine a position; and with
> each additional reference point, the position can be determined with increasing
> accuracy." (2020)

See also: [monetary_policy_inflation](monetary_policy_inflation.md),
[sovereign_debt](sovereign_debt.md), [portfolio_diversification](portfolio_diversification.md),
[technical_analysis](technical_analysis.md).

## What the framework is

The "Status Quo of Gold" is the **traditional assessment of the gold market** that
opens every report (Incrementum AG, authors Ronald Stöferle & Mark Valek), before
any thematic chapters begin. It deliberately rejects pure supply/demand analysis
as the primary lens. The methodological seed was planted already in the 2009
*Paradigm Shift Similar to 1974?* chapter, which framed each reflation cycle as
the setup for "another asset bubble" and introduced the 1974-bull-analog template
that the later Status Quo chapters operationalize into their multi-pillar
triangulation; the 2014 *The Status Quo* chapter then names the diagnostic
explicitly, diagnosing a market "under the influence of free-flowing liquidity."

> "In contrast to the majority of gold analysts, we do not consider the exclusive
> analysis of supply/demand statistics to be very helpful for assessing gold price
> development. Gold is a stock commodity and a monetary metal." (2020)

The **standard pillar list** (formalized in 2020): (1) USD and other fiat
currencies; (2) opportunity costs (equities, bonds); (3) commodity prices; (4)
inflation trend/expectations; (5) real interest rates; (6) credit spreads; (7)
debt dynamics; (8) monetary growth; (9) confidence in central-bank policy; (10)
confidence in politics/fiscal stability; (11) geopolitics; (12) technical
setup (positioning, sentiment).

## How the framework evolved

The pillars are remarkably stable; what changes is the *emphasis and reframing*:

- **2019** — Foundational, encyclopedic. Six sub-sections in one mega-chapter
  (~2,038 lines). Introduces the "three worldviews" taxonomy (Believers / Skeptics
  / Critics) as a marginal-buyer model. Heavy on US recession signals (yield
  curve), MMT, BBB credit risk, "zombie companies." Gold ~$1,280. Thesis: "early
  stages of a new bull market."
- **2020** — The Covid pivot. Methodology made explicit (the 12-pillar list),
  split across files 02-09. Introduces the **"golden decade"** thesis — the
  headline framing of the 2020 report. Inflation-adjusted ATH cited at $2,215.
- **2022** — Retitled "Status Quo of Gold **as a Currency**" — a deliberate
  narrowing to the currency/monetary pillar, treating gold as money not commodity.
  Introduces the Baur (2021) study on gold's correlation with local stock markets
  across 68 countries.
- **2024** — The major reframing: **"Mastering the New Gold Playbook."** The
  breakout above $2,400 (March 2024) is declared "a new chapter." The key
  conceptual move is the **inversion of perspective**: "The value of gold does not
  actually fluctuate... The gold price therefore does not 'rise', but the fiat
  currencies depreciate in relation to gold." Operationalized via the "Various
  Currencies in Gold" log chart and the **Incrementum World Gold Price (IWGP)**, a
  16-currency GDP-weighted basket. Crucially: the old correlation ("falling real
  rates = positive gold") has **broken down** — gold rose even as real rates rose,
  the heart of the "new playbook."
- **2026** — Full-throated thesis confirmation. Gold +64.4% in USD in 2025
  (strongest since 1979); ATH $5,595 (Jan 2026). The **DXY "tectonic shift"**
  thesis (three historical USD bear markets of -35%, -51%, -40%). Currencies lost
  on average 35.9% vs gold in 2025, 10.1%/yr since 2000.

## Key recurring diagnostics

1. **Gold vs USD / other currencies.** Every year opens with a multi-currency
   performance table; the average since 2000 is cited every year (9.1% in 2019 →
   11.1% in 2026). The "world gold price" (trade-weighted USD) chart shows gold
   closer to ATH when USD strength is netted out.
2. **Gold vs equities.** The Gold/S&P 500 ratio (with 50/200-day MAs); the
   GSCI/S&P 500 ratio (median 4.11) — used to argue commodities are at multi-decade
   lows vs equities.
3. **Gold vs bonds / real rates.** The gold-vs-US-real-interest-rates chart is a
   cornerstone (2019-2020). The 2024 report explicitly declares this relationship
   has *broken down* — the core of the "new playbook."
4. **Debt-to-GDP / debt dynamics.** Public-debt trajectories; CBO deficit
   projections; the "zombie companies" theme (BIS). The US-deficit-exceeds-euro-
   zone-combined framing recurs.
5. **Inflation tracking.** The **Incrementum Inflation Signal** (proprietary, based
   on gold/silver/commodities/miners); break-evens; the Bloomberg Businessweek "Is
   Inflation Dead?" cover (April 2019) cited as a contrarian signal.
6. **Demand breakdown.** Central-bank net purchases; ETF flows by region; the
   Western-vs-Eastern demand split.

**Recurring "house" charts:** the multi-currency annual-performance table; the
average-gold-price-since-1971 log chart; gold-in-mg-per-fiat-currency; the playful
**Gold/Oktoberfest-beer Ratio** (93 Maß/oz in 2019 → 130 Maß/oz in 2020).

## Notable findings by year

- **2019:** Gold -2.1% USD, +2.7% EUR for 2018. Euro down 77.5% vs gold since 1999.
  Since 1971, gold +10%/yr nominal, +4.5%/yr real.
- **2020:** Gold +18.9% USD / +22.7% EUR in 2019. Resistance at $1,360-1,380
  broken. Covid crash low $1,455 (March 16, 2020, VIX 82.7). "Golden decade"
  declared.
- **2022:** 2021 "disappointing" (-3.6% USD, +3.6% EUR). The 2020 ATH $2,075 (Aug
  7, 2020). March 8, 2022 nearly matched at $2,070.
- **2024:** Gold +13.1% USD / +9.7% EUR in 2023 (6th straight EUR gain). March
  2024 rally +15% to over $2,400; CHF crossed 2,000.
- **2026:** Gold +64.4% USD in 2025 (strongest since 1979); **51 new ATHs in 2025**
  (vs 55 in 1979). CAGR since 2000: 11.1% USD.

## Nuance — descriptive recap or forward-looking thesis?

**It is explicitly both**, and the authors are candid that the diagnostic is
constructed *in service of a thesis*, not as neutral description:

- Each sub-section ends with a forward-looking "Conclusion" ("we are in the early
  stages of a new bull market"; "negative real interest rates should have a
  supportive effect for the foreseeable future").
- The 2020 report states the thesis-building function directly: "Last year we took
  a clear position... This thesis was confirmed with the breakout above 1,360-1,380."
  The Status Quo is the *evidentiary record* for a standing bullish call.
- The "three worldviews" model (2019) is overtly a forecasting device — predicting
  that a shift of "Skeptics" into "Critics" triggers the next price leg. (Validated
  in 2019/2020.)

**How it sets up the report:** the Status Quo establishes the *baseline macro
picture* and *gold-specific technicals/sentiment* so the thematic chapters that
follow (de-dollarization, Asian flows, mining stocks, etc.) build on an agreed
factual foundation. Its conclusion typically previews the year's headline thesis
(2020's "golden decade," 2024's "new playbook," 2026's "USD bear-market catalyst").

> Its durability (stable pillars across eight years) and adaptability (the 2024
> "new playbook" reframing when the real-rates correlation broke) are its defining
> features.
